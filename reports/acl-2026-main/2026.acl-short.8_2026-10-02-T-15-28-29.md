Code: https://github.com/YuvMilo/sinks-are-provably-necessary (linked in the paper), cloned to `./codebase`, unmodified.
Environment: conda, Python 3.10, `pip install -r requirements.txt` (torch 2.14.1+cu130), 1×H200.

2. **Quantitative multi-seed check:** `analyze.py`, my own script outside the codebase. It imports the released `utils` (data, models, `train_model`) without changing them and trains every config with the released hyperparameters for seeds 0–4. Seed 0 gives the same trained models as the released scripts. For each model it reports:
   - held-out task error (MSE, and the fraction of sequences with ℓ∞ < 1e-2)
   - per head: BOS attention at non-trigger positions over 1000 test sequences (trigger at 8), and its standard deviation across examples

   Commands: `python analyze.py --config {1x1,2x2,4x4} --seeds 0 1 2 3 4 --out results/<cfg>_s5e-3.json`, plus `--config 1x1 --stop_loss 1e-2` to use the paper's stopping threshold. `summarize.py` turns these into `results/summary_all.txt`.

Settings were the paper's (Appendix A): Adam(0.9, 0.95), batch 128, MSE loss, n=16, L=16, U(−1,1) content, lr 1e-3 for one layer and 1e-4 for multi-layer. The only difference is the stopping threshold: the code stops at ℓ∞ < 5e-3, the paper says 1e-2 (see Discrepancies). Re-running the single-layer models with 1e-2 changes nothing.

Every one of the 40 trained models met the stopping criterion well before the 50k-step cap:
- 1×1: about 650–960 steps
- 2×2: 8k–15k steps
- 4×4 softmax: 18k–28k steps
- 4×4 ReLU: about 7k steps

Held-out test MSE was ≤ 2.5e-7 for every model.

The regenerated figures (`figs_released/*.png`) look the same as the paper's Fig. 2–6. For 4×4 softmax, the head-by-head pattern of sink heads and diffuse heads matches Fig. 5 exactly. Training is deterministic under the released seed.

**Note on robustness across seeds.** This goes beyond the paper, which reports one seed.
- Every softmax model, in every config and seed, has sink heads (≥6/16 in 4×4).
- No ReLU head in any model develops a sink: the largest BOS mass is 0.0024.
- So the central claims (softmax gives sinks, ReLU gives none; Theorem 2's "at least one layer") hold for all 5 seeds.
- The stronger Appendix C wording, "strong sink behavior in at least one head per layer", holds for seeds 0–2 but not 3–4. In those two seeds layer 1 has no sink head: its maximum BOS mass is 0.16 and 0.27.
- This fits the paper's own point that Theorem 2 is existential, but it means "every layer has a sink" depends on the seed.

## Discrepancies
1. **code_vs_paper (minor):** `utils/training.py::train_model` has `stop_loss=5e-3` and the figure scripts don't expose it on the CLI. Appendix A says to train until ℓ∞ < 1e-2. There is also a `max_steps=50000` cap that the paper doesn't mention, but no run hit it. Running with 1e-2 gives the same single-layer results.
2. **code_vs_paper (minor):** `utils/data.py`: during training the trigger position is drawn uniformly from {3..L} (1-indexed; `randint(2, L)` in 0-indexed terms). Sec. 3.2.1 says {2..L}. The evaluation trigger is fixed at 8, so this has no visible effect.
3. **code_vs_paper (minor, cosmetic):** In the code, the "non-trigger non-BOS indicator" is the last coordinate. The paper puts it at coordinate 3 and states that the layout is arbitrary (without loss of generality). Not an actual difference.
4. **paper_internal (no effect on results):** Sec. 4.2 says that in the 4L4H softmax model "head 3 in layer 4 places near-zero attention on BOS". In Fig. 5, and in our identical re-run, L4H3 is diffuse rather than near zero: BOS mass is about 0.21 averaged over non-trigger rows, and higher in the early rows. It is clearly not a sink, so the point being made still holds.

Multi-layer models use residual connections, with the target set to x + mean at the trigger. This differs from the pure composition in Sec. 3.4, but Sec. 4 states it explicitly, so it is not counted as a discrepancy. The ReLU normalisation n_i = max(i−1, 1) matches the code.
