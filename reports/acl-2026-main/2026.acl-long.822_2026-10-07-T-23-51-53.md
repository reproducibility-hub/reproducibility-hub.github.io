**Summary.** We re-ran the full P-ALIGN pipeline on the paper's setting (s1K-1.1 with DeepSeek-R1 traces as teacher data; Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Qwen3-8B as students; LoRA SFT for 3 epochs at lr 5e-5; vLLM sampling with n=3, T=0.6, top-p=0.9, 32k-token budget; scored with the released grader). Average pass@1 over the four benchmarks:

* **Qwen2.5-7B:** P-ALIGN 32.9 vs SFT (Long-CoT) 34.9 vs zero-shot 36.2. The paper reports 39.6 / 36.0 / 31.8.
* **Qwen3-8B:** P-ALIGN 71.5 vs SFT (Long-CoT) 72.0 vs zero-shot 53.4. The paper reports 51.1 / 48.3 / 47.5.

So P-ALIGN does not beat SFT (Long-CoT) on pass@1 for either student. It is slightly better on pass@3 for Qwen2.5 (+1.7) and worse for Qwen3 (−2.9).

The authors' **released Qwen2.5 checkpoint**, re-generated by us, gets 32.7 / 44.5 (pass@1 / pass@3). That is below zero-shot, and its generations hit the 32k-token cap much more often than the authors' released output files. The **released output files** themselves, re-scored with the released grader, do reproduce the paper's Table 1 numbers for AIME25/AIME24/AMC12 (no MATH500 file is released). The **released processed training data** (`P-ALIGN.json` on HF) does not match the paper's description:
* its prefixes average about 0.8k tokens, against about 4.5k in the paper's Table 4;
* it is not answer-filtered: about 71% of the verifiable continuations have wrong final answers, despite Eq. 9 and Table 7.

Our own SFT (Long-CoT) baseline for Qwen2.5 lands close to the paper (34.9 vs 36.0 avg pass@1). Our zero-shot baselines are stronger than reported (Qwen2.5 +4.4, Qwen3 +5.9 avg pass@1). For Qwen3, both SFT variants unlock long "thinking-style" reasoning in non-thinking mode and score far above every Qwen3 number in the paper.

## What we ran (exact pipeline)
Environment: conda env `env/` with Python 3.11 (LLaMA-Factory 0.9.4 needs ≥3.11), vllm 0.11.0, torch 2.8.0, transformers 4.57.1, peft 0.17.1, trl 0.24.0, LLaMA-Factory 0.9.4 (git tag v0.9.4, installed editable), math-verify 0.8.0, liger-kernel 0.6.2. Single H200 GPU.

1. **Data.** `scripts/prep_s1k.py` converts s1K-1.1 (1000 items) to `{question, Long-CoT = deepseek_thinking_trajectory, answer = solution}`. The average trajectory length is 9,292 tokens, which matches the paper's Table 4 value of 9,291. Eval sets (`scripts/prep_eval.py`):
   * AIME24, AIME25 and AMC12 (30/30/83 problems): problems and answers taken from the authors' released result files.
   * MATH500: HuggingFaceH4/MATH-500.
2. **Adaptive prefix truncation (Sec. 3.2.1).** Released `src/binary_search.py` (HF generate, student self-judging with the released prompt), run separately for each student:
   * `python codebase/src/binary_search.py <student> data/s1k_longcot.jsonl work/bs/<m>_prefix.jsonl`
   * Mean retained ratio is 0.586 for Qwen2.5 and 0.620 for Qwen3.
   * No sufficient prefix was found for 44 items (Qwen2.5) and 110 items (Qwen3). The script then keeps the full trajectory.
3. **Prefix alignment (Sec. 3.2.2).** Released `src/prefix-alignment.py` (vLLM, T=0.6, top-p=0.9, repetition penalty 1.05, max 32768) generates the student continuation: `python codebase/src/prefix-alignment.py <student> work/bs/<m>_prefix.jsonl work/align/<m>_align.jsonl`.
4. **SFT data (written by us; not in the release)**, built by `scripts/build_sft.py`:
   * P-ALIGN target = `<Begin_of_Prefix>{prefix}<End_of_Prefix>\n{continuation}`, the same layout as the released P-ALIGN.json. Instruction is "Please reason step by step, and put your final answer within \boxed{}.", input is the question.
   * Main P-ALIGN runs use **no answer filter** (1000 examples). The paper's Eq. 9 filter, implemented with the released grader on the s1K gold answers, keeps only 535 (Qwen2.5) or 652 (Qwen3) of 1000, against 972/988 in the paper's Table 7. The released data is also unfiltered. We therefore made the unfiltered set the main run and trained the Eq. 9-filtered variant for Qwen2.5 as an extra.
   * SFT (Long-CoT) target = full DeepSeek-R1 trajectory + "\n\n" + DeepSeek-R1 final attempt (1000 examples).
   * Average target length is 6,302 tokens (P-ALIGN, Qwen2.5; prefix 5,393), 7,754 (P-ALIGN, Qwen3; prefix 6,158) and 9,873 (Long-CoT). The paper's Table 4 gives 5,453 (prefix 4,531) and 5,732 (prefix 4,402).
5. **Training (config written by us; the release's `scripts/train.sh` calls a non-existent `src/train.py example.yaml`).** LLaMA-Factory LoRA SFT via `scripts/make_train_cfg.sh` → `configs/*.yaml`.
   * Hyperparameters from the paper and from the released checkpoint's `adapter_config.json`/`training_args.bin`: LoRA r=4, alpha=8, dropout 0, all linear layers; lr 5e-5, cosine schedule, warmup ratio 0.1; 3 epochs (paper); effective batch 2 (released checkpoint: 2 GPUs × 1 × 1; ours: 1 × GA 2); bf16; seed 42.
   * Also: cutoff_len 32768, gradient checkpointing.
   * Templates: `qwen` (Qwen2.5); `qwen3` with `enable_thinking: false` (Qwen3), which matches the eval prompt.
   * Qwen3 runs and the filtered Qwen2.5 run use `enable_liger_kernel: true` (fused linear+CE, same loss) to avoid OOM on a 32.5k-token sample.
   * `llamafactory-cli train configs/<run>.yaml`.
6. **Inference / eval.** `scripts/eval_model.sh`:
   * LoRA merge (`llamafactory-cli export`), then the released `src/test.py` with the exact `scripts/Inference.sh` sampling settings (n=3, T=0.6, top-p=0.9, repetition penalty 1.05). The exception is `--max_tokens 32768`: Inference.sh says 4096, but the authors' released outputs run up to 32.7k tokens.
   * All 643 problems go into one vLLM call, which is split back afterwards (`scripts/split_all.py`).
   * Scoring uses the released `src/evaluation.py` (math_verify OR oat grader). Pass@1 = correctness of the first sample, which reproduces the paper's numbers from the released output files. Pass@3 = any of 3.

## Why it does not reproduce (best understanding)
* Our regenerated P-ALIGN data has longer prefixes than the paper reports (Table 4), and the paper's answer filter (Eq. 9) cannot yield the reported 97% retention with any automatic grader we tried. The authors' own released data is unfiltered and has much shorter prefixes. So the exact training data behind Table 1 is unknown, and the released data does not match the paper's description.
* The released checkpoint does not reproduce the released outputs:
  * 77% of its AIME/AMC generations hit the 32k cap in our run, against 27% in the released output files.
  * Its MATH500 pass@1 is 66.6, against 75.8 in the paper.
  * It is a step-1200 snapshot of a 5-epoch run (`trainer_state.json`). Its eval split (≈44 examples) and step count imply ≈860 training examples, not the 966 in the released data or the 972 in Table 7.
  * Most likely it is not the model behind Table 1, but we cannot verify this.
* The baselines in the paper appear under-tuned relative to our runs, especially zero-shot Qwen3 (non-thinking) and Qwen3 SFT (Long-CoT). Our Qwen3 runs with the same LoRA recipe exceed every Qwen3 number in the paper by >20 points avg pass@1. The paper's Qwen3 rows therefore look like a much weaker training/eval setting that we could not identify from the release.
* Single sampling seed everywhere. AIME sets have 30 problems, so one problem = 3.33 points; per-dataset AIME differences of ±3–7 points are within noise. The averages, AMC12 (83) and MATH500 (500) are more reliable, and they show the same picture.

## Settings that differ from the paper / were guessed
* Training framework: LLaMA-Factory 0.9.4 with our YAML. The paper mentions TRL and LLaMA-Factory; the release has no training config. LoRA rank/alpha/target, batch size, scheduler and warmup come from the released checkpoint's adapter_config and training_args. cutoff_len (32768) was guessed.
* Effective batch 2 on 1 GPU (GA 2) instead of 2 GPUs × 1. No DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 (single GPU). 3 epochs as stated in the paper (the released checkpoint used a 5-epoch cosine schedule, stopped at ~2.93 epochs).
* Liger fused-CE kernel for the Qwen3 runs and the filtered Qwen2.5 run (memory only).
* SFT (Long-CoT) target format (trajectory + "\n\n" + attempt, no `<think>` tags) is our choice; the release has no script for it.
* P-ALIGN main runs use the unfiltered set (see above). The Eq. 9-filtered variant is reported separately for Qwen2.5.
* max_tokens 32768 (as in the authors' released outputs; `Inference.sh` says 4096).
* AIME24/AIME25/AMC12 problems are taken from the authors' released result files, since the README only links AoPS pages.
* `src/binary_search.py` was run with `sleep_sec=0` (it sleeps 0.5 s between local-model calls; no effect on results).
* Not run: SFT (Label), UPFT (no code/config released), Table 2 ablations, Tables 6, 8 and 9.

## Discrepancies
**code_vs_paper**
1. *Released processed data `qizheyanger/P-ALIGN/P-ALIGN.json` vs Sec. 3.2.2 Eq. 9, Table 7 and Table 4* (major, affects results):
   * Eq. 9 keeps only samples whose continuation answer matches the gold. In the released 966 examples, 462 of 647 verifiable items have a wrong final answer, so the data is not filtered.
   * The released prefixes average ≈805 tokens and full targets ≈3.0k, against 4,531 and 5,453 in Table 4.
2. *Answer-filter step (Eq. 9)*: not implemented anywhere in the code (major, affects results). The paper's 97% retention (Table 7) is not reachable with an answer-match filter on s1K: we get 53.5% (Qwen2.5) and 65.2% (Qwen3).
3. *Released checkpoint `checkpoint-qwen2.5-7b`* (minor, affects results):
   * It was trained with `num_train_epochs=5` (cosine over 2050 steps) and saved at step 1200 (≈2.93 epochs); the paper says 3 epochs.
   * Its step count and eval split imply ≈860 training examples, against 972 in Table 7.
4. *`scripts/Inference.sh` max_tokens=4096* vs the authors' released outputs, which run up to 32.7k tokens. The paper does not state the budget (minor, affects results).
5. *`src/evaluation.py` pass@1* is undefined in the code; the paper defines Pass@1 as the accuracy of a single answer. The released outputs match the paper only when pass@1 = first sample (minor).
6. *`src/binary_search.py`* uses `while left<=right … right=mid-1` and falls back to the full trajectory when no prefix is judged sufficient (4.4% of items for Qwen2.5, 11% for Qwen3). Paper Eq. 7 sets r=p and terminates at ℓ=r. Equivalent for a monotone judge; the fallback is not described in the paper (minor).
7. *Tag names*: the released data uses `<Begin_of_Prefix>…<End_of_Prefix>`, the released outputs/checkpoint use `<Begin_of_Draft>`, and Fig. 2 shows `<Prefix>…</Prefix>` (minor, no effect).

**paper_internal**
1. Table 1, Qwen2.5 SFT (Long-CoT) Avg Pass@3 is printed as 47.68, but the mean of its four entries (20.00, 26.67, 56.63, 86.60) is 47.48.
2. Table 8 (other teachers) reports P-ALIGN rows identical to Table 1 (DeepSeek-R1 teacher) for three different teachers. Qwen2.5 is 16.67/16.67/49.40 on AIME25/AIME24/AMC12 for R1, QwQ-32B and R1-Distill-7B; Qwen3 is 23.33/30.00/66.27/84.80 for all three. The Qwen3 SFT (Long-CoT) rows for QwQ and R1-Distill differ from Table 1's SFT (Long-CoT) row (58.81 AMC12) but equal each other on AMC12 (59.73). Identical results across different teachers are implausible.
3. Abstract says "outperforms all baselines by over 3%". Read as absolute points, Table 1 does not support it: the avg pass@1 gain over the best baseline is 2.38 (Qwen2.5, vs UPFT) and 2.60 (Qwen3, vs UPFT). The relative gains are 6.4% and 5.4%, so the wording is ambiguous; minor.
