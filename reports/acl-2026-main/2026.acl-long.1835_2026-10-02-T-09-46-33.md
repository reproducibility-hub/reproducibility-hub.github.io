**Summary of findings**
1. **Naive MIAs collapse under AI-generated "adversarial" samples. Reproduced.** In every model/dataset, Min-K%, Min-K%++, ReCall and Con-ReCall fall from roughly 70–95 AUC (Norm) to roughly 0–55 (HoMix), well below chance in most cases. HeMix shows the same pattern. Our Norm numbers for the naive baselines are often within a few points of Table 1 (e.g. BookMIA/13B Min-K%++: 65.06 vs 65.26; BookMIA/30B Min-K%: 80.57 vs 81.10).
2. **MoMIA stays close to the unattacked level in all attack settings and beats every Naive and Two-Stage baseline. Reproduced, but only when features are standardised (z-scored on the training set).** The released `train_MoMIA()` feeds raw feature values. On our features (DNA-GPT on a scale of ~10², Perplexity ~1–20) the run is unstable:
   - MoMIA on raw features averages 15–30 AUC points below the z-scored run on WikiMIA. Norm AUC is often below 55 (e.g. 46.6 at 13B), which is worse than the naive MIAs.
   - On BookMIA, raw features are 5–11 points worse.
   - The paper does not mention any normalisation, and none is in the code. We report both variants.
3. **"MoMIA beats LightGBM-MIA (+5.9 to +11.3 AUCavg)": not supported by our runs.**
   - With a default-config LightGBM on the same 7 features, LightGBM is on par with or better than MoMIA on BookMIA at all three model sizes.
   - It is slightly worse on WikiMIA at 13B and 65B, and slightly better at 30B.
   - The release has no LightGBM code or configuration, so we mark this comparison NOT_COMPARABLE. It is still worth noting: our LightGBM averages 92–96 AUC on BookMIA, against 77–83 in the paper.
4. Many MoMIA cells are within the ±1 pt / 5 % tolerance of Table 1. Others are outside it but keep the claimed ranking (TREND_MATCH). Some HeMix cells are lower (e.g. WikiMIA HeMix 92 vs the paper's 100), and our heterogeneous generator differs from the paper's.

## What we did (exact pipeline)
All scripts are in `./scripts`, logs in `./logs`, intermediate data and features in `./work`, results in `./results`. Environment: `env/` (python 3.10, torch 2.7.0, transformers 4.46.3, scikit-learn, lightgbm), on 1×H200.

1. **`scripts/build_base.py`: base pools.**
   - **WikiMIA:** the `WikiMIA_length256` split, 82 texts (51 members, 31 non-members). The released WikiMIA member and non-member texts all come from this split.
   - **BookMIA:** `swj0419/BookMIA`. Snippets are truncated to their first 256 words (matching the released file). We randomly sampled 520 members and 520 non-members, excluding the released texts:
     - 500 + 500 form the evaluation pool;
     - 20 + 20 are reserved for ReCall/Con-ReCall prefixes;
     - 18 UTF-16-garbled snippets (`\x00` between characters) are dropped, together with their generations.
2. **`scripts/gen_hete.py`: summaries and heterogeneous generations.** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct writes a one-sentence summary of each member text (greedy decoding). It then writes a roughly 256-word passage from the summary (T=0.8, top-p 0.95). This replaces GPT-4o (no API key available).
3. **`scripts/features.py <13b|30b|65b>`: per target model** (`huggyllama/llama-{13b,30b,65b}`, fp16).
   - **Homogeneous generations:** the target model continues `"The following is a {Wikipedia article|passage from a book}.\nSummary: <summary>\n\nText: "` (T=0.8, top-p 0.95, 300–400 new tokens, truncated to 256 words). Degenerate generations under 50 words are dropped.
   - **Min-K%:** k = 20 %.
   - **Min-K%++:** k = 20 %, token log-prob normalised by the mean/std of the next-token distribution.
   - **ReCall:** LL(x | 3 non-member shots) / LL(x).
   - **Con-ReCall:** (LL(x|non-member shots) − 0.5·LL(x|member shots)) / LL(x).
   - **Perplexity:** per-sample exp(mean NLL).
   - **FDGPT (Fast-DetectGPT):** analytic sampling discrepancy, with the target model as both sampling and scoring model.
   - **DNA-GPT:** white-box score; prefix = first 50 % of tokens; K = 10 sampled continuations of equal length; log p(y0|x′) − mean_k log p(yk|x′).
   - ReCall/Con-ReCall shots: for WikiMIA, from the `length128` split (articles disjoint from the eval pool); for BookMIA, from the reserved snippets.
   - Features are also computed for the 40-sample released files.
4. **`scripts/eval_all.py <size> <dataset> 10` (with `STD=0|1`): evaluation.**
   - **Splits:** 10 random 75/25 splits by source item. Each generated text follows its source member into the same split.
   - **Training mixture:** r=(2:1:0.5:0.5), i.e. all training members, #members/2 non-members, #members/4 homo and #members/4 hetero generations.
   - **Test sets:** built for the 5 settings with the paper's ratios. WikiMIA test: 13 members vs 8–13 negatives. BookMIA test: 120 vs 120.
   - **MoMIA:** trained with the **released** `train_MoMIA()` (K=3, lr 1e-3, 400 epochs, batch 32, its internal 80/20 validation split; the final-epoch model is used, as in the released code). `STD=0` uses raw features (as released); `STD=1` z-scores features with training-set statistics (our addition).
   - **LinearMIA:** the released `train_linear_model()`, same settings.
   - **LightGBM:** `LGBMClassifier` with defaults on the same 7 features (configuration not given in the paper or code).
   - **Two-Stage:** our implementation of the paper's description. An FDGPT filter (samples above threshold get the lowest score) is followed by the MIA score; the threshold is chosen to maximise training-mixture AUC.
   - **Naive MIA:** raw score AUC, with higher score meaning member.
   - Reported numbers are the mean over the 10 splits (± std in the tables).
5. **`scripts/make_tables.py`:** builds the paper-vs-ours tables below and `results/comparisons.json`.
6. **As-released check:**
   - `python src/MoMIA_train.py --train_data_file ... --eval_data_file ...` on the released 40-sample features **crashes in its eval loop** (`IndexError: Dimension out of range` in `torch.cat(..., dim=1)`, because single 1-D samples are fed to the model). After the 3-line fix it runs (AUC 0.957 when training and evaluating on the same 40 samples, which is not meaningful).
   - `python src/two_stage.py` needs `PYTHONPATH=.` (`from src.tool import ...`). Its fixed threshold grid in [-1, 1] does not fit the scale of the features (it gives AUC 0.29 for Min-K% on the released WikiMIA features).

## Discrepancies
**code_vs_paper**
1. **`src/two_stage.py` / `src/tool.py::two_stage_mia` (major; affects the Two-Stage baseline).**
   - **Paper:** Two-Stage MIA first filters adversarial samples with an AIGT detector, then applies an MIA to what remains (Sec. 4.1, App. A.11: FDGPT filter + Min-K%++).
   - **Code:** a "dual-threshold" band score on a single metric. The score is `(t2−t1)/2 − |s − (t1+t2)/2|`, with t1 < t2 searched on a fixed grid in [−1, 1]. The thresholds are selected to maximise AUC on the **evaluation labels themselves**. No AIGT detector is involved, and the grid does not match the scale of the MIA scores.
2. **`data/*.jsonl` (major; affects results if these files are used).**
   - **Paper:** homogeneous samples come from the target LLM and heterogeneous samples from GPT-4o, each regenerated from a one-sentence summary of a member.
   - **Release:** the 10 "homogeneous" texts are byte-identical to the 10 "heterogeneous" texts in both files. The texts are not regenerations of the member samples (e.g. BookMIA: "In this activity, students will explore the concept of gravity …").
3. **`src/MoMIA_train.py` CLI default (minor).** The paper trains for 400 epochs; `--epochs` defaults to 200. We passed 400.

**paper_internal**
4. **Sec. 3.1 vs Sec. 3.2 / Eq. 1.** Sec. 3.1 says "we first construct interpretable LightGBM that capture transparent combinations …". Sec. 3.2, Eq. 1 and Fig. 2 describe **linear** experts, and so does the code (`nn.Linear`).
5. **Sec. 4.2 text vs Table 1.**
   - **Text:** "average AUC drop of 67.31% on HoMix and 42.24% on HeMix"; MoMIA gains "up to 39.23% over Naive MIA, 17.51% over Two-Stage MIA, and 8.76% over LightGBM".
   - **Table 1:** recomputing from Table 1 gives a naive-MIA drop of 83.7 % relative (69.6 pts) on HoMix and 47.1 % (39.0 pts) on HeMix. The maximum AUCavg gains are 55.10 (Naive), 26.77 (Two-Stage) and 11.27 (LightGBM).
   - We could not reproduce the quoted figures from the table. Table 1's AUCavg column itself is internally consistent.
6. **App. A.2 Eq. 5.** "Perplexity4" is defined as a dataset-level statistic (a mean over N samples), yet it is used as a per-sample input feature. The README instead lists plain "Perplexity".

## Codebase completeness: MISSING_SCRIPTS

- the LightGBM baseline;

**Changes to the released code** (left uncommitted in `./codebase`; see `git diff`):
- `src/MoMIA_train.py` and `src/LinearMIA_train.py`, `main()` eval loop: added `.unsqueeze(0)` to the per-sample feature tensors, and `.item()` on the output. Without this the script crashes (`IndexError` in `torch.cat(dim=1)` on 1-D tensors) and `roc_auc_score` would receive tensors. This is a bug fix that does not touch the method.

**Scripts we wrote** (outside the release, in `./scripts`):
- `build_base.py`

- `paper_table1.py`
- `make_tables.py`

**Other notes:**
