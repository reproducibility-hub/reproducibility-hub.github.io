## TL;DR
* The proposed system (MuRIL tags + instruction tuning + contrastive loss) reproduces its own numbers. Our Llama-3.2-3B and Qwen2.5-3B contrastive models get BLEU 95.7–97.0 on the manually-edited sets and 77.0–91.0 on real ASR data. That is equal to or slightly above Table 3 / Table 4; for Qwen, every BLEU number is within ±2.3.
* **The improvements the paper attributes to its method do not reproduce.** Same pipeline, same data, same decoding:
  * **Contrastive loss vs. without it:**
    * Llama: **+0.00 BLEU** (paper +1.97)
    * Qwen: **−0.16 BLEU** (paper +4.68)
  * **Full proposed method vs. plain multilingual instruction FT** (no tags, no contrastive loss), Llama: **+0.45 BLEU** (paper +6.16).
  * **Qwen2.5-3B judge (Table 5):** 80–98% draws, no consistent preference. The paper reports e.g. 28% vs 9% for Hindi real.
  * **Simply deleting the words MuRIL tags as disfluent** (no LLM at all) gives the same scores as the proposed system (−0.05 BLEU). The fine-tuned LLMs' outputs are identical to this deletion for 89–100% of sentences, depending on model and eval set. This contradicts the paper's argument that "detection-only strategies are insufficient".
  * **mBART baseline** (the released script): BLEU 95.7–96.7 on the manual sets vs. 83.7–85.7 in the paper. The proposed method's +8.54 BLEU margin shrinks to +2.9, and the plain Llama FT baseline matches that margin too.
* As released, the code cannot produce the paper's tables. Missing pieces:
  * data preparation, MuRIL inference, inference for the contrastive model, and evaluation scripts;
  * paths hard-coded to the authors' machine;
  * the non-contrastive trainer crashes under the pinned `transformers==4.51.3`;
  * with the paper's 512-token limit, 70–89% of the tag-conditioned training examples lose their target;
  * a pad = eos bug: the Llama models never learn to stop generating. This is what made our first-pass Llama plain baseline collapse to BLEU 10–42 and gave a spurious +2.8 BLEU contrastive gain (see below).

## What the paper claims (main experiments)
* Table 3 (Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct) and Table 4 (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct) report BLEU, chrF2 and TER on the manually-edited and real test sets for Hindi, Bengali and Marathi. Systems compared: multilingual instruction FT, mBART, MuRIL-tag-conditioned FT without contrastive loss, and with contrastive loss (proposed).
* Text claims:
  * the contrastive loss adds +1.97 BLEU (Llama) and +4.68 BLEU (Qwen) on average;
  * the proposed method beats multilingual FT by +6.16 BLEU and mBART by +8.54 BLEU (README);
  * "detection-only strategies are insufficient".
* Table 2: MuRIL tagger performance. Table 5: LLM-as-a-judge (Qwen2.5-3B), proposed vs. "parallel fine-tuning".
* Not reproducible here:
  * Appendix I (GPT-4o / Gemini 2.5 Pro): closed models, no key;
  * human evaluation (Appendix H);
  * downstream QA/MT/TTS (Appendix B).

## What I did
1. **Data:** `scripts/prep_data.py`.
   * Combined the 3 × 40,000 synthetic pairs (`noisy/{lang}/train.processed.{dis,flu}`) and split them randomly 80/10/10 (seed 42): 96k train / 12k valid / 12k test.
   * Derived word-level disfluency labels with Kundu's right-to-left subsequence alignment (`prepare-labeled-dataset.py` logic). 119,970 of the 120k pairs are subsequences; the other 30 fall back to difflib alignment.
   * Evaluation sets are the public manually-edited (575/500/420) and real (150/300/250) test sets with their gold `test.labels`.
2. **MuRIL tagger:**
   * Trained with `codebase/muril_detection/train.py`, unchanged: `python train.py -d data --separate-files -id muril` (5 epochs, lr 2e-5).
   * Tagged the eval sets with `scripts/muril_predict.py`; a word's label is the prediction for its first sub-token.
3. **LLM training data:** `scripts/llm_formats.py`, `scripts/build_llm_train.py`. Each record is Alpaca-style instruction/input/output.
   * `contrastive`: the Appendix K prompt (tokens, labels, sentence, disfluent-token list) plus the `disfluent_tokens` field used by the contrastive loss.
   * `tags`: exactly the format of `without_contrastive_ft/alpaca_format.py` (word+label pairs).
   * `plain`: multilingual instruction FT, with no token-level signal (Sec. 3.3.3).
   * Training uses the gold alignment labels (Sec. 3.2: "identified through alignment"). Test inputs use the MuRIL-predicted labels.
4. **LLM training** (`scripts/run_train.sh`, `queue*.sh`):
   * Proposed method: `codebase/with_contrastive_loss/train.py`, i.e. λ=0.3 with warm-up fraction 0.3, label smoothing 0.01, lr 1e-5 cosine, batch 8×2, bf16, fused AdamW, early stopping patience 3.
   * Both baselines: `codebase/without_contrastive_ft/train.py` with its released hyperparameters (batch 16×1, lr 1.5e-5, cosine_with_restarts, torch.compile).
   * Models: Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (through a weight-identical ungated mirror) and Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct.
5. **Inference:** `scripts/infer_llm.py`. Greedy decoding using the exact training prompt (`### Instruction / ### Input / ### Response`). Generation stops at EOS or the first newline, and the first line is kept.
6. **mBART baseline:** the released `mBART/prepare_data.py` and `finetune_mbart_disfluency.py` (3 epochs, defaults, `--bf16`) on the same 96k training split, then the released `mBART/inference.py` (beam 5).
7. **Metrics:** `scripts/evaluate.py`, sacrebleu 2.5.1 corpus BLEU / chrF2 / TER with default settings.
8. **Judge:** the released `llm_as_a_judge/judge_qwen_vllm_optimized.py`, unchanged, with Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, order-debiased. It ran through a small HF-transformers stand-in for `vllm.LLM` / `SamplingParams` (`scripts/vllm_shim`), because installing vLLM would force transformers 5.x. Wrapper: `scripts/run_judge.py`.

## Why the run is not REPRODUCED
1. **The central claims fail on correctly trained baselines.** The contrastive objective adds nothing (Llama +0.00, Qwen −0.16 BLEU). Tag conditioning adds about +0.4 BLEU over plain instruction FT. The judge shows no preference. The task is close to deterministic deletion: the gold references are exactly the disfluent sentence minus the gold-labelled words (gold deletion scores BLEU 100). Every competent fine-tuned model converges to "delete what MuRIL tags", and MuRIL deletion alone matches the proposed system.
2. **The paper's baseline numbers look under-trained or mis-decoded.**
   * mBART: ours 95.7–96.7 BLEU on the manual sets vs. 83.7–85.7 reported.
   * Llama w/o contrastive and multilingual FT: ours +3 to +27 BLEU over the paper.
   * One concrete mechanism: the released training code sets `pad_token = eos_token` for Llama, so `DataCollatorForLanguageModeling` masks every EOS label and the model never learns to stop. With that bug our Llama multilingual FT collapses (BLEU 10–42, with run-on repetitions), and Llama w/o contrastive loses about 7–10 BLEU on Bengali. That reproduces the *direction* of the paper's gaps but is an artifact. We can't tell whether this is what happened in the authors' runs.
3. **Scale:** 2 epochs instead of the released 4. The proposed models already match or exceed the paper's numbers, and all compared systems used the same budget.

## Discrepancies
**Code vs. paper**
1. *(major)* `with_contrastive_loss/train.py`, `without_contrastive_ft/train.py`. Paper Eq. 2: the CE loss is over the gold fluent output tokens. Code: labels = all `input_ids`, so CE also covers the instruction and input (prompt) tokens.
2. *(major)* Both train scripts, Llama only. The paper describes standard CE training to generate the fluent reference. The code sets `pad_token = eos_token` and uses `DataCollatorForLanguageModeling`, which masks every EOS label, so the model never learns to terminate. Released Llama models produce run-on outputs; the plain-FT baseline collapses to BLEU 10–42.
3. *(major)* `without_contrastive_ft/infernce.py`, `generate_fluent_from_preds.py`. These are the released inference scripts. They use a different prompt ("Word+Label Pairs … Fluent Sentence:"), a different instruction text and `repetition_penalty=1.2`, none of which match the training format (`### Instruction/### Input/### Response`) or Appendix K. There is no inference script for the contrastive model.
4. *(minor)* `with_contrastive_loss/train.py`, contrastive penalty. Paper Eq. 3 averages per sequence over response steps, then over N. The code averages over all response tokens in the batch, and the mask is aligned to logit positions rather than shifted label positions (one-position offset).
5. *(minor)* Early stopping. Paper: "patience of 3 epochs". Code: `EarlyStoppingCallback(patience=3)` with evaluation every 1000 steps, i.e. 3 evaluations.
6. *(minor)* Baseline hyperparameters. Appendix D says all runs use identical hyperparameters. The baseline script `without_contrastive_ft/train.py` uses batch 16×1, lr 1.5e-5, `cosine_with_restarts`, no label smoothing, no early stopping and torch.compile. The contrastive script uses 8×2, 1e-5, cosine, label smoothing 0.01 and early stopping.
7. *(minor)* Instruction format. Appendix K gives a single prompt ending in "Fluent Sentence:". The code wraps instruction and input in an Alpaca `### Instruction/### Input/### Response` template.
8. *(minor)* README. The listed commands reference non-existent scripts.

**Paper-internal**
1. Source of the training-time disfluency labels:
   * Sec. 3.2 says the contrastive variant gets disfluent tokens "identified through alignment";
   * Sec. 3.4 / Fig. 2 / Sec. 3.5 say training uses MuRIL-predicted labels and "MuRIL-tagged disfluencies".
2. Max sequence length:
   * App. F states a 512-token sequence length;
   * the App. K prompt (tokens, labels, sentence and token list) makes 70–89% of training sequences longer than 512 for these languages, so the targets would be truncated.
