## 1. What the paper claims and what was targeted

The main result is **Table 1**: LLaDA-8B {Base, Instruct} and Dream-v0-7B {Base, Instruct}, each with
*Vanilla* (no conditioning), *Prefix Prompting*, and *Ours (TI = Template Infilling + Dynamic Segment Allocation)*,
on GSM8K, MATH500, HumanEval and Trip Planning ("CSR"). The headline claim (abstract, §4.2) is that TI gives
"consistent improvements of **9.40 %p** over baseline prompting", i.e. the mean of the per-model average TI-vs-Vanilla gains.
All runs use pure parallel generation of 128 tokens (gen_length = 128, block_length = 128, 128 steps). DSA expands by
8 tokens per expansion, with at most 10 expansions.

I reproduced the **TI and Vanilla rows of Table 1 for all 4 models × 4 benchmarks**. I also ran Prefix Prompting with a
configuration reconstructed from the release (see §3), and one extra control.

## 2. Headline outcome

* **The 9.40 %p headline reproduces: 9.59 %p** (mean over the 4 models of the per-model average TI − Vanilla gain).
  Per-model gains: LLaDA-Base −1.73 (paper −0.16), LLaDA-Instruct +11.64 (+10.93), Dream-Base +17.13 (+18.23),
  Dream-Instruct +11.32 (+8.61).
* **LLaDA (both variants): close to exact.** 12 of 16 TI/Vanilla numbers are within 1 pt of Table 1 and 15 of 16 within 2 pt.
  The exception is LLaDA-Base HumanEval TI (23.8 vs 28.05; same sign of effect, TI below Vanilla as in the paper).
* **Dream math: TI is far stronger than reported.** Dream-Instruct GSM8K TI is 65.9 vs 39.8 and MATH500 TI 20.6 vs 12.8.
  Dream-Base GSM8K TI is 52.5 vs 44.6. Vanilla numbers match the paper, so the direction of the claim holds with a larger gain.
* **Dream HumanEval: the claimed TI gain does not reproduce.** Dream-Base TI 18.3 vs Vanilla 17.1 (paper 29.9 vs 18.3).
  Dream-Instruct TI 20.1 vs Vanilla 22.6 (paper 33.5 vs 20.1), so the ranking flips. The cause is the released Dream code
  templates (`minimal_code_base` / `minimal_code`). They inject `"""\n Implementation details:\n """` after the
  docstring, so the model writes prose after a closing `"""`. 83 of 164 Dream-Instruct TI completions are Python syntax errors.
* **Trip Planning (subset): TI helps Dream as claimed, but less** (Base 11.5 vs 2.0, Instruct 10.0 vs 1.0; paper 15.9 / 16.3
  vs ~1). There is no effect on LLaDA, matching the paper.
* **Caveat on the Dream comparison (extra control, §4).** The released Dream Vanilla baseline uses random-order unmasking,
  while TI+DSA uses confidence-ranked unmasking. With DSA on but no template, Dream-Base already reaches 46.6 on GSM8K
  (first 500), vs 8.2 Vanilla and 55.6 TI. So most of the large Dream-Base gain comes from the sampler, not the template.

Because the method's claimed gain holds on most model/benchmark cells but fails (or flips) on Dream HumanEval, the overall
result match is **MIXED**. That makes the verdict **PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED**, and coverage is also scaled down.

### Deviations from the paper's setting
1. **Trip Planning subset (compute).** One 5-shot trip prompt is about 2,600 tokens, and each run takes 128–300 full-sequence
   denoising passes at batch size 1, with no KV cache. The full 1600 × 8 TI/Vanilla runs did not fit in the 48 h single-GPU budget.
   I used a stratified subset: every 8th example (200 items, 25 per city-count 3–10) for LLaDA TI/Vanilla and Dream-Base TI,
   and every 16th example (100 items, a subset of the former) for Dream-Base Vanilla, Dream-Instruct TI and Dream-Instruct Vanilla.
   On the shared 100-item subset: LLaDA-Base Van/TI 13.0/16.0, LLaDA-Instruct 11.0/11.0, Dream-Base TI 9.0.
2. **Prefix Prompting GSM8K** used only the first 500 problems (`--limit 500`, compute). MATH500 / HumanEval prefix runs are full.
   Prefix configs are reconstructed (see table), so all Prefix rows are `NOT_COMPARABLE`.
3. **Dream GSM8K/MATH500 steps = 128.** The release has no main-table script for these; its ablation scripts default to
   steps = 64 and `LIMIT=500`. I used steps = 128 and the full test sets, as in every other main script and in paper §4.1.
4. Single-process evaluation (no `accelerate launch`); greedy decoding (temperature 0) throughout, so results are deterministic.

**Extra control: is the Dream gain from the template or from the sampler switch?** In the release, Dream Vanilla goes through
`_sample` with `alg='origin'` (random-order unmasking). TI+DSA goes through the DSA branch of `diffusion_generate`, which
unmasks by top-k confidence. Running the DSA branch *without a template* on the first 500 GSM8K problems gives:
Dream-Base **46.6** vs Vanilla 8.2 / TI 55.6; Dream-Instruct **43.0** vs Vanilla 37.6 / TI 67.2. So for **Dream-Base most of the Vanilla→TI gain on GSM8K (+47 pt) comes from switching the sampler** (+38 pt without any template). The template itself adds about +9 pt on top. A confidence-sampled, template-free Dream-Base (46.6) already beats the paper's TI number (44.58). For Dream-Instruct the split is about +5 pt from the sampler and +24 pt from the template. The paper's Table 1 attributes the whole difference to TI (see Discrepancy 2). Separately, the paper's Table 2 ablation (TI without DSA = 36.0, run through the same random-order sampler as Vanilla) does show a template effect on its own.

## 5. Why it is not fully reproduced
* **Dream HumanEval (method claim fails).** The released Dream code templates break Python syntax in about half of the completions
  (unbalanced triple quotes followed by free text). The paper's appendix Table 5 shows a different HumanEval template
  ("# Initialize necessary variables" / "# Process the logic"), which matches none of the released ones. This is a
  release/paper mismatch. I did not change templates, since that would mean guessing the authors' template.
* **Dream math numbers are much higher than the paper's TI numbers** (GSM8K-Instruct +26 pt). The released Dream TI
  path (steps = 128, DSA max_expansions = 20) evidently differs from what produced Table 1. The ablation scripts use steps = 64,
  so the paper may have used a different step count; the direction of the claim is preserved.
* **Compute:** the trip-planning subset and GSM8K-Prefix subset (§3) mean coverage is SCALED_DOWN.
* **Prefix Prompting** has no released run configuration, so its numbers are NOT_COMPARABLE. My reconstruction reproduces
  LLaDA-Base exactly on HumanEval (26.22 = 26.22) and closely on GSM8K (21.2 vs 22.74), but not the other variants.

## 7. Discrepancies
**Code vs paper**
1. *DSA rule (major, affects results)*. `ti_ours.generate_with_ti_dsa`, `ours_llada_trip`, `generation_utils_dream.diffusion_generate`.
   The paper (Eq. 5, Alg. 1) expands **each segment** M_i whose minimum token confidence is below τ, by δ tokens.
   The code instead (a) takes the **global** minimum over all masked positions and requires it to be < `tau_low=0.1`, and
   (b) **additionally requires** that the mean EOS probability over the last 32 masked positions is < `tau_expand=0.6`
   (not in the paper). It then inserts 8 masks at that single position (at most one expansion per step), and (c) adds 8
   extra denoising steps per expansion (not in the paper).
2. *Dream baseline uses a different sampler than Dream TI (major, affects results)*. Vanilla and Prefix (`use_dsa=False`) run
   `_sample` with `alg='origin'` (random-order unmasking). TI+DSA runs the DSA branch with top-k confidence unmasking. The paper presents
   Vanilla vs TI as differing only in conditioning. Control (§4, GSM8K first 500): DSA sampler without a template gives
   Dream-Base 46.6 vs released Vanilla 8.2 and TI 55.6, so most of the Dream-Base GSM8K gain comes from the sampler.
3. *HumanEval templates (major, affects results)*. Paper appendix Table 5 shows anchors "# Initialize necessary variables" /
   "# Process the logic". The release uses `code_generation_base` for LLaDA ("# Setup and Input Validation" /
   "#Main Algorithm Implementation" / "return"), and for Dream `minimal_code_base` / `minimal_code`. The Dream templates insert a closed
   `"""…"""` block that makes about 50 % of completions syntactically invalid.
4. *max expansions (minor)*. Paper §4.1: "a maximum of 10 expansions". `llada_dist` defaults to 10, but `dream_minimal`
   (`ours_dream_verify.py`) and both trip CLIs default to `max_expansions=20`, and the run scripts do not override it.
5. *"single static template per task" (minor)*. Two different `math_template1` definitions exist: `ti_ours.py` for LLaDA
   uses the final anchor "\nTherefore, the answer is:" at L−20, while `templates.py` for Dream uses "The answer is $" at L−10.
   HumanEval templates differ per model/variant. The GSM8K example in Table 5 shows yet other anchors.
6. *Trip metric name (minor)*. The paper reports "Constraint Satisfaction Rate"; the code computes whole-plan exact match
   (`trip_metric.compute_example_score`, hard 0/1 score). Our numbers suggest the paper also used this EM metric.
7. *No Dream GSM8K/MATH500 main script (minor)*. The only commands provided (ablation scripts) use steps = 64 and `LIMIT=500`,
   while the paper reports full test sets and other main scripts use 128 steps.

**Paper-internal**
1. §4.2 says TI "yields consistent performance improvements across all benchmarks", but Table 1 shows TI below Vanilla
   for LLaDA-Base GSM8K (49.89 < 51.63), HumanEval (28.05 < 35.4) and Avg (26.26 < 26.42), and for LLaDA-Instruct Trip
   (12.06 < 12.13).
2. Appendix Table 5 (GSM8K, HumanEval examples) shows template anchors that differ from the "single static template" per
   task described in §4.1 and from each other.
