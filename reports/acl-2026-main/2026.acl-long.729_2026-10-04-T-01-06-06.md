Summary: the released repo (https://github.com/cisnlp/multypo, linked in the paper) contains only the MulTypo
typo-generator package. It has no evaluation code, no data selection, no prompts and no aggregation scripts. I wrote
the whole evaluation pipeline from the paper's description (§4, App. C, App. F) and ran it on all 18 models.

- **Qualitative conclusions mostly reproduce.** These hold in every family:
  - typos degrade performance monotonically with the typo rate;
  - MGSM and Flores are the most fragile tasks, while XNLI and MMMLU are comparatively robust;
  - instruction-tuned models beat their base models on clean input but lose at least as much under typos (Fig. 5, holds in 15/15 family x task cells);
  - English has the smallest relative drop in all three families (Table 5);
  - Arabic, Hindi and Bengali lose more;
  - translation from English is more robust than translation into English (11/11 languages, all three families; Fig. 8/14/15);
  - more shots raise clean accuracy but do not shrink the typo gap (Fig. 6).
- **Absolute numbers match only partly.** 35 of 97 compared numbers are within tolerance (≤5% relative or ≤1 point):
  - Table 3 instruct-family averages match for Gemma-M/L, Qwen-M and OLMo-L;
  - small models and the Qwen family score higher than in the paper (e.g. Qwen Belebele 74 vs "over 50", Qwen MGSM 61.5 vs "around 40").
- **Claims that do not reproduce:**
  - "Larger models show smaller relative drops, particularly Gemma and OLMo" (Table 3): this holds for Gemma (7.8% → 6.2% → 3.7%) but is reversed for OLMo (4.8% → 6.2% → 6.4%).
  - The "sharp" Russian drop (Gemma 11.9%, OLMo 15.8%) does not appear: we get 4.8% / 5.8%.
  - Gemma's slight *improvement* under typos for English and Greek is not seen; we get small drops instead.

## What the paper claims / what I targeted
Main experiment (§4–5): 18 open LLMs are evaluated on 5 tasks, with MulTypo typos injected at rates 0 / 0.1 / 0.4 / 0.7, 3-shot.
- Models: Gemma-3 1B/4B/12B, Qwen3 1.7B/4B/8B, OLMo-2 1B/7B/13B, each in base and instruct versions.
- Tasks: XNLI, Belebele, MMMLU, MGSM (+ Arabic/Hindi GSM8K), Flores200 in both directions.
- Results appear in Figure 3 (performance vs typo rate), Figure 4 and Table 3 (model size), Figure 5 (base vs instruct),
  Table 5 and Fig. 8 (languages / translation direction), and Figure 6 (number of shots).

I reproduced all of these settings at the paper's scale. I did **not** run:
- Table 4 (WikiTypos comparison): needs typo pairs extracted from WikiTypos, and that code is not released.
- Fig. 7 (multilingual AIME): a complementary experiment.
- App. D (naive random-typo baseline): the naive generator is not in the release, so I did not reconstruct it.
- Table 2 (human evaluation): not reproducible.

## What I did (commands)
Everything runs in the conda env `env/` (python 3.11, vllm 0.30.0, torch 2.13, transformers 5.18, sacrebleu 2.6, stanza 1.15, nltk, pyarabic).

1. `git clone https://github.com/cisnlp/multypo codebase`, symlinked as `multypo` so it can be imported as a package.
2. `python scripts/build_data.py`: builds the clean evaluation sets following App. C.1:
   - **XNLI**: 1000 test items, label-stratified, with the same indices in all 7 languages. The 3 exemplars are the first validation item of each label.
   - **Belebele**: 12 languages; the first 3 rows are exemplars and the other 897 are queries.
   - **MMMLU**: 6 languages (English taken from `cais/mmlu`). 1000 items stratified by subject, with the same indices in all languages; the first 3 test rows are exemplars.
   - **MGSM**: bn/de/en/fr/ru with 250 test items each and the first 3 train exemplars. Arabic uses `Omartificial-Intelligence-Space/Arabic-gsm8k` (first 250 test items, 3 train exemplars). Hindi uses `sarvamai/gsm8k-indic` (hi), with test rows 0–2 as exemplars and rows 3–252 as queries.
   - **Flores200**: the original FLORES-200 tarball (`openlanguagedata/flores_plus` is gated for this token). The first 500 devtest sentences are used in each direction (X→En and En→X) for 11 non-English languages, with dev rows 0–2 as exemplars.
3. `python scripts/make_typos.py fixed`: injects MulTypo typos at rates 0.1/0.4/0.7 into the instance fields only (and into the exemplars' inputs; answers untouched, App. C.2).
   - Fields: premise/hypothesis; passage/question/options; question/options; MGSM question; Flores source.
   - Settings: package defaults (sentence tokenization on, excluding sets on, default typo distribution and neighbour weights), with a deterministic seed per field.
   - `python scripts/make_typos.py orig ben_Beng,eng_Latn` does the same with the **unmodified** package, for the as-released comparison.
4. `scripts/run_all.sh` runs `scripts/run_eval.py --model M` for all 18 models, then `scripts/score.py`.
   - App. F settings: vLLM with default sampling parameters, i.e. `SamplingParams(max_tokens=N)` (temperature 1.0, top-p 1), `seed=0`. N = 5 (XNLI), 100 (Belebele), 5 (MMMLU), 200 (MGSM), 100 (Flores).
   - Raw completion prompts use the App. C.2 templates. Each of the 3 demonstrations is the full filled-in template followed by its gold answer, and the query is the open template. Blocks are separated by a blank line.
5. `scripts/run_shots.sh` re-runs the instruct models with 0, 1 and 5 shots (Fig. 6). The 5-shot data comes from `scripts/build_data5.py` plus `make_typos.py` with `CLEAN_DIR=clean5 TYPO_DIR=typo5`.
6. `scripts/aggregate.py`, `scripts/aggregate_shots.py`, `scripts/merge_orig.py` and `scripts/compare.py` produce `results_fixed.{md,json}`, `results_origmerged.json`, `results_shots.{md,json}` and `comparisons.json`.

Scoring:
- XNLI, Belebele and MMMLU: the first label digit or letter in the output.
- MGSM: the generation is cut at the next "Question:" or blank line; the number after "answer is" is used, otherwise the last number.
- Flores: corpus BLEU (sacrebleu default 13a) on the output up to `<EOS>` or the first newline.
- Aggregation: Table 3 averages all (task, language) entries of a model. Flores counts both directions as separate entries. This choice matched the paper much better than averaging the 5 task means, which is also reported in `results_fixed.md`. Table 5 averages each language over all tasks and the family's 3 instruct models.

### Settings that differ from / are unspecified in the paper (my choices)
- **Few-shot layout** is not specified. My first choice (instruction once, then 3 blocks) made Qwen3-1.7B answer "0" for 99.9% of XNLI items. I switched to repeating the full template per demonstration, applied uniformly to all tasks and models; this is the most literal reading of App. C.2. Small models are very sensitive to this choice (`scripts/xnli_format_probe.py`).
- **No chat template** is applied to instruct models. The paper's templates are completion-style ("Label:", "Answer:"). If the authors applied a chat template, possibly with Qwen3 "thinking", that could explain why our Qwen numbers are higher.
- **Decoding**: "vLLM default sampling parameters" read literally means temperature 1.0 sampling. One seed (0); the paper reports no seeds or variance.
- **Exemplar and instance selection**: the exact subsampled IDs, exemplars, random seeds and typo'd data are not released. I rebuilt them from the App. C.1 description.
  - The Hindi GSM8K source is not named precisely ("Gumma et al., 2024"); I used `sarvamai/gsm8k-indic` (hi). It only has English reasoning, so the Hindi exemplars have English rationales.
  - Arabic MGSM labels come from the parallel English GSM8K test set, because some Arabic rows lack "####".
- **Context limit**: OLMo-2 has a 4096-token context. Prompts that do not fit fall back to fewer shots: 10,643 of 146,056 prompts per OLMo model, all of them Belebele Armenian, Georgian or Tamil (none in Table 5). The paper does not say how it handled this.
- **BLEU tokenizer** is unspecified; I used sacrebleu 13a. Non-Latin En→X BLEU is therefore low, e.g. Qwen En→Tamil about 1.3.
- **Flores**: the first 500 devtest sentences; the paper does not say which 500.
- **Shot-count analysis**: MMMLU exemplars 4–5 are the next unsampled test items. For Belebele, rows 3–4 become exemplars, so its 5-shot query set has 895 items.

## Codebase completeness: MISSING_SCRIPTS
The method (the MulTypo generator) is released and works. The entire experiment pipeline is missing, and I wrote it from the paper:
- data selection and subsampling;
- few-shot prompt construction;
- vLLM inference;
- answer parsing and metrics;
- aggregation into the tables and figures.

The subsampled instance IDs, exemplars, seeds and generated typo'd datasets are not released either. They are rebuildable from public data, which I did, though not identically.

Changes to the released code (left uncommitted in `./codebase`):
1. `codebase/tokenization.py`, Bengali sentence splitter.
   - The bug: `re.findall(r".*?[।?!]", text)` silently drops any trailing text without final punctuation, and returns nothing for texts with no danda. `insert_typos_in_text` then returns the original text unchanged.
   - The fix: `r"[^।?!]*[।?!]|[^।?!]+$"`.
   - Effect on as-released typo data at 10%: Bengali MMMLU loses 2,320 of 64,337 words across 163 fields; Belebele loses 111 words; Flores loses 13 words. Many short fields get no typos at all (3,128 vs 2,344 unchanged MMMLU fields).
   - This only affects Bengali, but it changes the Bengali numbers noticeably. For example, Table 5 Bengali relative drop: Gemma 4.3% fixed vs 6.4% as released; Qwen 6.5% vs 3.9%; OLMo 9.8% vs 3.3%. Part of these differences is also sampling noise (temperature 1).

Scripts I wrote (all in `scripts/`): `build_data.py`, `build_data5.py`, `make_typos.py`, `run_eval.py`, `score.py`, `aggregate.py`, `aggregate_shots.py`, `merge_orig.py`, `compare.py`, `run_all.sh`, `run_shots.sh`, `xnli_format_probe.py`.

Environment notes:
- The vLLM FlashInfer sampler needs `nvcc` for JIT compilation, which is not available on the node. I ran with `VLLM_USE_FLASHINFER_SAMPLER=0`.
- Stanza tokenizers (hy/ka/he/hi/ta) and NLTK punkt are downloaded into `resources/`.

## Discrepancies
Code vs paper:
1. *Keyboard neighbours* (`core.py::_get_neighbours_with_orientation`): App. A says only the left and right keys on the same row are neighbours. The code also uses the vertical neighbours (row above and below, same column index), weighted 9:1 horizontal:vertical, and a vertical weight of 0 is impossible (`max(1, round(w))`). Minor; it affects which characters get substituted or inserted.
2. *Typo-operation probabilities* (`core.py::DEFAULT_TYPO_DISTRIBUTION`): the paper uses insert 15.25% and others 28.25%; the code uses 0.15/0.28/0.28/0.28, normalised to 15.15%/28.28%. Minor; negligible effect.
3. *Transposition across the word boundary* (`core.py::insert_typos/_transpose`): the code appends a space to the word and allows swapping its last character with that space (e.g. "word next" → "wor dnext"). The paper describes transpositions only between characters typed with different hands. Minor.
4. *Bengali sentence splitting* (`tokenization.py`): it drops trailing text and skips typo injection for texts without a danda, so the Bengali inputs are not corrupted at the stated rate τ and some content is deleted. Major (a bug, Bengali only); see above.
5. *Evaluation pipeline / data selection* is described in the paper but absent from the release (completeness issue, listed under codebase notes).

Paper-internal:
1. §6.1 says MulTypo "generally exhibits degradation patterns that lie *between* Baseline and WikiTypos", but Table 4 has MulTypo **highest** in 12 of 15 cells: Belebele, MMMLU and MGSM at 10% (76.58 / 43.43 / 53.80), everything except XNLI at 40%, and all five tasks at 70%. It lies strictly between Baseline and WikiTypos in only 2 cells (Flores 10%, XNLI 40%). (App. D likewise says models do better under MulTypo than under the naive baseline.)
2. §6.4 says "Across all three model families, English consistently exhibits the highest robustness", but Table 5 Qwen English has a 5.6% drop, larger than fra (3.3%), deu (4.0%) and rus (4.7%). Also, Russian is said to suffer "sharp degradation" even though Qwen Russian drops only 4.7%.

## Not reproduced and why
- The absolute numbers are often outside tolerance. The release gives no prompts, exemplar IDs, seeds, chat-template or decoding details, and evaluation is sampling-based (temperature 1). Small models are extremely sensitive to the few-shot layout: XNLI for Qwen3-1.7B went from 30% to 48% with layout alone. So exact numbers cannot be expected.
- OLMo's size-robustness trend and the large Russian/Bengali drops did not appear in our run.
- Table 4, Fig. 7, App. D and Table 2 were not run (data, code or human annotations unavailable; complementary experiments).
