### Interpretation
- **Table 1 (GENOME-DARE-TIES):** reproduces well.
  - Most cells are within the paper's tolerance.
  - The saturation pattern holds, and is somewhat stronger in our runs. The best n is 6 on most tasks, and going from 6 to 10 experts lowers the score on every task.
  - The largest deviations are MATH with 2 experts (paper 11.8, ours about 15) and MGSM at n = 2/4/6 (about ±2–4 points).
  - With 2 experts the released pairing code builds a population of only 1 individual, so this cell is very sensitive to implementation details (see Discrepancies).
- **RHT claims:** only partly reproduce.
  - The per-cell RHT numbers are mostly within tolerance of Table 9. The exceptions are MGSM (higher than the paper at n ≤ 6, lower at n ≥ 8), CSQA with 2 experts (about 62 vs 71) and MATH with 2 experts (14.7 vs 13.4).
  - The claimed advantage over GENOME is much weaker. On average RHT gains +0.32 (paper +0.97) points and wins 18/30 (paper 22/30) cells.
  - There is no RHT gain on MMLU or MATH. The paper says the gains are "particularly pronounced" there.
  - The gain on MGSM is clear, but it is largest at small n, not at large n.
  - RHT also declines from 6 to 10 experts on MMLU, MGSM and CSQA, and is flat on MATH. That contradicts "when the performance of other methods approaches saturation, RHT continues to maintain steady improvement".
- **Overall:** MIXED. The baseline and saturation analysis reproduce. The method claim, consistent and growing RHT gains, does not.

## 4. Why not fully reproduced
- **The method as described is absent.** The paper's RHT (Gaussian difference followed by the nonlinear heavy-tail amplification T(x)) is not implemented. The released "RHT" is magnitude top-k pruning plus 1/density rescaling plus TIES inside GENOME. So I could only evaluate the released variant.
- **Data/code not released:** the D_know data and experts (Table 2/3), the GPT-2/GLUE merging code (Table 4), and the LLaMA-3.1-8B experts (Table 7).
- **Compute:** each GENOME/RHT run takes 0.5–2.5 h (10 evolution iterations, then a 10-individual test sweep). That allowed 4 complete seeds in 47 h. The Model Swarms, GENOME-DARE and GENOME-TIES baselines were not run.

## 6. Discrepancies
1. **code_vs_paper, major (RHT).**
   - *Paper (Sec. 3.3, Theorem 4):* RHT applies a Gaussian difference w' = w − g, then T(x) = sign(x)|x|^γ(1 + α e^{−β|x|}).
   - *Code (`scripts/RHT.sh` → `process_dare_ties_magnitude_with_rescaler`):* magnitude top-k pruning at density 0.7, rescaling by 1/0.7, then TIES. There are no γ, α, β or σ_g parameters anywhere.
2. **code_vs_paper, major (`generate_pair_sequences`).**
   - *Paper:* says it follows GENOME, with a population of N = 10.
   - *Code:* modified relative to upstream GENOME. The pools are shuffled with a time-based seed, so runs are not reproducible. Pairs are not repeated, so 2 experts give an initial population of 1 and 4 experts give 6. This affects the small-n cells.
3. **code_vs_paper, minor (seeds).**
   - *Paper:* averages over 5 seeds.
   - *Code:* `RHT.sh` lists 11 seeds, and the 5 used are unspecified.
4. **code_vs_paper, minor (MBPP context length).**
   - *Paper:* reports MBPP pass@1.
   - *Code:* the repo's vLLM deploy script uses max-model-len 4096 while MBPP asks for 4096 new tokens, so MBPP cannot run as released.
5. **paper_internal (RHT consistency).**
   - *Sec. 4.5:* "RHT consistently outperforms other baseline methods across all tasks".
   - *Table 9:* RHT is below GENOME-DARE-TIES in 8 of 30 (task, n) cells.
