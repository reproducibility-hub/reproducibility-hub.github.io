## Summary

The main result of the paper is Table 1: SIVR versus uncertainty baselines for 3 LLMs × 7 QA datasets. The "Average" column also includes 5 fact-checking datasets. We re-ran the full pipeline at the paper's scale:
- all 3 models (Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, Ministral-8B);
- all 12 datasets;
- greedy decoding, the released prompts and the released 80/20 split.

**Released baselines reproduce almost exactly.** Max Prob, Perplexity, Entropy, Temp-Scaled, Energy, CoE-R and CoE-C come within a median of 0.39 AUC points of the paper over 147 cells, with 84% of cells within 2 points. This confirms our data, prompts, labels and generation settings. One condition: generation must use `--max_new_tokens 1024`, which the paper does not state. The released default of 128 truncates almost all chain-of-thought answers.

**SIVR.**
- *Headline averages.* The Table-1 "Average" AUC (12 datasets) matches the paper:

  | Model | Default seed 42 | Mean over 5 split seeds | Paper |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Llama-3.2-3B | 78.86 | 79.54 | 79.53 |
  | Llama-3.1-8B | 78.91 | 79.93 | 80.86 |
  | Ministral-8B | 78.35 | 78.54 | 78.11 |

- *Per-cell AUC.* 12 of 21 cells are within tolerance (≤5% relative or ≤1 point) with the default seed, and 15 of 21 with 5-seed means. The largest shortfalls are all on Llama-3.1-8B:

  | Llama-3.1-8B dataset | Ours (5-seed mean) | Paper |
  |---|---|---|
  | MMLU | 54.7 | 64.1 |
  | MGSM | 83.5 | 89.0 |
  | MedMCQA | 71.3 | 76.0 |

- *FPR@95 and AUPR.* These come from 150–300-sample test splits and vary by many points across seeds. FPR@95 in particular often misses the paper's value.
- *Against the released baselines.* SIVR beats every released unsupervised baseline on 20/21 cells with the default seed and on 21/21 with seed averaging. The paper also reports 21/21.
- *Ablation (Tables 2 and 9).* The ordering SIVR > internal-variance-only > HS(PCA)-only holds for all three models. Our margins of SIVR over internal-variance-only are +1.5, +1.6 and +1.3 AUC (Llama-3B, Llama-8B, Ministral). The paper reports +1.9, +5.6 and +1.8, so the margin is much smaller for Llama-8B.

**What could not be checked.**
- *Comparisons with SE, SAR, P(True), SATMD+MSP, Lookback Lens, SAPLMA and TAD* (TAD is the strongest baseline in the paper). None of their code is released; P(True) only covers the true/false datasets. The claim "SIVR beats TAD/SAPLMA" is therefore NOT_COMPARABLE.
- *Generalisation and label-efficiency claims* from the abstract (Table 3 / Fig. 5 OOD transfer; Fig. 6 training-set size). The release has no OOD script, and these experiments and the Qwen-3 table (Table 6) were not run.

**Problems found in the release** (details under Discrepancies):
- **Untrained attention pooling.** The classifier's attention pooling is created fresh and random on every forward pass and is never trained, whereas Fig. 7 shows a registered, learned module. Fixing it changes nothing on average.
- **MMLU prompts omit the answer choices.** This explains the near-chance MMLU numbers for every method, in the paper as well.
- **Selection on the test split.** The best epoch is chosen on the test split. A held-out validation split lowers SIVR AUC by 3.6 points on average.
- **Mixed-checkpoint FPR@95/AUPR.** The reported FPR@95/AUPR come from the last epoch while AUC comes from the best epoch.
- **Facts dataset crashes** with an IndexError.
- **RAM.** The unmodified `eval.py` needs about 190 GB of host RAM for a 1000-sample CoT dataset.

## What was attempted

**Target.** Table 1 is the paper's main result: SIVR against 14 uncertainty-estimation baselines, for 3 instruction-tuned LLMs (Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, Ministral-8B) on 7 QA datasets, reporting AUC / FPR@95 / AUPR. We also ran:
- the 5 fact-checking datasets (Table 10), which the Table 1 "Average" column needs;
- the released ablation variants of Tables 2 and 9: HS (PCA) only, internal variance only, RNN head, full SIVR.

**Pipeline.** Everything follows `scripts/main.sh`. For every (model, dataset) pair:
1. Run `eval.py`: greedy generation, label extraction, the logit/CoE baselines, and the per-token features (10 hidden-state PCs plus circular variance, covariance log-det and entropy).
2. Run `train.py --arch transformer --features all`: the SIVR classifier on an 80/20 random split.

**Commands actually run** (all inside `./env`):
- `run_extract.sh` / `run_extract_fact.sh` call `codebase/eval_stream.py --model M --dataset_name D --subdataset S --data_portion 1.0 --max_new_tokens 1024 --save`.
  - `eval_stream.py` is a memory-streaming copy of `eval.py` (see below).
  - It produces bit-identical files: verified against the unmodified `eval.py` on TriviaQA with Llama-3.1-8B (labels, all 11 baseline scores, `tokenDict.pkl` and `hsPCA.pkl` have max abs difference 0.0).
- `run_train.sh` calls `codebase/train.py` once per variant:
  - `asrel`: the released logic;
  - `fixed`: `--learned_pool`;
  - `fixed_holdout`: `--learned_pool --holdout_val 0.125`;
  - `hs_*`, `var_*`: `--features hs|var`;
  - `rnn`: `--arch rnn`.
- `run_seeds.sh` repeats the training with 4 extra split seeds (`--seed 0..3`). This covers SIVR, IV-only and HS-only.
- Tables are built by `final_tables.py` (paper numbers are parsed from the PDF text by `paper_numbers.py`). Outputs: `final_tables.md`, `comparisons.json`, `comparison_all.csv`.

**Settings that differ from, or are not stated in, the paper/release:**
- **Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct** was loaded from the ungated mirror `unsloth/Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct`, which has the same weights. The official `meta-llama` repo returned HTTP 403 for our token.
- **`--max_new_tokens 1024` instead of the released default 128.**
  - The paper does not give the value.
  - With 128, CoT answers are truncated and dropped by the parser: Llama-3.2-3B parsed only 29/1000 MedMCQA answers, i.e. a 6-sample test set.
  - The repo history shows `default=1024` until the "restore default arguments" commit (012d4d1, 13 Apr 2026). The pre-release `scripts/eval.sh` did not override it.
  - At 1024, 90–100% of answers parse, and our baseline AUCs match the paper's to within a median of 0.33 points.
  - The 128-token outputs are kept in `codebase/outputs_128tok/`, `results_128tok/` and `logs/run128/`.
- **Environment.** Python 3.12, torch 2.6.0+cu124, transformers 4.56.2, datasets 3.6.0, plus `evaluate`, `rouge_score`, `nltk` and `accelerate`. The released `requirements.txt` cannot work as written:
  - it pins torch 2.3.1, which has no Python 3.13 wheels, while the README asks for Python 3.13;
  - it pins transformers 4.51.3, which does not accept the `dtype=` argument used in `eval.py`;
  - `evaluate` and `rouge_score` are missing.
- **Single seed per (model, dataset).** Seed 42 is the released default, as in the paper. Extra seeds were run only to measure variance.
- **Baselines.** Only the baselines whose code is released were run: Max Prob, Perplexity, Entropy, Temp-Scaled, Energy, CoE-R, CoE-C (all in `eval.py`/`score.py`). As in the release, they are scored on the full dataset, while SIVR is scored on its 20% test split.
- **Baselines not run.** SE, SAR, SATMD+MSP, Lookback Lens, SAPLMA and TAD are not in the release. P(True) (`p_true.py`) only supports the true/false datasets and needs a missing `prompts/p_true_f.txt`.
- **Not run:** the OOD transfer experiment (Table 3, Fig. 5/8), the training-set-size curve (Fig. 6) and the Qwen-3 results (Table 6). The release has no script for OOD transfer; the other two were left out for compute and time. The HS (full hidden states) and "CovDet, AngVar" ablation rows have no released switch.

## Result match: after fixes vs as released

**After fixes (`ours`).** This is SIVR with a learned AttnPool (`--learned_pool`, as in Fig. 7). FPR@95 and AUPR are taken at the epoch with the best AUC.

**As released (`as rel.`).** This is the released training logic: a fresh random AttnPool on every forward pass, and FPR@95/AUPR as printed by the released `train.py`, i.e. from the last epoch.

How faithful the as-released numbers are:
- Features come from `eval_stream.py`. Its outputs are bit-identical to the unmodified `eval.py`, which we ran on TriviaQA and SciQ with Llama-3.1-8B.
- For those two datasets, the pristine `git archive HEAD` copy in `codebase_asreleased/` gives the same AUCs (90.51 at 128 tokens on TriviaQA, 87.89 on SciQ). Logs are `logs/asrel_*`.
- The unmodified `eval.py` cannot finish the CoT datasets at 1024 tokens in 111 GB of RAM. It reached 85 GB after 454 of 1000 MedMCQA samples; `logs/asrel_mem.log` has the watchdog trace.
- At the released default of 128 tokens it does run, but it keeps only a handful of samples: 29/1000 on MedMCQA for Llama-3.2-3B (see `logs/run128/`).
- With the RAM-only change, the as-released numbers are as close to the paper as the fixed ones. The 5-seed 12-dataset averages are 79.85 / 80.04 / 78.91. Both labels are therefore TREND_MATCH.
- `true_false/facts` crashes as released (IndexError), so the "True-False" ablation column is marked DOES_NOT_RUN as released.

## Discrepancies

### Code vs paper
1. **AttnPool is never trained** (`utils/models.py`, `TransformerClassifier.forward`). *Major.*
   - The code calls `AttnPool(self.d_model).to(h.device)(h, mask)`, which creates a fresh random pooling network on every call, in both training and evaluation.
   - Paper Fig. 7 shows `(pool): AttnPool(...)` as a registered sub-module.
   - Effect on single-seed AUC over 36 settings: +0.14 mean, 1.13 mean absolute difference, at most 9.5 (Llama-8B MMLU). No effect after averaging over seeds.
2. **`--max_new_tokens` default is 128** (`eval.py`). *Minor.*
   - The paper does not state a value. Its numbers require near-complete CoT answers, and the repo default was 1024 until the commit just before release.
   - With 128, CoT answers rarely finish: Llama-3.2-3B kept 29/1000 MedMCQA answers, and 3/24 in a CommonsenseQA smoke test.
3. **MMLU prompt has no answer choices** (`utils/dataset.py::format_prompt`, `prompts/mmlu.txt`). *Major.*
   - Only the question is inserted, so the model guesses A–D blind.
   - Every method, in the paper and here, sits near AUC 50 on MMLU.
4. **Epoch selection on the test split** (`train.py`). *Major (evaluation protocol).*
   - The reported AUC is the best of up to 500 epochs, measured on the 20% evaluation split itself. The paper describes no validation split.
   - Selecting the epoch on a held-out 12.5% of the training split instead gives 3.58 points lower AUC on average over 36 settings. Part of that drop comes from the smaller training set.
5. **Final print mixes checkpoints** (`train.py`). *Minor.*
   - FPR@95 and AUPR are printed from the last epoch, AUC from the best epoch.
6. **CovDet centring** (`score.py::VarianceScore.covdet`). *Minor.*
   - The code centres each layer vector over its own hidden dimensions, not across layers as in Eq. (1). It also returns the mean, not the sum, of the log-eigenvalues.
   - Its correlation with the Eq. (1) version is 0.95 (Pearson) on 466 tokens; see `covdet_check.py`.
7. **True-false subsets are truncated or crash** (`prepare_dataset`). *Minor.*
   - Animals is cut from 1008 to 1000 statements.
   - Facts has only 613 rows, so `select(range(1000))` raises an IndexError. Paper Appendix C.1 says all statements are used.
8. **CommonsenseQA size.** The code uses all 1221 questions; the paper says 1000 random ones. *Minor.*
9. **SciQ/MMLU split wording.** Appendix C.1 assigns "validation/test, 1531/1000" to SciQ/MMLU; the code uses SciQ test (1000) and MMLU validation (1531). *Minor, no effect.*
10. **Requirements are inconsistent.** torch 2.3.1 has no wheels for the README's Python 3.13. transformers 4.51.3 rejects the `dtype=` argument used in `eval.py`. `evaluate` and `rouge_score` are not listed. *Minor, no effect.*

### Paper-internal
1. **"Average" column.** In Tables 1, 2 and 9 it is the mean over all 12 datasets, including the five fact-checking sets of Table 10. It is not the mean of the columns shown. For example, Llama-3B SIVR: the 7 shown AUCs average 74.03, while the column says 79.53.
2. **Table 9(a) vs Table 10(a).** The True-False SIVR AUC is 90.81 in Table 9(a), but the four Table 10(a) values average 87.29.
3. **Table 4 vs Table 1(a).** The SE and SAR AUCs are swapped between the two tables.

## Codebase completeness: MINOR_FIXES

The released code implements both the method and the Table-1 pipeline (`scripts/main.sh` runs `eval.py` and then `train.py`). Its logic reproduces the paper's numbers. What it needed:
- one CLI value (`--max_new_tokens 1024`);
- a one-line crash fix (`facts.csv`);
- environment repairs;
- because of our 111 GB RAM limit, a restructuring of the evaluation loop that streams features per sample (`codebase/eval_stream.py`, new file). Its outputs are bit-identical to `eval.py`.

We judged this MINOR rather than MAJOR because the restructuring changes no results. On a machine with about 200 GB of RAM, the original `eval.py` would produce the same data.

**Changes and additions** (all uncommitted in `./codebase`; `git diff` shows them):
- `eval_stream.py` (new): the memory-streaming copy of `eval.py` described above. It has the same generation, scores, PCA and saved files, plus a `baseline_results.csv`.
- `utils/dataset.py`: `select(range(min(1000, len(dataset))))` for the true/false data (fixes the IndexError on `facts`).
- `utils/models.py`: optional `learned_pool` flag that registers AttnPool as `self.pool`. The default is the released behaviour.
- `train.py`, new options, all optional:
  - `--learned_pool`, `--seed` (the released code hard-codes 42), `--holdout_val`;
  - `--result_json`, which also records FPR@95/AUPR at the best-AUC epoch;
  - a final `[repro]` print line.
  - With default arguments it gives exactly the released numbers (checked against the pristine `train.py`).
- `outputs/` (generated features) and `outputs_128tok/` (the first pass at 128 tokens).

**Scripts written outside the release** (top-level directory):
- `run_extract.sh`, `run_extract_fact.sh`, `run_all_model.sh`: extraction drivers.
- `run_train.sh`, `train_daemon.sh`: the 8 training variants per (model, dataset).
- `run_seeds.sh`, `run_seeds_asrel.sh`: split seeds 0–3.
- `run_asreleased.sh`, `run_asrel_mem.sh`: pristine-code runs and the RAM test.
- `covdet_check.py`: CovDet versus Eq. (1).
- `paper_numbers.py`: parses the paper's tables from the PDF text.
- `aggregate.py`, `final_tables.py`, `make_status.py`: produce the tables, `comparisons.json` and `status.json`.

**Not in the release:**
- the SE, SAR, SATMD+MSP, Lookback Lens, SAPLMA and TAD baselines;
- P(True) for QA (only the true/false version exists, and it needs a missing `prompts/p_true_f.txt`);
- the OOD-transfer experiment (Table 3 / Figs. 5 and 8);
- switches for the "HS (full)" and "CovDet, AngVar" ablation rows.
