Protocol I used (the paper is silent on most of these; each choice is an assumption):
- **Relevance:** the out position is [CLS] for encoders and DeiT, and the last token for LLMs. The [CLS] relevance is dropped, as the repo does. The class for In+Class is the model's predicted class/token. Tensor slices are computed with the repo's Jacobian method in fp32.
- **Positive perturbation:** remove the top-k most relevant elements, k = round(f·n) for f ∈ {0, 0.01, …, 0.30} (31 steps).
  - Text: the tokens are deleted ("Fraction of tokens removed").
  - Images: the 16×16 patches are set to 0 in normalized pixel space.
- **HS-MSE:** mean over D of (h_orig − h_pert)², where h is the output-position hidden state after the final norm. This is the same "output" the repo uses for In+Out; for DeiT it is LN(hidden_states[-1]).
- **AOPC:** |p_orig(c) − p_pert(c)| for the original prediction c.
- **AUC:** trapezoid integral over f ∈ [0, 0.3], averaged over samples.
- **Data:**
  - IMDB test, 1000 random reviews (seed 0), truncated to 128 tokens.
  - WikiText-103-raw test, concatenated, 500 random non-overlapping 128-token windows.
  - ImageNet: the official ILSVRC/imagenet-1k is gated and this account has no access, so I used the ungated mirror `mrm8488/ImageNet1K-val` (the ImageNet val set; the paper says "test set", whose labels are not public, and labels are not needed here). 1000 random images from 2 of its 14 shards.
- **Sample counts:** the paper gives none. I used 1000 per encoder/vision model and 500 per LLM (because of runtime).

The repo's hooks require all of these, so the authors must have used an unreleased modified model. With the adapter, the tensor reproduces the Pico logits to 1e-5.
- `compare.py`, `summarize.py`, `run_all.sh`: reporting and orchestration.
- `smoke.py`, `check_deit.py`, `check_pico.py`: correctness checks.

Environment fix:
- **transformers pinned to 4.53.3.** With 4.57.1, ViT's `hidden_states[-1]` already has the final LayerNorm applied, so the repo applies it twice for DeiT. The tensor-vs-model hidden-state check was then off by up to 2.9, and In+Out used a wrong output vector. With 4.53.3 the tensor matches the model output to about 1e-5.

Other notes:
- The repo's attention hook rejects batch size > 1, so I run perturbations one at a time.
- The repo adds a "tensor_input+norm" variant that the paper doesn't report; it is shown in the logs only.

## Discrepancies
- **paper_internal:** In Sec 4.1 (vision), the text says Tensor In+Out AUC "exceeds 0.82", Norm "reaches 0.66" and baselines are "below 0.6". The Fig 3 legend gives 0.084 / 0.066 / ≤0.059, a factor-10 typo.
- **paper_internal:** App. A says "across all benchmarks … consistently outperforming the baselines", and Sec 4.1 says "top or second-best". But Table 1 has several cells where baselines beat every tensor variant:
  - Phi HS-MSE: 0.934 and 0.905 > 0.892, so tensor is only 3rd.
  - Pico HS-MSE: GlbEnc Mean 0.225 > 0.223.
  - Pythia AOPC: 0.148 > 0.147.
- **code_vs_paper (minor):** Pico is used in the paper, but the release can't run it. The name isn't mapped, and no adapter for the public model is provided.
