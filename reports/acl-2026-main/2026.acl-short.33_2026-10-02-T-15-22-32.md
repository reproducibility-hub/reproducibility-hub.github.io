## What the paper claims (target: Table 1, Section 3.2, Figure 3)
- Scoring in full BF16 creates spurious ties, which give large metric ranges and positive biases of the tie-oblivious score. For Qwen3-Reranker on MIRACL the paper reports nDCG@10 range 25.59 and MRR@10 range 38.03, with bias up to +9.08.
- HPS (upcasting the final logits or embeddings to FP32 before softmax, sigmoid or dot product) collapses these ties. Softmax ranges fall to about 1, sigmoid ranges drop by about 10x, and pairwise-product models reach exactly 0.
- TRM's E[M] shows a ranking flip that M_obl hides: under BF16 M_obl, Qwen3-Reranker looks better than gte, but E[M] and HPS put it below gte.
- Figure 3: under M_obl, BF16 curves lie above FP32 at every cutoff, while under E[M] BF16 lies below FP32.

## What I did
1. Cloned `https://github.com/kisuyang/tie-aware-retrieval-metrics` into `./codebase`.
   - It is a small library (`trm.evaluate`) for expected, max, min and tie-oblivious values of nDCG, MRR, MAP, Recall, etc.
   - Its 23 unit tests pass. I made **no edits** to it.
   - The README states that HPS is omitted. There are no scripts for models, datasets or tables.
2. Created the env with Python 3.11, torch 2.6.0+cu124, transformers 4.53.2, mteb 1.38.38 (the version the paper cites), sentence-transformers 4.1.0, and the codebase installed with `pip install -e`.
3. Wrote the pipeline myself:
   - `scripts/score.py`:
     - Loads data exactly as MTEB 1.38.38 does:
       - `MIRACLReranking`, English, BM25 top-100 candidates in BM25 order.
       - `AskUbuntuDupQuestions` test set, positives followed by negatives (the index order the paper says MTEB uses).
     - Runs each model with its official usage recipe in the model dtype:
       - Qwen3-Reranker: softmax over the yes/no logits.
       - bge and gte rerankers: sigmoid.
       - Qwen3-Embedding: last-token pooling plus cosine.
       - e5: mean pooling with `query:`/`passage:` prefixes plus cosine.
     - Uses batch size 16 and max length 4096 (512 for e5), as in the paper.
     - From the same forward pass it stores the low-precision score (scoring function computed in BF16/FP16) and the HPS score (logits or embeddings upcast to FP32 first).
   - `scripts/evaluate.py` builds Table 1 using `trm.evaluate`; M_obl is TRM's index-preserving order.
   - `scripts/cutoffs.py` covers Figure 3 and `scripts/compare.py` compares against the paper.
   - Commands:
     ```
     HF_DATASETS_TRUST_REMOTE_CODE=1 scripts/run_all.sh
     ./env/bin/python scripts/evaluate.py [--low fp16] [--filter mteb]
     ./env/bin/python scripts/cutoffs.py
     ./env/bin/python scripts/compare.py
     ```

### Settings the paper does not specify (my choices)
- **Qwen3 instruction.** I used the models' default instruction, "Given a web search query, retrieve relevant passages that answer the query".
  - With the MTEB task prompt instead, Qwen3-Reranker on AskUbuntu gives E[MAP@3] 33.68 with almost no BF16 ties (range 0.75). With the default instruction it gives 31.12 (range 3.91), and the paper reports 31.13 (range 4.03). So the default instruction is clearly what the authors used.
  - The other variants are kept as `data/scores/*_mtebinstr.pkl` and `*_noinstr.pkl`.
- **Query sets.**
  - MIRACL: queries with at least one relevant candidate. This gives exactly 717 of 799, as stated in the paper.
  - AskUbuntu: all 375 queries, as stated in the paper (an average of about 6 positives only holds with all 375). MTEB's own evaluator drops 14 all-positive queries; that variant is in `logs/table1_bf16_mtebfilter.txt`.
- **Pooling and normalisation.** For bi-encoders, pooling runs in the model dtype. The low-precision variant does normalisation and the dot product in the model dtype; HPS upcasts the pooled embedding before normalising.
