## 1. What the paper claims
HOPE fuses three parts with a learnable MLP:
- a supervised DepRoBERTa-large + LoRA multi-label "Symptom Extractor";
- an unsupervised, transductive K-means over the test texts, with centroids initialised from LLM (Claude Sonnet 4) few-shot-enhanced symptom descriptions and paired-stream embeddings (nomic-embed-text-v1.5 + modernbert-embed-base);
- the fusion MLP itself.

Main results:
- **Table 2**: eRisk 2025 Task 1 ranking (MAP 39.45, R-PREC 45.84, P@10 81.43, NDCG 69.29).
- **Table 3**: classification on ReDSM5, PRIMATE and DepressionEmo (mean ± std over 10 runs).
- **Figure 3**: ablations on eRisk and ReDSM5 only.

## 3. What I ran
Everything ran in `env/` (Python 3.11, torch 2.6.0+cu124, transformers 4.51.3, peft 0.14.0, sentence-transformers 3.4.1, scikit-learn 1.2.2, spaCy 3.8.5; these are the paper's App. B.1 versions) on 1×H200.

Pipeline for each seed s = 0..9 (10 runs, as in the paper):
1. `repro/prep_data.py`: DepressionEmo json → CSV with 8 label columns, in the symptom order the codebase uses in `fusion/*`.
2. `repro/run_extractor.py --seed s`: supervised extractor.
   - Uses the codebase's `extractor.model.get_model` (DepRoBERTa-large, LoRA r=32, α=64, dropout 0.1, query/value) and `extractor.training.train_model`: AdamW, lr 1e-5, cosine schedule, warmup 0.1, wd 0.01, batch size 32, 20 epochs, max_len 256, BCE.
   - Trained on train. The final (last-step) model is used, as in the code (no best-checkpoint reload).
   - Writes sigmoid scores for train / val / test.
3. `repro/run_unsupervised.py`: transductive clustering, run separately on each split.
   - Relevance filter τ=0.1. Every DepressionEmo post passes it.
   - spaCy sentence segmentation via the codebase's `data_processing.text_preprocess`.
   - Both embedding models, L2-normalised as in the codebase.
   - K-means: K=8, init = mean embedding of each symptom's descriptions, max_iter 300, tol 1e-4.
   - d = 1/(1+‖E(s)−c_j‖) using the *fitted* centroids (paper Eq. 5), max over a post's sentences, averaged over the two models (Eq. 6–7).
   - Also writes a variant that mirrors the released `kmeans.py` scoring: cosine distance to the *initial* centroids.
4. `repro/run_fusion.py`: fusion MLP.
   - Uses the codebase's `fusion.training.main` (hidden 64, lr 2e-3, 100 epochs, batch size 32, cosine) and `fusion.infer.infer`.
   - The checkpoint with the best val macro-F1 among those the code saves is selected.
   - Test metrics: Mac-P/R/F1 and Mic-F1 at threshold 0.5.
5. `repro/aggregate.py`: mean ± sample std over the 10 seeds.

Commands: `repro/run_extractors.sh <seeds>` and `repro/run_downstream.sh`. Logs are in `logs/`, per-seed outputs in `work/runs/seed*/`, and the summary in `work/summary.json`.

**Substitute centroid descriptions:** `repro/descriptions/depressionemo/*.txt`. I wrote these myself as an LLM, following the paper's recipe: 5 sentences per symptom, varying expression and severity, informed by symptom definitions and 20 randomly sampled training posts (random.seed(42)). They are **not** the authors' Claude Sonnet 4 outputs.

Settings that differ from the paper:
- the descriptions (substitutes, as above);
- the fusion MLP has no dropout (released code; the paper says 0.1);
- fusion checkpoint selection is by best val macro-F1, because the paper doesn't specify it;
- nomic models are used without task prefixes, as the code does.

What these show:
- **Where the gain comes from.** Fusion improves Mac-F1 by about 2.3 points over the thresholded extractor. All of that gain also appears when the MLP sees only the supervised scores.
- **The clustering scores add nothing measurable.** HOPE minus the sup-only MLP is +0.03 Mac-F1 and −0.02 Mic-F1 on average (paired t-test over 10 seeds: p = 0.74 and p = 0.65).
- **Other branch choices don't matter either.** The paired stream vs. a single model makes no difference, and neither does fitted vs. initial centroids.
- **Why the descriptions probably aren't the reason.** The clustering scores do carry some signal (46.6 Mac-F1 on their own), but it is redundant with the supervised scores. Since the unsupervised branch contributes nothing regardless of these choices, my substitute descriptions are unlikely to explain the ~4-point gap. Still, I can't rule out that the authors' exact descriptions matter.
- **The gap is mostly upstream.** Most of the gap is already in the supervised extractor: 75.0–76.8 val macro-F1 across seeds with the paper's hyperparameters.

## 7. Discrepancies
**Code vs paper**
1. **major** — `clustering/kmeans.py:find_symptom_sentences`.
   - Paper (Eq. 5): d = 1/(1+‖E(s)−c_j‖), Euclidean, to the K-means-*adjusted* centroids ("automatic class-anchor adjustment").
   - Code: cosine distance to the *initial* description centroids (`centroids_norm`). The fitted `cluster_centers_` and `labels_` are never used, so K-means has no effect on the scores.
   - On DepressionEmo this made no measurable difference (75.72 vs 75.72 Mac-F1). It may matter for eRisk.
2. **major** — `clustering/kmeans.py` and `clustering/ensemble.py`. The code adds steps the paper doesn't describe:
   - top-k (5000) selection per symptom with min-max rescaling inside the top-k;
   - a hard-coded first-person-pronoun substring filter (e.g. "me", "my", "am ");
   - per-model max-normalisation before averaging.

   The paper describes a plain average of the two models' scores (Eq. 7). These steps affect the eRisk ranking results, which I could not test.
3. **minor** — `fusion/model.py`: the MLP has no dropout. The paper (App. B.2.3) says dropout 0.1.
4. **minor** — `fusion/infer.py` / `fusion/training.py` defaults use 32 unsupervised features (`unsup_size=32`, `multilabel-questionaire-32_avg_sim_merged_output.csv`). The paper's MLP input is 2×|L| (= 16 for DepressionEmo). It is unclear what the 32 columns are.
5. **minor** — `extractor/infer.py`: max_length=128 at inference. The paper (and the training code) use 256.
6. **minor** — `clustering/kmeans.py`: K-means runs on L2-normalised embeddings, while the paper describes Euclidean distance on raw embeddings.

**Paper-internal**
1. Sec. 3.3 says the LLM descriptions were generated from "20 randomly selected seed samples from the training set". App. B.2.2 lists "Few-shot seeding examples: 5".
