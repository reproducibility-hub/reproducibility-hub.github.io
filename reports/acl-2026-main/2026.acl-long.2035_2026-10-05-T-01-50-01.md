# Reproduction report — RATE (2026.acl-long.2035)

## Verdict
- **Verdict: FAILED.** The dashboard rule forces this because codebase completeness is `MISSING_DATA`. In plain terms: the released checkpoints reproduce every LR-bench number exactly, but the training and the CMU Gold column cannot be reproduced from what was released.
- **Result match (after fixes): NUMBERS_MATCH.** This covers all 8 LR-bench numbers for RATE-8B and RATE-0.6B in Table 1, matched to 4 decimals. No fixes were actually needed.
- **Result match as released: DOES_NOT_RUN.** The rules require this label for `MISSING_DATA`. The unmodified code does produce the LR-bench numbers exactly; it cannot produce the Gold column or any training run.
- **Coverage: SUBSET.** We ran LR-PC and LR-RC for both released models at full scale. The Gold column was run only with substitute keywords. Training, baselines, the human evaluation and Table 4 were not run.
- **Codebase completeness: MISSING_DATA.**
- **Flags:** `BASELINE_STRONGER_THAN_REPORTED`.

## What the paper claims
- **LR-bench.** A new benchmark with 212 paper-centric (PC) and 1,184 reviewer-centric (RC) preference tuples.
- **RATE.** Reviewer profiles built from LLM-extracted keywords, then a Qwen3-Embedding model fine-tuned with LoRA on 3,000 BM25-mined, annotation-free preference tuples.
- **Main result (Table 1).** Qwen3-Max + RATE-8B is best on LR-PC, LR-RC and the CMU Gold set: average loss 0.1904 and average accuracy 77.41%.
- **Ablation (Table 3).** Fine-tuning brings a large gain over zero-shot Qwen3-Embedding-8B (average accuracy 61.04%).

### Interpretation
- **RATE's own numbers reproduce exactly.** The released checkpoints and inference code give exactly the LR-bench numbers in the paper.
- **The fine-tuning gain is much smaller than Table 3 implies.**
  - The paper reports zero-shot Qwen3-Embedding-8B at 64.46% (PC) and 61.73% (RC).
  - Run through the released `main.py`, the same model gets 70.75% (PC) and 75.25% (RC).
  - On LR-RC that ties RATE-8B's accuracy, 75.25%, and the loss gap is only 0.2030 vs 0.1926.
  - With a different prompt format, zero-shot 8B reaches LR-RC loss 0.1957 and 75.68% accuracy, which beats RATE-8B on RC.
  - RATE-8B still clearly beats zero-shot on LR-PC: 0.1795 vs ≥0.2301 loss.
  - On Gold with substitute keywords, RATE-8B also beats zero-shot (0.2167 vs 0.2368), but by far less than the paper's 0.1991 vs 0.4307.
- **The comparison with baselines can't be checked here.** Claims against SPECTER2 PRX, SciNCL, TPMS, CoF and the LLM scorers have no released baseline code, so they are NOT_COMPARABLE.
- **The training can't be re-run.** The annotation-free training itself cannot be re-run because its training data and data-construction code are not released.

## Why not fully reproduced
- **Missing generated data (Gold profiles).** Gold reviewer keyword profiles are not released. Regenerating them needs closed APIs (Qwen3-Max or GLM-4.6), which aren't available here, so we used an open-model substitute.
- **Missing training data and pipeline.** The 3,000 training tuples and the pipeline that builds them are not released: arXiv crawl of 161k papers, GROBID, author disambiguation, LLM keywords, embedding recall and BM25 mining.
- **No baseline or extra-evaluation code.** Nothing is released for the baselines, the human evaluation (Table 2) or the Table 4 comparison.

## Discrepancies
1. **code_vs_paper, minor, affects results: profile text format.**
   - Paper (Sec. 4.1, App. D): keywords are joined by commas after the prefix "The reviewer's research keywords include:", capped at 512 keywords, with the App. D task prompts.
   - `train/training.py` follows the paper's format.
   - Inference (`scripts/RATE.py`), which produced the paper's numbers, does something else: it space-joins per-paper keyword strings, adds no prefix and no cap, and uses different "Represent this …" instructions.
   - With the paper's format, RATE-8B gets LR-PC 0.1928/74.06%.
2. **code_vs_paper, minor, affects results: pooling mismatch.**
   - Training uses last-token pooling.
   - The released checkpoints have no sentence-transformers config, so inference silently uses mean pooling.
   - The paper's numbers correspond to mean pooling. With last-token pooling, RATE-8B drops to LR-PC 0.2434/68.87%.
3. **code_vs_paper, minor: training defaults differ from Table 8.**
   - `training.py` defaults: lr 5e-5, τ 0.05, patience 5, ce_weight 1.0, and reviewer-centric tuples off unless `--silver_include_reviewer_centric` is passed.
   - Table 8: lr 2.3e-5, τ 0.0634, patience 6, CE weight 0.915, dual-view training.
4. **paper_internal, affects results: the Gold "Acc." column is 1 − Loss.**
   - In 15 of 17 rows of Tables 1 and 3, the Gold "Acc." equals exactly 1 − Loss. Examples: TPMS 0.2811 → 71.89%; RATE-8B 0.1991 → 80.09%; zero-shot 0.4307 → 56.93%.
   - That is not the pairwise accuracy defined in Sec. 5.3. The true pairwise accuracy of TPMS on Gold is 65.43%.
   - This inflates every "Avg. Acc." value.
5. **paper_internal: SciNCL average.** Table 1 gives SciNCL Avg. Acc. as 73.64%. The mean of its own per-dataset values is 73.94%, which is the figure in Table 5.
6. **paper_internal / unverifiable: the zero-shot row.** The paper doesn't specify how the Table 3 zero-shot row was configured, and none of the 4 configurations we ran reproduces it (see the flag above).
