# Reproduction report — Saber (ACL 2026, 2026.acl-long.165)

**Verdict: PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED** · Result match: **MIXED** · Coverage: **FULL** · Codebase completeness: **RUNS_AS_IS**

## Summary
We ran every cell of **Table 1** at full scale: LLaDA-8B-Instruct × 10 sampling methods × HumanEval(+ET), MBPP(+ET) and LiveCodeBench. We also ran **Table 8** (µ sensitivity, HumanEval) and a numerical-sensitivity probe. The released code ran with **no source edits**; only YAML config values were changed.

* **Baselines reproduce very well.** On HumanEval, Confidence gives 43.29 (paper 43.29), Entropy 41.46 (41.46) and SAR 35.98 (35.98). Most other baselines are within ~1–2.5 points. 64 of the 88 compared numbers fall within tolerance.
* **MBPP Saber reproduces exactly:** 44.73 Pass@1 (paper 44.73), ET 32.08 (33.02), 110.35 steps (110.96). It is the best method on MBPP, as claimed.
* **HumanEval Saber does not reproduce.** In the paper's main setting (µ=1/8) we get **39.02 vs 45.12** (ET 31.10 vs 35.98), even though the step count matches (118.09 vs 118.92). That is *below* the Confidence baseline (43.29), which contradicts the headline claim for this benchmark. Every µ in Table 8 also lands 3–6 points below the paper, while its step count matches to within about 5%.
* **LiveCodeBench Saber:** 8.75 vs 11.00. This is below Confidence (9.25), WINO (9.75) and Entropy (12.50), so Saber is not the best method on LCB here.
* **Speed claim holds.** Saber's generation-time speedup over Confidence is 2.23× / 2.37× / 2.11× (HE / MBPP / LCB), vs. the paper's 3.15× / 2.05× / 2.34×. The paper's "251.4% average speedup" is exactly the mean of these three ratios; ours averages 2.24×.
* **Pass@1 improvement claim does not hold.** Our Saber − Confidence Pass@1 is −4.27 (HE), +2.11 (MBPP) and −0.50 (LCB), vs. the paper's +1.83 / +1.87 / +1.25.
* All methods run at temperature 0, and outputs are **deterministic and byte-identical across the flash, mem-efficient and math SDPA kernels** (tested for Saber and for Confidence). So the HumanEval gap is not run-to-run noise or attention-kernel numerics. We cannot rule out other hardware or library differences (A6000 vs H200), but the baselines reproduce to the digit on the same setup.

## Setup
* Hardware: 1× NVIDIA H200 (the paper used an A6000). Times are therefore not directly comparable, but ratios between methods are.
* Env: conda Python 3.11, `pip install -r codebase/requirements.txt` (torch 2.1.2+cu121, transformers 4.49.0), no changes.
* Model: `GSAI-ML/LLaDA-8B-Instruct` from the HF cache.
* Data: HumanEval, HumanEval-ET, MBPP (sanitized, 427 problems) and MBPP-ET as shipped in the repo. LiveCodeBench = `livecodebench/code_generation_lite` **release_v1 (400 problems)**. The paper doesn't give the LCB version; its numbers are multiples of 0.25, which is consistent with 400 problems.
* Generation: gen_length 256, block_length 256 (128 for SAR), temperature 0, one sample per problem.
* Commands: each run is `cd codebase && python eval.py --config ../run_configs/<dataset>_<method>.yaml`, wrapped by `run_one.sh` (flock-serialized so timings are clean) and queued by `queue.sh` / `queue2.sh` / `queue3.sh`. Logs are in `logs/`, generations in `codebase/results/`, and the parsed summary is `results_summary.json`.
* LCB scoring: `python score_lcb.py codebase/results/livecodebench_*.jsonl`. This calls the official `lcb_runner` grader (`codegen_metrics`, timeout 6 s) on the repo's own extracted completions.

## Why it is not fully reproduced
* **HumanEval Saber (main claim)** is 6.1 points below the paper, and **LCB Saber** is 2.25 points below. On both benchmarks Saber no longer beats the standard Confidence sampler, while Saber's step counts and all key baselines match the paper closely.
* We found no bug on our side. The code runs unchanged, it's deterministic, and it's insensitive to attention kernels. The Saber algorithm in `decoding.py` is logically unchanged since the initial commit (only µ was turned into a parameter). A likely explanation is that the paper's HumanEval/LCB Saber numbers came from a code or setting state that isn't in the release, but we cannot verify this.
* Minor: LCB baselines Entropy (12.50 vs 4.00), Fast-dLLM(+parallel) (8.75 vs 2.30) and ReMDM (5.75 vs 3.30) are much higher than reported. MBPP Fast-dLLM(+parallel) is 43.33 vs 39.34 with 99 vs 73 steps. The paper does not give exact settings for these baselines or the LCB version/scorer.
* Timing: in the HE Fast-dLLM run (only), the first ~7 minutes overlapped with an accidental duplicate WINO run (a bash wrapper re-executed after being overwritten mid-run; see `logs/dup_humaneval_wino_accidental.log.txt`). Its accuracy is unaffected, but its time (22:56) is slightly inflated. All other runs were strictly serialized.
