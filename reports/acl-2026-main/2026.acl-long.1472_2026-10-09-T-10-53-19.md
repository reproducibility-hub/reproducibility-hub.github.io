## What the paper claims / what was targeted
The paper scores the original and 7 GPT-4o meaning-preserving perturbations of long-document summaries with 6 reference-free factuality metrics. Scoring is retrieval-based: SBERT picks the top-K source sentences, a ±w window of context is added, the score is the max over snippets, then averaged over summary sentences. MiniCheck-7B instead sees the full document (32k context). The main results are:
- Fig. 2 / Tables 4–6 (App. D): mean score per metric × perturbation × dataset;
- Table 1: effect of window size w;
- Fig. 3: score vs. claim–document similarity;
- App. E: mean absolute deltas.

The perturbed summaries are released, so GPT-4o is **not** needed: it was only the perturbation generator. Everything else uses open models: facebook/bart-large-cnn, SummaC (mnli), AlignScore-base, UniEval-sum, bespokelabs/Bespoke-MiniCheck-7B, and SBERT bert-base-nli-mean-tokens.

## Discrepancies
1. **[code_vs_paper, major, affects results] SQuALITY originals are scored on 4 concatenated references.** The SQuALITY `response` column is a stringified list of 4 reference summaries. The perturbations were made from reference [0], and Table 2's statistics (12.5 sentences / 273 tokens) also describe a single reference. Yet the released/paper "Original" scores for all retrieval-based metrics (Tables 1 and 6) were computed on the raw list string: re-running on that string reproduces the released per-example values, while reference [0] alone doesn't. As a result, the SQuALITY Δs in Fig. 2 / App. E compare a 4-reference original against a 1-reference perturbed summary. MiniCheck full-document scoring correctly uses [0].
2. **[code_vs_paper (released artifacts vs. released data), major, affects results] LexAbSumm Paraphrased.** The released scores for SummaC-Conv, AlignScore and UniEval in this column are uncorrelated (r ≈ 0) with the released `summ_paraphrased` texts. All other cells match row by row (r ≈ 0.99–1.0). They were probably computed on a different (earlier) set of paraphrases. On the released texts the paper's values (0.21 / 0.38) don't reproduce (ours 0.34 / 0.54), and neither does the claim of a paraphrase-induced AlignScore drop on legal text.
3. **[paper_internal] MiniCheck in Table 1 vs. Tables 4–6.** Section 4 says MiniCheck receives the full document, and Tables 4–6 report Original = 0.84 / 0.32 / 0.56. Table 1 at the same default w=1 reports 0.53 / 0.15 / 0.15, which is a retrieval-based MiniCheck run. The paper doesn't explain the difference.
4. **[code_vs_paper, minor] Per-claim filtering.** The per-claim analysis (Fig. 3) drops sentences of ≤20 characters from both source and summary. The paper doesn't mention this.

## Why not fully reproduced
Every number traceable to the released data reproduces. The gaps come from inconsistencies between the released score files / paper and the released inputs, not from compute or missing models:
- the LexAbSumm Paraphrased cells (scores computed on different texts than the released ones);
- the SQuALITY original-vs-perturbed reference mismatch, which reproduces numerically but makes the SQuALITY deltas methodologically inconsistent.
