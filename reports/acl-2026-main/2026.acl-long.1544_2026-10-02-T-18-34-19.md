## What the paper claims (and what I targeted)

The paper runs 72 ranking methods from the Scorio library on a response tensor R ∈ {0,1}^{20×30×80} for each of AIME'24, AIME'25, HMMT'25, BrUMO'25, plus Combined (M=120). Its claims:

1. **Full-trial agreement (Table 1).** At N=80, rankings agree closely with the gold standard Bayes_U@80: mean τ_b = 0.93–0.95, and 19–34 methods give exactly the same ordering.
2. **N=1 regime (Table 2, Tables 18/19).**
   - Best gold-standard agreement: about 0.86 on Combined (Bayes class).
   - Bayes_R0 is best on AIME'24, AIME'25 and BrUMO'25.
   - Rasch-MML-LCB is the most self-consistent on AIME'24/'25/HMMT.
   - Nanson (average ties) is the most self-consistent on Combined.
3. **Greedy prior Bayes_R0@N (Table 4, Fig. 2, Fig. 7).**
   - At N=1 it reduces the std of τ_b by 16–52%.
   - Mean τ rises on AIME'24/'25/BrUMO and falls on HMMT/Combined, tracking greedy–sampling alignment.
4. **Bootstrapped model pools (Table 3).** Same winners; across-subset std shrinks with pool size.

Regenerating the traces was out of scope: 192k generations at 7,445 GPU-hours. I used the authors' released tensors instead (HF dataset `harimo/scorio-trace`, linked from the repo's notebooks).

## Regenerated greedy decodes (sensitivity)

I regenerated the greedy decodes with vLLM 0.30.0 (bf16, temperature 0, 32,768 max tokens) on the same prompts.
- For non-gpt-oss models the prompt token counts match the released `num_prompt_tokens` exactly (0/120 mismatches).
- Per-benchmark greedy accuracies differ from the paper by roughly ±2–3 questions (e.g. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B AIME'24 0.267 vs 0.200; Qwen3-4B-Thinking HMMT 0.567 vs 0.467).
- gpt-oss-20b can't be matched:
  - The released prompts are 27 tokens longer than any template variant I could build (HF chat template, `openai_harmony` with or without date or developer instructions).
  - Under greedy decoding the medium/high efforts fall into repetition loops: high hit the 32k cap on 88/120 prompts, with AIME'24 accuracy 0.233 vs the paper's 0.700.
- So greedy decoding is not reproducible from the release. That is why the primary analysis uses the count-equivalent R0 built from the paper's tables. All 20 configs were completed; the full results are in "Status of the greedy regeneration" at the end.

## Why it is not a full reproduction

1. The released labelled traces are not exactly the data behind the paper. HMMT'25 and BrUMO'25 per-model accuracies differ from Tables 6–9 by up to 0.06, with a different grader. This shifts the HMMT/Combined counts in Table 1 and makes the near-ties at N=1 come out differently (AIME'25, Combined).
2. The greedy decodes (R0) are not released. I recovered the exact Bayes_R0 rankings from the paper's reported counts, but couldn't verify the per-question data.
3. No experiment scripts are released, so the N=1 protocol, gold standard, statistics and bootstrap were written by me from the paper's description.
4. Table 5 / Section 3.5 (categorical ranking): the 25 schemes of Table 21 are not in the repo. `scorio.categorical` implements five different schemas. Not run.
5. The current HEAD of the library can't run the IRT methods on these benchmarks.

## Discrepancies

1. **code_vs_paper (data), major, affects results.** HF `harimo/scorio-trace` `is_correct` vs the paper's Tables 6–9. HMMT'25 mean accuracy is 0.305 in the release vs 0.333 in the paper (e.g. Qwen3-30B-Thinking 0.492 vs 0.554). BrUMO'25 is 0.597 vs 0.588 (gpt-oss-medium 0.661 vs 0.610). The released grader is an inconsistent string match.
2. **code_vs_paper, major, affects results (as released).** `scorio/rank/irt.py` at HEAD (strict validation since commit `d217bba`) raises errors for Rasch/2PL/3PL/MML/dynamic IRT whenever a question is all-correct or all-wrong. The paper reports results for these methods on exactly such data. `bradley_terry`, `plackett_luce`, `bradley_terry_luce` and `rank_centrality_tie_ignore` also fail on some N=1 draws. Tag v0.2.2 works.
3. **code_vs_paper, minor.** Library defaults differ from the hyperparameters in Appendix J.2. The paper's values were used:

   | Method | Library default | Paper (J.2) |
   |---|---|---|
   | `elo` K | 32 | 0.05 |
   | `trueskill` tau | 0.0833 | 0.00333 |
   | `pagerank` tol | 1e-6 | 1e-12 |
   | `plackett_luce` max_iter | 10000 | 500 |

   At HEAD, `dynamic_irt(variant="growth")` additionally requires `assume_time_axis=True`, which J.2 doesn't list.
4. **code_vs_paper, major (Table 5 unreproducible).** `scorio/categorical` implements five schemas: Confident & Correct, Token Surprise, Format Compliance, Difficulty-Adjusted, IO Ratio. These are not the 25 schemes / 8 representative schemes of Section 3.5 / Table 21 (Conservative, Efficiency-adj., Verifier-only, …).
5. **code_vs_paper (data), minor.** Appendix I.1 says OpenReasoning-Nemotron-1.5B used the prompt "Solve the following math problem. Make sure to put the answer (and only the answer) inside \boxed{}". The released data's `trigger` field shows the standard "Please reason step by step…" trigger for it. The released `num_prompt_tokens` is only reproduced, to within one token, with the paper's Nemotron instruction, so the data's `trigger` field is the inaccurate one.
6. **paper_internal, minor.** Appendix A.1 describes R0 as "a shared prior matrix R0 ∈ {0,1}^{M×D}". The greedy decode is per model (Tables 6–9 report per-model greedy accuracy, and Bayes_R0 changes rankings), so R0 must be L×M×D.
