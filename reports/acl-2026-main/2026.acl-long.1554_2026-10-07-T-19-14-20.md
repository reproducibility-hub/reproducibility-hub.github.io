**Summary.**
- **Llama-3.2-1B, the paper's main model: reproduced at the paper's scale, mostly within chart-reading error.**
  - **Identification (Figs. 1–4).** The romanization and word-shuffling Jaccard overlaps of LAPE raw neurons and SAE-LAPE features reproduce. Figure 1's Hindi counts are exact: native raw neurons n=206, native SAE features (≤3 languages) n=134.
  - **Causal interventions (Tables 1/2, 5/6).** These reproduce to within about 1–2% of the paper's PPL ratios. Examples:
    - romanization, Hindi only-native: 0.313 vs 0.312
    - romanization, English only-native: 1.507 vs 1.498
    - shuffling, Hindi overlap: 2.80 vs 2.79
- **Appendix validations also reproduce.**
  - Gemma-2-2B raw neurons: Figs. 8 and 23, Tables 7 and 8. The target-set PPL ratios match to 3 decimals in most rows.
  - Llama-3-8B: Tables 5 and 6 (8B rows) match to about 3 decimals, e.g. Hindi only-native 7.75 vs 7.74 and Hindi shuffling-overlap 4.34 vs 4.35. Word-order robustness at 8B: original-vs-shuffled Jaccard 0.39–0.66.
  - All of the paper's qualitative claims that we could test hold:
    - romanized units are near-disjoint from native-script and English units;
    - shuffling leaves units largely intact;
    - shuffling-overlap neurons are causally necessary while only-unshuffled ones are not;
    - script-specific (only-native) sets cause extreme effects or language switching;
    - typological information becomes more linearly accessible with depth.
- **Important caveat (environment, not paper).** The gated `meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B` had to be replaced by the `unsloth/Llama-3.2-1B` re-upload. Its `tokenizer_config.json` sets `padding_side="left"`. The released causal and probing code pads every input to 128 tokens and counts pad positions in the loss and the mean vectors, so with the mirror's tokenizer the 1B causal numbers come out wrong (romanization overlap en 1.45, hi 0.78). Restoring the Meta default (right padding) reproduces the paper's numbers.
- **As released, the code does not run.**
  - Every language-specific-features script crashes on a circular import.
  - The shuffling pipelines pass a wrong `--hidden-dim`.
  - The shipped FLORES+ pickles cannot be read with the pinned `datasets`.
  - The glue from identification outputs to neuron lists, and the Figs. 1–4 Jaccard statistics, are missing (we wrote both).
  - The neuron-subset probing of Figs. 5/6 has no script at all.
- **Not covered:** Gemma-2-9B, the SAE tracks of Gemma and Llama-3-8B, Table 9 (translation BERTScore), and Figs. 5/6. Hence coverage = SUBSET.

## Settings that differ from the paper

**Model weights**
- `meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B` and `meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B` are gated for our token. We used the ungated re-uploads `unsloth/Llama-3.2-1B` and `NousResearch/Meta-Llama-3-8B`; checksums against the gated originals were not verified. Gemma-2-2B came from the shared HF cache.
- The unsloth 1B repo modifies the tokenizer and config: `padding_side="left"`, `pad_token=<|finetune_right_pad_id|>`, and `pad_token_id` in `config.json` / `generation_config.json`.
  - For all padded computations (causal interventions, probing) we used `models/Llama-3.2-1B-metatok/`: the same weights (symlinked), with these fields removed so the tokenizer behaves like Meta's original (right padding, no pad token).
  - The identification pipelines trace one unpadded sentence at a time, so they are unaffected.
  - Results with the unmodified mirror are kept in `codebase/causal_results_neuron_lists_100ex/` and `results_causal_*.csv` (without the `_metatok` suffix) for reference.

**Data**
- FLORES+ (`openlanguagedata/flores_plus`) is gated. The repo ships FLORES+ dev for 14 languages; these were used.
- For mr, bg, th and vi (not shipped), we took the FLORES-200 dev split from the ungated `mteb/FloresBitextMining`. For hi, en, zh, ru and ja this source is 997/997 sentence-identical to the shipped FLORES+ dev.
- Both were exposed through a local `openlanguagedata/flores_plus` dataset directory (`scripts/build_local_flores.py`).
- XNLI and PAWS-X were downloaded from the HF hub as the scripts do.
- Romanized data were generated with the released `data/romanize_flores_plus.py` (PyICU 2.16) in both variants. The release's `icu_dakshina` loader uses ICU-FLORES for all languages, so the supplementary Dakshina corpus is not needed.

**Causal random controls**
- The release hard-codes the per-layer random-control sizes in `summarize_results.py`. We derived them as round(|set|/#layers) from our own lists.
- For Llama-3.2-1B this gives exactly the release's sizes in 11 of 12 cases. For the 12th (shuffling en only-unshuffled) we used the release's 3/layer.
- For Gemma and 8B there is no released mapping, so the derived sizes were used. Random controls are single draws (seed 0), as in the release.
- The paper's own Gemma controls vary wildly (0.15 to 3.15), so control rows are sanity checks rather than claims.

**SAE track**
- The SAE scripts use `--topk-threshold-ratio 0.5`, while the paper says 0.8 (see Discrepancies). We kept the scripts' value, which reproduces Fig. 1 exactly.

**Engineering (no effect on results)**
- `OMP_NUM_THREADS=2` for parallel runs; without it, CPU oversubscription slowed everything about 10×.

### Probing (Fig. 7 / 27): trend only

We ran the released raw-model probe (`run_llama_probe.sh` arguments, 16 layers, Meta-default tokenizer). Note that it probes 2048-d residual-stream states, not MLP neurons.

- The per-unit max R², taken as the max over each family's feature sets, rises with depth.
  - Mean over units, genealogy: 0.03 (layer 1) → 0.53 (layer 16).
  - Mean over units, phonology: 0.01 → 0.31.
- Measured as the max over units, genealogy is accessible early (0.75 at layer 3), while phonology stays at 0.16–0.36 through layer 13 and reaches 0.47–0.74 in the last three layers.
- This is the hierarchy described for Figs. 7/27 ("genealogy accessible early, phonology emerges in the deepest layers"). The paper's aggregation and the SAE version (Fig. 7) are not released, so this is a trend-level check and is not counted among the numeric comparisons.
- Figs. 5/6 (probing restricted to romanization/shuffling neuron subsets) **could not be run**. No script exists, and the released raw probe works on hidden states, which MLP-neuron indices cannot address.

## Discrepancies

1. **code vs paper (minor, affects results).** The SAE-LAPE top-k threshold ratio is 0.8 in the paper (App. C.2, Table 3, "fixed across all experiments"). Every released SAE pipeline script passes `--topk-threshold-ratio 0.5`. Our 0.5 runs reproduce Fig. 1 exactly, so the paper's figures were evidently produced with 0.5.
2. **code vs paper (major, affects results).** The paper describes the cross-language mean ablation as *replacing* activations by the other language's mean (§7, App. G.2). The code *shifts* them: `h + α(μ_other − μ_curr)`. There is no CLI option for replacement. The paper's numbers come from the shift.
3. **code vs paper (major, affects results).** Perplexity is computed on sequences padded to 128 tokens, with EOS as the pad token and `labels=input_ids` (pads not masked). The PPL therefore includes predicting padding, which is why clean PPLs are in the thousands. Mean vectors are also averaged over pad positions, and generations continue after the padding. This makes results depend on the tokenizer's padding side: a left-padding tokenizer changes Table 1's overlap rows qualitatively.
4. **code vs paper (major, affects results).** The paper says it probes "raw MLP activations" and neuron subsets (§6, App. F). `run_probing_cv.py --raw-model` probes residual-stream hidden states (2048-d), so the MLP-neuron subsets of Figs. 5/6 cannot be probed with the released code.
5. **code vs paper (minor).** The paper says matched random controls have "an equal number of neurons uniformly from the overall pool". The code samples a fixed number *per layer*, hard-coded per language/category, so the total does not exactly match the target set's size or layer distribution.
6. **code vs paper (minor, crash).** The shuffling LAPE pipelines pass `--hidden-dim` 2048 (Llama) / 2304 (Gemma), which is the residual width, not the MLP width (8192 / 9216).
7. **paper internal.** App. G.5.2 says Gemma overlap neurons "again show smaller and more symmetric effects". But Table 8 has Hindi overlap 2.228 vs Hindi only-native 0.684.
8. **paper internal.** The abstract and Takeaway 5 say generation is most sensitive to units *invariant* to surface perturbations. For romanization, Table 1 and App. G.6 instead show the script-specific (only-native) sets as the more causally important ones ("Romanization-Overlap neurons … are not causally important"), so the claim holds only for the shuffling-derived sets.
9. **paper internal (unclear).** App. H says romanized–native overlap is "∼0.25 for Llama-3-8B vs. ∼0.11 for Llama-3.2-1B", without saying how it is averaged. The mean of the paper's own Fig. 2 raw-neuron bars for 1B is ≈0.08, which our 1B run reproduces (0.086). With the same pipeline we get 0.148 for 8B. The ratio direction agrees; the stated absolute values cannot be matched to any figure.
