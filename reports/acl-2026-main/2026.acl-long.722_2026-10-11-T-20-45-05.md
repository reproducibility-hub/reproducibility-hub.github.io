## Discrepancies
- **Code vs. paper (minor): QAdoc instruction.** The code's prompt (`eva/utils/config.py`) reads "retrieve **the** most relevant document". Paper Table 6 reads "retrieve most relevant document". This has no effect: the code's version reproduces the paper's numbers exactly.
- **Code vs. paper (minor): epochs and GPU counts in the training scripts.** The paper (Appendix I) says 1 epoch per fine-tuning iteration on a single H200, 1 epoch of pre-training, and 2 epochs of bidirectional adaptation. The scripts instead use:
  - `ft_encoder.sh`: 40 epochs with `save_steps 10`, on 2 GPUs;
  - `ft_decoder.sh`: 12 epochs, on 2 GPUs;
  - `pt_encoder.sh` and `pt_stage2_decoder.sh`: 2 epochs each;
  - `pt_stage1_decoder.sh`: 10 epochs.

  They look like templates with checkpoint selection on DevLite. Not tested here.
- **Code vs. paper (minor): learning rate.** `ft_decoder.sh` uses 5.9e-5; Table 8 gives 6e-5 for 596M.
- **Code vs. paper (minor): LoRA defaults.** `DMRetriever/config.py` defaults to r=64, α=16; the paper uses r=16, α=32. The shipped scripts pass 16/32, so this does not matter in practice.
- **Code vs. released data (minor; affects re-training):** `dataset.py` formats `query` with `str.format`. Because the released MTT stores the query as a list, the input becomes `"<prompt>: ['<query>']"` unless the data is flattened first.
- **Paper internal:** Section 5.1 says 7.6B beats the previous best "by 4.3 points", but Table 2 gives 71.37 − 67.19 = 4.18. The text also says 33M retains "82.2%" of 7.6B's score; 58.61 / 71.37 = 82.1%.
