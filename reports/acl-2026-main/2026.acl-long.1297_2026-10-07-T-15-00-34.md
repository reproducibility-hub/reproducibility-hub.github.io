## 1. What the paper claims and what was targeted
UMR is an unbiased (probability-reweighting) multi-bit watermark for diffusion LMs. It has three parts: a stability-aware bidirectional constraint, bit-controlled reweighting of the vocabulary partition (δ̃=4, γ=0.5, 4-bit message), and "Regret-based Remasking" (R-remasking), which re-generates unwatermarked tokens without extra diffusion steps. The central quantitative evidence is **Table 1**: bit accuracy (Acc) and OPT-1.3B perplexity (PPL) for 4 DLMs (LLaDA-8B-Instruct, LLaDA-1.5, Dream-v0-Instruct-7B, RND1-Base-0910) × 3 datasets (C4, ELI5, Alpaca), at text lengths ≈50/100/200/300 and with 2/6/8-bit messages, 100 filtered samples per cell. I also ran the ablations that only need the released method: **Table 3** (δ̃ = 2, 3, 4, 5) and **Table 4** (random vs. low-confidence standard remasking).

Not run:
- Table 2 (CNN/DM summarization, WMT19 translation): no scripts released, and the GSJ/DMark baselines are not released.
- Fig. 4 / Table 7 (zero-bit comparison with GSJ/DMark): baselines not released, so these are `NOT_COMPARABLE`. The paper's comparative claims ("better quality than GSJ/DMark") could not be checked.
- Fig. 5–7 (overhead, attacks, Pure-vs-R-remasking): these numbers are only given in figures, and Fig. 7's "Pure" variant has no switch in the code.

### Settings that differ from, or are not specified by, the paper
- **Length buckets (not specified anywhere).** The paper says sequences are generated with "max length 256 / 256 steps / block 64" and then "filtered by token length" with minimums of 40/80/180/270. A 256-token run cannot produce the 300 bucket, and with block 64 LLaDA almost never produces ≥40 tokens at gen 64 or ≥80 at gen 128 (probe: 4/330 and 1/80 pass). I therefore used **gen_length = steps = 64 / 128 / 256 / 320 with 4 blocks (block = 16 / 32 / 64 / 80)** and min tokens 40 / 80 / 180 / 270. Gen 64 / block 16 is exactly the configuration hard-coded in the released `main()`. Bit-length cells use the 200 setting (Dream: the 100 setting, as the footnote says). RND1 and Dream have no blocks: gen_length = steps = 64/128/256/320 (Dream: 64/128 only).
- **Messages for 2/6/8 bits are not given** in the paper. I used `10`, `100110`, `10011010`. The 4-bit default `1001` is from the code.
- RND1 temperature 1.0 (paper; the code hard-codes 0.9). All other models use temperature 0. δ̃=4, γ=0.5, key 42 everywhere except Table 3.
- C4 cells for the 2/6/8-bit, L300 and Table 3/4 settings were first run with 50 samples, then extended to 100. The extension skips the C4 stream documents already consumed (`--skip_docs`) and merges: 0 duplicate prompts, and generation is deterministic (temperature 0, unpadded 30-token prompts). Dream cells first run at 50 were re-run from scratch at 100. The 50-sample originals are kept in `results_n50/`.
- RND1 used a grouped-GEMM MoE forward that is numerically equivalent up to bf16 summation order (§5).

### Summary of the match
- **Bit accuracy:** 65/84 Table-1 cells are within tolerance, 8 more are `TREND_MATCH`, and 11 are `NO_MATCH`. Every `NO_MATCH` is LLaDA or LLaDA-1.5 on **Alpaca**: Acc is 0.78–0.88 against a reported 0.87–0.96. That contradicts "Acc > 0.92 under default parameters" and "Acc ≈ 0.95 for length > 100" for these two models on Alpaca. RND1 and Dream on Alpaca match. The reason is not clear. The prompt format is the released wrapper's chat template with `instruction + "\n\nInput: " + input`, and the subset is shuffle(seed=42).
- **Claims that hold:**
  - Accuracy rises from the 50 to the 100+ buckets for the LLaDA models and Dream (RND1 is already ≈0.96–0.97 at 50).
  - Accuracy falls with message length (2 > 6 > 8 bits) for all 12 model × dataset pairs.
  - Acc stays ≥ 0.82 at 8 bits everywhere (minimum 0.821, LLaDA-1.5 / Alpaca).
  - PPL falls monotonically with length in every model × dataset series.
- **PPL:** 35/84 cells are within 5%, and the rest are the same order of magnitude (`TREND_MATCH`). Our short-text PPL is usually somewhat higher and long-text PPL somewhat lower than reported.
- **Table 3 (δ̃):** Acc is 0.868 / 0.917 / 0.922 / 0.920 against 0.815 / 0.915 / 0.950 / 0.960, and PPL is 4.34 / 4.51 / 4.38 / 4.97 against 4.57 / 4.62 / 4.78 / 5.17. The direction holds (δ̃ = 2 is weakest; Acc > 0.9 for δ̃ ≥ 3; δ̃ = 5 raises PPL with no Acc gain). The "substantial boost from 2.0 to 4.0" is much smaller here (+0.054 vs +0.135). One likely reason: `caculate_delta` caps δ at (1−τ)/τ, so δ̃ often does not bind.
- **Table 4:** random 0.907 / 6.82 vs paper 0.930 / 6.949; low-confidence 0.922 / 4.38 vs 0.946 / 4.820. This matches the paper (Acc ≥ 0.9 under both; random remasking has higher PPL).
- **Not checkable:** the comparative claims against GSJ/DMark (Fig. 4, Table 2, Table 7), because the baselines and those pipelines are not released.

Overall: the proposed method's own numbers largely reproduce, but the Alpaca results for LLaDA-family models and the size of the δ̃ effect do not. Hence **MIXED**. With coverage SUBSET, the verdict is **PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED**.

## 4. Why it is not fully reproduced
1. Systematic Bit-Acc gap for LLaDA / LLaDA-1.5 on Alpaca (cause unknown; data and prompt handling as released).
2. Table 3's δ̃ = 2.0 accuracy is much higher than reported, so the claimed strength trade-off is weaker.
3. Experiments that need unreleased code (GSJ/DMark baselines, downstream tasks, zero-bit/attack comparisons, the Fig. 7 "Pure" ablation) were not run.
4. Table 1's length protocol and the 2/6/8-bit messages are unspecified, so my choices (§2) may differ from the authors'.

## 7. Notes on compute
- LLaDA on C4 (raw 30-token prefix, no chat template, as released) mostly emits EOS early. Only ~7–10% of generations reach 180 tokens and ~9% reach 270, so each C4 L200/L300 cell needed 400–1300 generations. The random-remasking cell needed ~2500.
- Dream on ELI5 at gen 128 likewise rarely reaches 80 tokens (~550 batches for 100 samples).
- Up to 3 jobs shared the H200 at once. No other users' processes were touched.
- The 12 bitmaps (29 GB, `codebase/bitmap/`) are regenerable with step 1. The conda env was removed at the end.
