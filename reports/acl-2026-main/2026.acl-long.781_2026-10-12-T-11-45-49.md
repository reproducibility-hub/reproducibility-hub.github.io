**Summary.** The central claims reproduce:
1. ELECTRA is the best foundation model. In 10-fold CV, ELECTRA beats both BERT and RoBERTa in all 10 folds (paired t-test p=2e-5 vs BERT, p=0.002 vs RoBERTa).
2. ELECTRA reaches about 94-95% Macro F1.
3. Moderate chunk sizes (5-10 sentences) perform best. Ours peaks at chunk 5 (94.96); the paper peaks at chunk 6 (94.68); chunk 6 is 94.79 here.

All 16 chunk-sweep numbers are within tolerance. Our 10-fold CV means are systematically 0.6-1.0 points **higher** than the paper's and outside its reported std, so the headline rows are TREND_MATCH rather than NUMBERS_MATCH. The likely cause is our rebuilt random split and sampling (the authors' splits are not released). The from-scratch KAN models and Mamba score much higher than reported (KAT 87.0 vs 79.4, KAN-GPT 84.8 vs 80.4, Mamba 92.8 vs 85.0). ## What was done
1. **Paper.** Read the paper. Main results are Table 1 (9 models, chunk size 4, 5 epochs; 10-fold CV mean±std for ELECTRA/RoBERTa/BERT) and Table 2 (ELECTRA Macro F1 for chunk sizes 1-16).
2. **Code.** Cloned the repo (`codebase/`): electra.py (simpletransformers), kat_classifier.py+katransformer.py, kangpt_classifier.py, mamba.py, LIT_tool.ipynb.
3. **Data.** ENNTT raw corpus (`https://github.com/nlp-unibuc/nlp-unibuc-website/releases/download/v1.0/ENNTT.tar.gz`, linked from the ENNTT repo the paper cites). natives.dat has exactly 116,340 lines, matching the paper's 116,340 originals. `scripts/build_data.py` does the following:
   - originals in corpus order, label 0;
   - the first 16,620 sentences in corpus order for each of NL, EL, DE, FR, ES, IT, PT (label 1), so consecutive sentences stay together, as fn.5 implies;
   - chunks of n consecutive same-label (same-source-language) sentences, joined with spaces, with the incomplete tail dropped;
   - shuffle with seed 42, then a 70/30 split;
   - KFold(10, shuffle, seed 42) for the chunk-4 CV.
   Chunk 4 gives 40,719 train / 17,451 test items.
4. **Env.** `conda create -p ./env python=3.10`; torch 2.6.0+cu124, transformers 4.55.4, simpletransformers 0.70.8, mamba_ssm 2.2.4, causal_conv1d 1.5.0.post8, kan_gpt, timm 1.0.15, rational_kat_cu (via PYTHONPATH).
5. **Runs** (one H200, up to 6 jobs sharing the GPU):
   - `python codebase/electra.py data/train_cN.tsv data/test_cN.tsv runs/NAME/out <model_type> <model_name>`, driven by `scripts/worker.sh` with `scripts/jobs*.txt`. Hyperparameters are exactly the released electra.py ones: 5 epochs, batch 8, grad-accum 2, simpletransformers defaults (lr 4e-5, max_seq_length 128, fp16), and the final epoch-5 model is evaluated.
   - Same script with bert-base-cased, roberta-base, albert-base-v2, microsoft/deberta-base (fp32, `REPRO_FP16=0`) and xlnet-base-cased.
   - `python codebase/kat_classifier.py|kangpt_classifier.py|mamba.py train test out` (5 epochs, lr 5e-5, batch 8, max_len 128).
   - Aggregation: `scripts/aggregate.py`, then `scripts/make_status.py`. Raw numbers are in `results_raw.json` and `results_summary.txt`; per-run logs are in `logs/`; predictions and metrics are in `runs/*/out/`.

### Settings that differ from or are unspecified in the paper
- Splits and sampling were rebuilt (random seed, which 16,620 sentences per language, chunk ordering). The authors' splits are not released.
- The HF checkpoints for BERT/RoBERTa/ALBERT/DeBERTa/XLNet are guesses (base-size models).
- DeBERTa ran in fp32 because fp16 overflows. KAT ran 5 epochs (released script: 2) to match the paper's "epoch five".
- Mamba needed crash fixes, and its pooling uses backbone hidden states (mean-pooled, as the code intended).
- For the CV, each fold trains on 9/10 of the chunk-4 data (52,353 items) and tests on 1/10, as standard 10-fold CV. The paper gives no further details.
