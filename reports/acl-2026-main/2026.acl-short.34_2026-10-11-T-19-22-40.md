In short, the released code and data reproduce every number in the paper exactly. However, the released CLIPScore contrast data has an alignment bug, and the paper's numbers inherit it. For about 95% of captions in the four main conditions (Original, Verbose, Composite, Image-to-Text), the per-image "expanded" CLIPScores used as the contrast set belong to **a different image's caption**.

I recomputed the CLIPScore matrix independently with CLIP ViT-B/32 on the 5,000 COCO images, mirroring the released CLIPScore code. With correctly aligned ranks, the central claims hold:

- Composite descriptions are more specific than Original and Verbose ones, with or without controlling for length.
- The overall specificity hierarchy holds.
- 200-character-limited descriptions are less specific than Concise ones.

Several specific claims do not hold:

- **Concise vs. Image-to-Text (Sec. 4.4) reverses sign.** The paper reports β = −1.97, i.e. Concise is *more* specific. We get β = +1.33, z = 2.42, p = 0.016, i.e. Concise is *less* specific.
- **Verbose vs. Original is no longer significant** (t-test p = 0.079 at every contrast-set size). The paper reports p < 0.001 everywhere.
- **The within-condition length effect for Original disappears** (p = 0.25; the paper reports p < 0.05).
- **Table 2 magnitudes change.** For example, mean rank at N = 4999 is 4.63 vs. 7.91 for Image-to-Text and 10.25 vs. 12.23 for Composite.
- **The hallucination effect shrinks** from +13.9 to +6.1 ranks, but stays significant.

The human-subject results (Sec. 4.1, App. D) do not depend on CLIPScore and reproduce exactly.

## Root cause of the discrepancy (data/pipeline bug)

- In `out_final_4_k_limited_with_scores.json`, the field `<type>_expanded_clipscores` gives, for each caption, its CLIPScore against all 5,000 images. For the first 250 images (file order) these match my recompute for the caption's own text (max abs diff about 0.001).
- **From image #250 onward**, i.e. after exactly 1,000 captions (= 250 images × 4 types, which suggests a batch/chunk-concatenation error in the unreleased step that maps `result_clipscores.txt` back into the JSON), each image's block of four score vectors is the block of **another image's** captions. For example, image #250's vectors are those of image #92 (caption offset −632), and image #252's are those of image #1611 (offset +5436). In each case the match is near-perfect (max abs diff ≈ 0.001), while the image's own captions differ by 0.08–0.25.
- Consequently, for these images the expanded "self" score disagrees with the stored target `<type>_clipscore` (median |diff| 0.37–0.51; 95% of Original, Composite and Verbose captions and 73% of Image-to-Text captions are off by > 0.01). `create_pickle.py` computes `rank = 1 + #(expanded scores > target score)`. For about 95% of captions, this compares the caption's score on its own image against an *unrelated caption's* scores on all images, which is not the contrast-set specificity defined in Sec. 3.2.
- **k-Limited** (same file) and **Concise / 200-Character-Limited** (`OF4_gpt4o_updated.json`) are correctly aligned. My recomputed ranks match the released ones exactly for 93–96% of captions (corr 0.9999); the remaining differences are fp16/fp32 ties.
- All released caches (`processed_data_cache.pkl`, `contrast_set_size.pkl`, and the `composite_rank` in `hallucination_robustness_results.csv`) inherit the bug, and therefore so do Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4, Table 2 and Sec. 4.2–4.4 / Limitations.

## Discrepancies

1. **code_vs_paper (major, affects results):** released `*_expanded_clipscores` in `out_final_4_k_limited_with_scores.json` / `create_pickle.py` ranks. The paper (Sec. 3.2) ranks the target image among the 4,999 others by the *same description's* CLIPScore. In the released data, for images #250–4999 of the main four conditions, the contrast scores belong to another image's caption, so the rank compares against an unrelated caption's score distribution.
2. **code_vs_paper (major, affects results):** `dataset/compute_hallucination_ranks.py`. The paper (Sec. 4.2.1) reports the hallucinated captions' specificity ranks. The code ranks each hallucinated caption's target score against the *composite* caption's expanded scores (including the composite's own target score), not against the hallucinated caption's own scores on the contrast images, so the hallucinated description is never actually scored against the contrast set.
3. **code_vs_paper (minor):** `create_pickle.py` includes the target image's own fp16 expanded score in the comparison set, compared against the fp32 target score. The contrast set is meant to be the "remaining 4,999 images". `contrast_set_size.py` excludes self, so the shipped `contrast_set_size.pkl` (which matches `processed_data_cache.pkl`) is not what the current script produces: about 2% difference in Table 2.
4. **code_vs_paper (minor):** App. D.3 reports the cross-study correlation as r = 0.21, p < 0.001. The released analysis (and the Rmd comment) gives r = 0.21, p = 0.0056.
5. **code_vs_paper (minor):** `analysis_paper.Rmd` sets `study_type = "preference"` and then immediately overwrites it with `"informativity"`, so the as-shipped output is the App. D.3 models, not the Sec. 4.1 ones.
