  * **Table 3:** only the Psychopathy column is run. PowerSeeking and AxBench need the closed gpt-4.1-mini judge, which is unavailable (`CLOSED_JUDGE_UNAVAILABLE`).
  * **Tables 2 / 6 / 7:** run for both models on all three datasets, but the fits on the *training* split use 100 of the 500 Psychopathy/PowerSeeking training items and 16 of the 64 per AxBench concept.

What the rebuilt-data runs show (indicative only, not counted as a match):

* **The unified-dynamics claim (Tables 2 and 6) holds for preference and only partly for utility.**
  * Preference R² for Eq. 15 is 0.94–1.00 for all 84 Table-2/Table-6 entries (both models, 3 datasets, 7 baseline rows), at or above the paper's 0.70–0.99.
  * Utility R² for Eq. 17:
    * AxBench: matches the paper (0.80–1.00; 12 of 14 entries ≥ 0.95 on both splits).
    * PowerSeeking: 0.63–1.00, only 5–6 of 14 ≥ 0.95 (paper 0.93–0.99).
    * Psychopathy: 0.40–0.99, only 5 of 14 ≥ 0.95 (paper 0.94–0.99).
  * Much of the gap comes from the unsteered point m=0. The vanilla model gives the trained answer format almost no probability, so UtilOdds(0) is far below the curve for trained LoRA/LW interventions. Refitting without m=0 raises the count to 10/14 (Psychopathy) and 7–9/14 (PowerSeeking).
* **The SPLIT claim (Table 3, Psychopathy) is not supported.** With the released configs, SPLIT never beats the better of the SFT/RePS baselines in any (model, form) cell. The only exception is the near-inert vector form at m=1, where Qwen SPLIT scores 15% vs 8%/4%. SPLIT is clearly worse for LoRA. * **Vector-form interventions are essentially inert at the release default m=1** (1–15%; paper 97–99%), because they were trained with steering factors 2–20. Even at a multiplier chosen on the train split they reach only 22–91%.

### Figures 2, 4 and 5 (qualitative)
* **AxBench**, averaged over 10 concepts (`results/curves/*/axbench_avg`): reproduces the paper's description.

## What I ran (exact commands)

Environment: `conda create -p ./env python=3.10`, then a trimmed version of `requirements_2.txt` (torch 2.11.0, transformers 5.10.2, accelerate 1.13.0, hydra-core 1.3.2, pandas, pyarrow, datasets 4.8.5, scipy, sklearn, …). vLLM, sae-lens and the other optional heavy dependencies were not needed. Models: `google/gemma-2-9b-it` and `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct`, symlinked into `codebase/models/{gemma-2-9b-it,qwen2.5-7b-it}`. One H200 GPU.

1. **Training and generation through the release** (`examples/run_SPILT.py`, i.e. `vectors_generate.py` / `vectors_apply.py` / `examples/axbench_generate.py`):
   * `run_psy_grid.sh`, `run_qwen_grid.sh`: `python examples/run_SPILT.py --dataset psychopathy --method {caa,sft,reps,spilt} --model_name {gemma-2-9b-it,qwen2.5-7b-it} --intervention_method all --mode both` (generation at the default `--multipliers 1.0`).
   * `run_pwr_grid.sh`: the same with `--dataset powerseeking --mode generate`. The release defines no PowerSeeking generation split, and scoring would need the judge.
   * `run_axb_grid.sh`: `--dataset axbench --mode generate` for Gemma, plus `run_axb_spilt_lora.sh` to rerun SPLIT-LoRA after the YAML fix.
   * `run_axb_qwen2.sh`: for Qwen, `examples/axbench_generate.py device=cuda:0 model_name_or_path=./models/qwen2.5-7b-it dtype=float32 +axbench_output_dir_name=… steer_train_hparam_paths=[…/axbench/qwen2.5-7b-it/<method>/generate_<method>_<form>.yaml] steer_vector_output_dirs=[vectors/qwen2.5-7b-it/<method>]`. This is called directly because `run_SPILT.py` does not forward `--model_name` on the AxBench path. Methods: DiffMean, SFT and RePS (the Table-2 rows). SPLIT is skipped because the Qwen AxBench SPLIT LoRA/LW configs are unfinished `*_XXXXX.yaml` files.
   * `run_followup.sh`: vanilla generations via `--method caa --mode apply --multipliers 0.0`, and reruns of OOM-killed configs.
   * `run_selected_gen.sh`, `run_selected_gen_vec.sh`: generation at a multiplier chosen on the **train** split (the m>0 maximising train log-prob accuracy; `scripts/select_m.py`, with a 0.25-step grid for vector forms).
2. **Psychopathy accuracy** (`scripts/psy_acc.py`): a generation counts as correct if it *starts with* Yes/No equal to `answer_matching_behavior`. I also report log-prob accuracy, i.e. the fraction of test items with PrefOdds = Ln − Lp > 0.
3. **Log-odds curves** (`scripts/logodds_curves.py`, driven by `run_curves.sh`, `run_axb_curves*.sh`, `run_fine_vec.sh`):
   * Each trained intervention is loaded through the release's `BaseVectorApplier`, and the multiplier is swept over m ∈ [−4, 4] (step 0.2) for LoRA/LW and m ∈ [−20, 20] (step 1) for vector/DiffMean.
   * Lp and Ln are summed teacher-forced NLLs of the matched answers. The answer is encoded as the training target (`answer + <end_of_turn>/<|im_end|>` suffix); an "alt" encoding uses the stripped answer without the suffix.
   * PrefOdds (Eq. 7) and UtilOdds (Eq. 8) are averaged over queries. Splits: test = full test set; train = first 100 rows for Psychopathy/PowerSeeking (the full 500 for three Gemma Psychopathy runs) and 16 rows per AxBench concept. AxBench curves are averaged over the 10 concepts (`scripts/avg_axb.py`).
4. **Fits** (`scripts/fit_curves.py`, `run_fits.sh`): Eq. 15 and Eq. 17, with the piecewise rational-quadratic D(m) of Eq. 12. SLSQP optimises the six decay parameters with an equality constraint making D continuous at m=0, the linear coefficients are solved exactly (variable projection), and the best of 40 restarts is kept. Table-7 analogue: `scripts/generalization.py`.
5. Aggregation: `scripts/compare.py` → `results/comparison_tables.md` and `results/comparisons.json`.

## Settings that differ from the paper

* **Data (main difference):**
  * Psychopathy: rebuilt 500/100 random split of Anthropic persona `psychopathy.jsonl`.
  * PowerSeeking: 500 random rows of BiPO train (2 NaN-question rows dropped) and BiPO's 200-row test set.
  * AxBench: concepts 0–9 of EasyEdit2's `dpo_train_data.parquet`, with a random 64/8 split per concept.
  * The paper's exact samples are unknown.
* **Qwen dtype:**
  * All Qwen training uses `dtype: float32` (in `config.yaml` / `apply_config.yaml` and as a CLI override for AxBench), because bf16 training diverged to NaN for every Qwen method (finite in fp32).
  * Qwen AxBench training additionally used `TORCH_ALLOW_TF32_CUBLAS_OVERRIDE=1` for speed.
  * Qwen generations use the fp32 model. All curve sweeps use bf16 models with the interventions cast to bf16.
  * Gemma training and all its m≠0 generations are bf16, as in the release. The single vanilla (m=0) Gemma generation ran with the fp32 apply config.
* **Steering multiplier for Table 3:** the paper does not state which m was used. I report both the release default m=1 and a train-split-selected m.
* **Psychopathy scorer:** the paper says only "classification accuracy". My generation-based metric gives 0% for the vanilla models, because both refuse or answer at length and never start with Yes/No, versus the paper's 50%. Their scorer evidently differs, so vanilla and DiffMean numbers are not comparable in kind.
* **Generation:** sampled with T=1.0 and max 128 new tokens (`apply_config.yaml` defaults), single sample, so gen-acc carries about ±5 points of sampling noise.
* **Train-curve subsampling** (100 of 500, 16 of 64), to bound compute.
* **Not run:**
  * PowerSeeking/AxBench Table-3 scores (closed judge).
  * The BiPO/DPO baseline of Table 4 (no config in the release).
  * Qwen AxBench SPLIT (config missing).
  * Table 1 (formula only) and Figures 2/4/5 as plots; curve data are in `results/curves*/`.

## Discrepancies

### Code vs paper

1. **Major — the AxBench runner ignores the model choice** (`examples/run_SPILT.py`, AxBench branch). `--model_name` is not passed to `axbench_generate.py` / `axbench_apply.py`, which read Gemma from `axbench_config.yaml`. A Qwen AxBench run as released therefore silently trains Gemma with the Qwen layer-14 configs.
2. **Minor — Qwen AxBench SPLIT configs are unfinished.** The LoRA/LW configs exist only as `*_XXXXX.yaml` and are skipped, so the Table-3 Qwen AxBench SPLIT LoRA/LW rows cannot be produced from the release.
3. **Minor (affects results) — vector multiplier mismatch.** Vector interventions are trained with steering factors 2–20 but applied at the default multiplier 1.0, giving 1–15% accuracy vs the paper's 97–99%. The paper never states the evaluation multiplier.
4. **Minor (no effect) — `lora_alpha` is ignored.** The configured value (32) is never passed to `LoraIntervention`, and no alpha/r scaling is applied.
5. **Minor — config typos block runs.** `intervention_method: "weight"` (PowerSeeking Gemma SFT/SPLIT local weight) and a duplicate `lora_alpha` key (AxBench Gemma SPLIT LoRA) stop these runs as released.
6. **Minor (no numerical effect) — wrong algorithm name.** The AxBench RePS apply YAMLs declare `alg_name: sft`.

### Paper-internal

1. **Section 5.2 text vs Table 3.** The text says SPLIT "consistently achieves higher scores compared with baseline methods across all three intervention types". Table 3 has RePS above SPLIT on AxBench harmonic mean for Gemma local weight (1.6362 vs 1.6225) and Gemma LoRA (1.6525 vs 1.6412).
2. **Table 7 average.** The Gemma Weight/SFT preference "Avg" is listed as −0.81, but the mean of the listed values (0.96, 0.85, −4.25) is −1.15. All other rows' averages are consistent.

## Why it is not reproduced (summary)
* **The paper's own data is not released** (`MISSING_DATA`) — the main reason.
* **The closed LLM judge (gpt-4.1-mini) is unavailable**, so 4 of the 5 Table-3 metric columns could not be run.
* **The released code needed import, transformers-5 and config fixes** to run at all.
* **On rebuilt data:**
  * The SPLIT advantage does not appear on Psychopathy.
  * Utility-curve fits are clearly weaker than reported on Psychopathy and PowerSeeking.
  * Vector-form steering does not reach the reported accuracies with the released settings.
