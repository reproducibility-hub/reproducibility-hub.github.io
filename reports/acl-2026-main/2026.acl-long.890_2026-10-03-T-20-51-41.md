# Reproduction report — 2026.acl-long.890
**"Modeling LLM Unlearning as an Asymmetric Two-Task Learning Problem"** (SAGO; Xiao et al., ACL 2026)

## Verdict
- **Verdict: PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED**
- **Result match (after fixes): MIXED**. **As released: MIXED** (the WMDP runs used no source edits; the RWKU runs used config-only changes, plus a memory-only edit for two NPO runs).
- **Coverage: SUBSET**. WMDP Cyber and RWKU Batch 1-50 were run at full paper scale (all Table 1 rows plus Table 2 Global PCGrad on Cyber). WMDP Bio was not run because its forget corpus is gated. Single seed, as in the paper.
- **Codebase completeness: MINOR_FIXES**

**Summary.**
- **WMDP Cyber (Zephyr-7B-beta) reproduces well.** The target model, all baselines (GA, NPO, SimNPO, RMU, the three +GD baselines), PCGrad, Global PCGrad and SAGO mostly land within ~1 point on MMLU (exceptions: NPO+GD 49.3 vs. 46.8; GA 23.3 vs. 26.6, both at chance) and ~1–3 points on forget accuracy.
- **The main WMDP claim holds:** +PCGrad / +SAGO recover most of the MMLU lost by naive +GD, and SAGO's MMLU is ≥ PCGrad's for all three base objectives. The margins are +0.6, +2.8 and +0.3 (paper: +1.2, +1.0, +0.4). However, SAGO's forget accuracy is ~2 points worse than PCGrad's for GA+GD and SimNPO+GD.
- **RWKU (Llama-3-8B-Instruct) does not reproduce with the released configs.** The GA/NPO families (and the SimNPO+GD baseline) unlearn far less than Table 1 reports; e.g. GA+GD forget All is 82.6 vs. 10.7.
- **The paper's central RWKU claim does not appear with the released configs.** The paper says SAGO improves neighbor retention over PCGrad by +13.3 / +13.3 / +3.4. Here, in every family, SAGO lands at a different trade-off point: it forgets more but retains less than PCGrad.
- **Supplementary runs (outside the released configs) suggest why:**
  - PCGrad at lr 1e-5 reproduces the paper's PCGrad row almost exactly. The released PCGrad lr of 3e-6 does not, so the RWKU configs in the repo are likely not the ones behind Table 1.
  - SAGO at lr 3e-5 (released: 1e-5) lands at 3.7 / 36.6 vs. the paper's 3.9 / 32.1. At comparable forgetting it keeps much more neighbor knowledge than PCGrad (19.8), so the GA-family frontier claim holds qualitatively with tuned learning rates.
  - In the NPO family at matched lr 1e-5, PCGrad (14.3 / 55.9) is slightly *ahead* of SAGO (15.4 / 53.3).

## What was run
Hardware: 1× H200 (141 GB), ~111 GB host RAM. Env: conda env at `./env` with Python 3.11, torch 2.4.1+cu121, transformers 4.45.1, accelerate 0.34.2, deepspeed 0.15.4, lm-eval 0.4.8, flash-attn 2.6.3 (prebuilt wheel), and cuda-nvcc 12.1 (needed so deepspeed can import).

Data:
- `setup_data.py --wmdp`: the zip contains `cyber-forget`, `cyber-retain` and `bio-retain`. **The bio forget corpus is HF-gated** (`cais/wmdp-bio-forget-corpus` returns "not in the authorized list" for this account), so all WMDP Bio rows are blocked. I did not request access on the user's behalf.
- `setup_data.py --rwku_batch` for RWKU.

**WMDP Cyber.** Run exactly as `scripts/wmdp_unlearn.sh` (surgery: `configs/accelerate/multi_gpu.yaml`, per-device 2, no gradient checkpointing) and `scripts/wmdp_baselines.sh` (baselines: DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 `default_config.yaml`, per-device 4, gradient checkpointing). Both used NUM_GPUS=1, so gradient accumulation keeps the scripts' global batch of 32. The released experiment yaml was used for each row (`run_wmdp.sh <experiment>`).
- Evaluation is the scripts' lm-eval commands: `wmdp_cyber` 0-shot and `mmlu` 5-shot. Batch size was 16 instead of the scripts' 2/4 (`eval_wmdp.sh`).
- In-trainer evaluation was disabled (`do_eval=false`, `eval_on_start=false`). With 1 process the trainer would otherwise run its own lm-eval, which the authors' 4-process runs skip.

**RWKU Batch 1-50.** Released experiment yamls with the scripts' CLI args (per-device 4, global batch 32, 2 epochs = 60 steps), followed by `src/eval.py experiment=eval/rwku/default.yaml` (ROUGE-L recall; FB = level1, QA = level2; All = mean of the two).
- The scripts train with DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 on 4 GPUs (bf16 compute, fp32 master weights). On one 141 GB GPU this does not fit for an 8B model plus the two fp32 surgery-gradient buffers.
- What I did:
  1. **First attempt, pure bf16 (DDP config):** the model barely moved, because Adam updates of ~1e-5 are lost to bf16 rounding (SAGO forget All 79.6 vs. target 83.6). Discarded; kept in `results/rwku_Batch50_SAGO_purebf16_invalid`.
  2. **All reported RWKU runs:** fp32 weights with bf16 autocast (`model.model_args.torch_dtype=float32`, `attn_implementation=sdpa`), a single-process accelerate config (`single_gpu.yaml`, `distributed_type: NO`), gradient checkpointing with `use_reentrant=false`, and the unchanged `paged_adamw_32bit` (states spill to host). This is numerically equivalent to fp32-master mixed precision. Speed was 50–120 s/step for surgery runs.
  3. **NPO+GD+PCGrad / NPO+GD+SAGO only:** the NPO reference model (a deepcopy of the policy, so fp32) left no room. These two runs stored the forget/retain gradient accumulators in bf16 (env-gated edit, see below).
  4. **Diagnostic (`diag.sh`; Llama-3.2-1B, 12 steps, same RWKU configs):** compared the released ZeRO-3 path, my fp32 single-GPU path and pure-bf16 DDP. GradDiff gives identical loss trajectories under ZeRO-3 and fp32-single (e.g. step 12 forget/retain NLL 2.697/2.572 vs. 2.692/2.570). SAGO is close (2.568/2.327 vs. 2.573/2.398; ZeRO-3 appears to advance the LR schedule one step earlier). So my RWKU setup behaves like the released one.

Exact commands: `run_wmdp.sh`, `eval_wmdp.sh`, `run_rwku.sh`, `eval_rwku_target.sh`, `queue.sh`, `extra.sh`, `extra2.sh`, `diag.sh` in this directory. Logs are in `logs/`, raw results in `results/`, and the aggregate is `results/summary.json`.

## Discrepancies
1. **code_vs_paper (major), WMDP retain data.** Sec. 4.1 says Wikitext is the retain set (following Li et al. 2024). The released configs train on WMDP's `{bio,cyber}-retain-corpus.jsonl` (for cyber this is GitHub/code-style text). Wikitext appears nowhere in the code. This affects results.
2. **code_vs_paper (minor), WMDP training budget.** Appendix A gives a default of 100 training steps. The released SAGO configs use 500 (GradDiff_sago), 300 (NPO_sago_cyber), 200 (SimNPO_sago_cyber) and 150 (SimNPO_sago_bio) steps, plus their own learning rates. PCGrad and the baselines use 100.
3. **code_vs_paper (minor), WMDP precision regime.** The surgery script (`wmdp_unlearn.sh`, `multi_gpu.yaml`) trains the bf16 model with no fp32 master weights, per-device 2 and no gradient checkpointing. The baseline script trains with DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 (fp32 master weights). The paper presents +PCGrad/+SAGO as changing only the gradient combination.
4. **code_vs_paper (minor), RWKU hyperparameters.** The paper reports tuning the RWKU learning rate but not its values. Run through the released trainers (the ZeRO-3 equivalence was checked on a 1B model), the released RWKU configs give much weaker unlearning than Table 1:
   - GA (lr 7e-7): forget All 82.3 vs. 3.1
   - GA+GD (8e-7): 82.6 vs. 10.7
   - GA+GD+PCGrad (3e-6): 66.4 vs. 2.3; at lr 1e-5 it gives 1.6 / neighbor 19.8, matching the paper's 2.3 / 18.8
   - GA+GD+SAGO (1e-5): 49.3 vs. 3.9; at lr 3e-5 it gives 3.7 / 36.6, close to the paper's 3.9 / 32.1

   The released RWKU configs do not appear to be the ones behind Table 1.
5. **paper_internal.** The abstract says +PCGrad recovers 94.0% of target MMLU on WMDP Bio (SimNPO+GD); Table 1 gives 56.4/59.8 = 94.3%.

## Why it is not fully reproduced
- **WMDP Bio:** third-party gated data (HF access not granted to this account).
- **RWKU:** the released hyperparameters don't produce Table 1's forgetting levels. The supplementary lr runs indicate the paper used different (higher) learning rates for at least PCGrad.
- **Compute:** the authors' 4-GPU ZeRO-3 RWKU setup had to be emulated on 1 GPU. A diagnostic shows this emulation is equivalent, but the NPO-surgery runs used bf16 gradient accumulators.
