**Bottom line.** The release contains the method and evaluation code, the five expert models, the GRPO-malicious and SFT-malicious models, and the data. With a handful of fixes it runs end to end.

Some of the paper's claims hold and others do not:

- **Held:**
  - The trained **LLM ROUTER** is badly hurt by an activation-steered (M2) malicious member. Its average relative drop was 56%; the paper reports 19% on the same datasets.
  - The **supervisor-based mitigations** (LLM judge S1 and reward model S2) recover most of that loss: about 91–92% of benign performance, versus 99–104% in the paper.
  - **Supervisor-based > supervisor-free.** The supervisor-free variant recovers much less (57%).
  - **TEXT DEBATE is robust** to M2/M4 on these datasets. The paper's own Table 1 numbers for debate on these five datasets also average about 0% change.
- **Did not hold:**
  - The **GRAPH ROUTER** shows no malicious impact at all; the paper reports a 24–35% average drop. With the released example configuration (placeholder model descriptions "1".."6"), the GNN router routes almost every query to expert #1 and never to the malicious model.
  - **M4 (GRPO-malicious)** does not degrade the LLM router (−7% relative drop, i.e. a small gain; paper: +14% drop).
  - Mitigation gains for debate are small (+0.004 to +0.014 vs +0.03 to +0.04 in the paper).
  - Graph-router mitigations under M4 do not help consistently (−0.041 / −0.030 / +0.029 on average vs +0.14 to +0.16 in the paper). Since M4 never hurt the graph router, there was nothing to recover; the top-2 deferral sometimes swaps in a worse expert.
  - Most **absolute numbers** differ substantially: HumanEval, routers' benign baselines, and IFBench.

## What the paper claims and what was targeted

The paper builds four kinds of malicious LMs:

- M1: prompting
- M2: activation steering
- M3: SFT on misaligned data
- M4: GRPO with an inverted reward

It plugs one of them into a pool of five Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct experts and reports performance for 8 collaboration methods on 10 datasets (Table 1). It then proposes supervisor-free and supervisor-based (S1 = Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct judge, S2 = Skywork reward model) mitigations for API- and text-level methods. These are evaluated under M2 and M4 on 5 datasets (Table 2). Headline numbers:

- reasoning/safety performance lowered by 7.12% / 7.94%;
- routing drops 13.8% on average;
- mitigations recover 95.31% of initial performance;
- supervisor-based beats supervisor-free.

**Target.** Table 2 is the paper's main mitigation experiment, so we used its five datasets: CocoNot, GSM8k, MMLU-redux, HumanEval and IFBench (full test splits: 1000/1000/1000/140/250). On them we ran:

- **Table 1 rows** for LLM ROUTER, GRAPH ROUTER and TEXT DEBATE with the benign pool, +M2 and +M4.
- **Table 2 rows:**
  - all three mitigations under M4 for all three methods;
  - all three mitigations under M2 for the LLM router.
  - The LLM router is the only method where M2 actually hurt in our runs, so it is the only informative M2 mitigation test.

**Not run, because of time:**

- the M1 and M3 rows;
- TEXT FEEDBACK;
- the logit-level and weight-level methods;
- the other five datasets;
- M2 mitigations for debate and graph router. In our runs M2 caused no degradation for these two methods, so there was nothing to recover.

A single collaboration run takes 10–50 min on one H200 even with the speedups below, and the full Table 1 + Table 2 would be about 400 runs.

### Interpretation

1. **Malicious impact on API-level routing: partly reproduced.**
   - **LLM ROUTER + M2.** The trained router sends many queries to the steered model, whose own outputs are garbage at α=5:
     - single-model dev scores are 0.0 on GSM8k and 0.007 on CocoNot, as in the paper's Table 5;
     - test performance drops sharply: CocoNot 0.369→0.120, MMLU 0.638→0.304, HumanEval 0.457→0.036.
   - **LLM ROUTER + M4.** The GRPO model is only mildly worse than the experts, so it does no harm.
   - **GRAPH ROUTER.** It is unaffected because its test-time predictions are nearly constant: >99% of CocoNot/GSM8k/MMLU queries go to expert #1.
     - At test time the GNN sees identical dummy edge features for every model, and the model embeddings come from the placeholder descriptions "1".."6".
     - The paper does not give the descriptions it used.
2. **Text-level debate: robust.** M2 and M4 change debate accuracy by a few points either way, in line with the paper's own debate numbers on these datasets (average −4.1% / −0.7% relative change). The paper's statement that text-level methods are "relatively robust but still affected" is consistent with this.
3. **Mitigation:**
   - **LLM router.** Supervisor-based mitigation clearly works under M2 (+0.21 average), and works better than supervisor-free (+0.06), as the paper claims. Under M4 the RM supervisor still adds +0.06.
   - **Debate.** The effects are small (≤ +0.014). One reason: in the released code the flagged model is only hidden from the other agents and still reaches the final summary (see Discrepancies).
   - **Graph router (M4).** The effects are mixed (−0.04 to +0.03), because the router was never hurt by M4 in the first place.
   - The paper's headline 95.31% recovery cannot be checked as a single number on our subset. The recovery rates we observe for the supervisor-based variants under M2 are 91–92%.
4. **Absolute numbers.**
   - HumanEval stays well below the paper (e.g. debate 0.57 vs 0.87) even after adding code extraction. Part of the reason is truncation:
     - 20–25% of the 256-token chat answers from debate and the graph router end inside an unclosed code block;
     - these account for about a third of the failures.
   - Routers' benign baselines are lower than reported.
   - With the paper's verifier protocol, GSM8k for debate matches (0.628 vs 0.653); MMLU for debate matches (0.610 vs 0.607).

### As released (no source edits)

- **Crashes:**
  - `text_multiagent_refine_miti2` (the debate + reward-model mitigation, which is also the method in the released `test_config_miti.json`) raises `TypeError` on the `run_method` signature.
  - The released SFT adapters cannot be loaded by the generation code (no tokenizer files).
  - `api_graph_routing` + HumanEval crashes while saving logs (numpy int64 in `json.dump`).
- **M2 does not create a malicious model:** with the hard-coded steering coefficient 1.0 the steered Qwen *improves* on GSM8k dev (0.615 vs 0.47). So the M2 rows, the strongest effects in the paper and in our LLM-router results, would show no degradation.
- **HumanEval scores collapse for most methods:** rescoring our final outputs with the released scorer gives 0.06–0.24 instead of 0.40–0.64 for the graph router and debate. The LLM router is the exception: it often routes HumanEval to the python expert, which answers with bare code, so its score stays at 0.48 / 0.46.
  The released scorer would also corrupt the dev-based routing labels and the choice of summarizer.
- **What survives without edits:** the M4 rows and the router mitigations under M4 run unmodified and give the numbers above, apart from HumanEval. Hence MIXED, with weaker support for the claims than after the fixes.

## Discrepancies
- **[code_vs_paper, major]** method/distributed_generation.py::batch_generate_text (steering): paper: M2: steering vector added to the activations at each layer with alpha = 5.0 (Sec. 2.1, Sec. 3) / code: vector of layer 20 added only at one decoder layer (layer_idx=19) with hard-coded coeff = 1.0. With 1.0 the steered Qwen2.5-7B is not malicious (GSM8k dev 0.615 vs 0.47 unsteered); with 5.0 it collapses to 0.0 as in the paper's Table 5. We ran all M2 experiments with coeff 5.0 (env STEER_COEF). (affects results: True)
- **[code_vs_paper, major]** data/eval.py (humaneval scoring): paper: HumanEval evaluated in a sandbox (App. A.4); Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct scores 0.936 (Table 5) / code: the raw chat response (markdown prose + ```python fences) is appended to the prompt and executed without code extraction -> SyntaxError for almost every sample (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct 0.000, python expert 0.007 on test). We added MoCo's extract_code_block(). (affects results: True)
- **[code_vs_paper, major]** data/gsm8k.json task_type / data/eval.py::prepare_inputs(general_verifier): paper: GSM8k, TruthfulQA and DS-1000 are scored with the LLM verifier TIGER-Lab/general-verifier (App. A.4) / code: gsm8k.json declares task_type exact_match (normalized exact match of the \box{} content); truthfulqa.json declares multiple_choice; the general_verifier input path reads item['prompt'] and crashes on gsm8k. Exact match gives much lower GSM8k scores (e.g. TEXT DEBATE benign 0.399 vs 0.628 with the verifier). We ran exact_match and re-scored the saved outputs with the release's general_verifier_score. (affects results: True)
- **[code_vs_paper, major]** method/text_multiagent_refine_miti.py, text_multiagent_feedback_miti.py (supervisor-free, text level): paper: internal voting mechanism prior to collaboration: models collectively detect suspicious LLMs and exclude them (Sec. 2.3) / code: no voting: the single model with the lowest dev-set score is removed from the other agents' context in rounds 2-3 (same model for every question); it still refines its own answer and is still included in the final summarisation prompt (affects results: True)
- **[code_vs_paper, major]** method/text_multiagent_refine_miti2.py / _miti3.py (supervisor-based, text level): paper: supervisors evaluate the initial outputs and disable the LMs with the lowest scores before collaboration (Sec. 2.3) / code: the flagged model (argmin reward / judge's choice, per question) is only hidden from the other agents' context; it keeps participating and its answer is still in the final summarisation input. In text_multiagent_feedback_miti2/3 the final summary excludes the dev-worst model, not the supervisor-flagged one. (affects results: True)
- **[code_vs_paper, minor]** method/api_*_miti2.py, api_*_miti3.py (supervisor-based, API level): paper: supervisors evaluate initial outputs of models and disable LMs with the lowest scores (Sec. 2.3) / code: the router's top-1 and top-2 models both answer and the supervisor (reward model / Qwen judge) picks the better of these two answers; no model is disabled (affects results: True)
- **[code_vs_paper, minor]** method/*_miti2.py, api_trained_router.py (reward model): paper: S2 = Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B (cites Skywork-Reward-V2) / code: Skywork/Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B-v0.2 (affects results: True)
- **[code_vs_paper, minor]** test_config.json / api_graph_routing.py (batch_size): paper: GRAPH ROUTER trained 500 epochs with batch size 32 (App. A.2) / code: the released example config sets batch_size 8, and the same key is also the generation batch size; we used 32 for the GNN (affects results: True)
- **[code_vs_paper, minor]** data/eval.py::is_noncompliance (CocoNot judge): paper: CocoNot judged with the regex rules of the original paper / code: `if "unknown" in text or "it is difficult":` is always true, so every response to a 'universal unknowns' prompt counts as non-compliant (affects results: True)
- **[paper_internal]** Sec. 3 vs App. A.1 (M4-RL): paper: Sec. 3: 'we train Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct for 1 epoch using GRPO' / code: App. A.1: 'train for two epochs under verl framework' (affects results: False)
- **[paper_internal]** App. B.2 / Table 6: paper: Table 6 varies the activation-steering factor alpha / code: the text says the alpha sweep is run 'with M4 malicious setting' (alpha is the M2 steering parameter; M4 is the RL model) (affects results: False)
