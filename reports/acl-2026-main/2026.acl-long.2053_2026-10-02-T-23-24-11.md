## Summary
The paper's core analysis (RQ1, Figs. 2/7/8) reproduces with the released code on all 11 models × 4 MCQ datasets × 500 questions × 12 templates. The quantity is ‖Δh‖ between meaning-preserving prompts. It grows from ≈0 at the embedding layer to large values at the last layer. The Cauchy–Schwarz upper bound ‖∇h log π‖·‖Δh‖ increases with depth in 44/44 settings, and it is always far above the observed |Δlog π|. For Llama3.2-3B on ARC, ‖Δh‖ rises from 0.77 to 69.1 (paper: "close to 0 to approximately 70").

- **RQ2 (Fig. 3a–e, 9–11)** mostly reproduces:
  - More misalignment > fewer misalignment in 44/44 settings.
  - Misalignment > modification in 44/44 settings.
  - The size-dependent first/latter transition in Qwen1.5 and Llama3.2 reproduces: latter > first for 0.5B, 1.8B and Llama-1B; first > latter for Qwen1.5-4B; comparable for Llama-3B.
  - Exception: for GPT2-small we get latter > first, the opposite of what App. F.2 states.
- **RQ4 (Fig. 5)** reproduces qualitatively. The prompt template explains more logit variance than the question in 42/44 settings; the only exceptions are GPT2-small on ARC and MMLU. Pythia has the lowest across-dataset variance and Qwen1.5 the highest.
- **App. G steering:** the code is not released, so I implemented it myself. It reproduces the paper's Qwen1.5-4B/ARC numbers exactly (2.49 → 1.66 / 1.06 / 0.73). Steering reduces |Δlog π| monotonically with depth in 44/44 settings.
- **CIFAR-10 ResNet-101 (Fig. 1)** reproduces: F1 is 0.497 / 0.642 / 0.682 at epochs 5 / 10 / 15 (paper: 0.499 / 0.646 / 0.672), and best F1 is 0.697 (paper: 0.705). Intra-class distance falls from stage 1 to stage 3 and rises at stage 4.
- **RQ3 (Fig. 4)** does **not** reproduce. The paper claims the average upper bound is positively correlated with PromptSensiScore (PSS) within each model series. With our PSS implementation the slope is positive in only 7/16 (series, dataset) combos (6/16 with the alternative correctness definition). Our PSS ranges also differ strongly from the paper's (e.g. MMLU: paper 0.020–0.035, ours 0.055–0.288). The release has no PSS code, so the answer-extraction protocol behind Y(p) is unknown. This is our best-effort implementation of App. D (Eq. 23–24).
- **Not run:** Fig. 3f / Fig. 12 (Alpaca with typo/orthographic/paraphrase variants; the paraphrases need gpt-5.4). The release has neither code nor data for them.

### Settings that differ from the paper
- Llama3.2: the `meta-llama/*` repos are gated and our HF token has no access, so I used the identical-weight ungated mirrors `unsloth/Llama-3.2-1B` / `-3B`.
- Models were run in fp32, the transformers default and what the released code does.
- PSS (RQ3): Y(p) = 1 if the argmax over the option-letter tokens (" A".." D"/" E") of the next-token distribution equals the answer. A variant uses the unrestricted vocabulary argmax. The paper does not say how answers were extracted.
- Steering: I overwrite only the **last-position** hidden state of pA at layer l (HF `hidden_states[l]`, i.e. the output of block l), with l ∈ {L/4, L/2, 3L/4} using integer division. Pairs are the 66 unordered template pairs, with A = lower index. This reproduces the paper's numbers exactly.
- App. H was run on only 2 models × ARC (paper: all models/datasets).
- Fig. 3f / Fig. 12 (Alpaca; typo/orthographic/paraphrase) were not run.

## Why it is not fully reproduced
1. **RQ3 (Fig. 4) mismatch.** The PSS pipeline is not released, and our faithful implementation of App. D does not show the claimed positive within-series correlation. Our PSS values are also on a different scale from the paper's. The paper does not specify how Y(p) (correctness) is obtained, e.g. generation plus parsing versus option-logit argmax. That is the most likely cause, so this claim is unverifiable from the release.
2. **Fig. 3f / Fig. 12 not run.** There is no Alpaca data, no typo/orthographic generators, and the paraphrases need a closed model (gpt-5.4).
3. **App. F.2 GPT2-small ordering** ("Latter lower than First") is not observed; we get latter > first on all four datasets.
