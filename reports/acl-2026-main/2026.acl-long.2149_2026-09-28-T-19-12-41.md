## What the paper claims
The paper hashes tokens with truncated SHA-256 (the default length is 2 hex chars), then aligns a user's own edition of the text to the shared hashes with difflib. Extra strategies recover tokens that fail to align: case, retokenize, mlm (ModernBERT-base), propagate, and pipe (all combined). Headline claims:
- With the closest user edition, pipe leaves 0.21 %–1.3 % of tokens misaligned (98.7–99.79 % correct; abstract, Sec. 4.4, conclusion).
- pipe beats every single strategy, and every strategy beats naive (Fig. 6, Fig. 7).
- Errors grow as hashes get shorter (Fig. 5). Fig. 4 gives the collision counts.
- Alignment is perfect when the user's text is identical (Fig. 7).
- OCR noise at (WER, CER) = (0.2, 0.05) gives 6.66 % errors with pipe (Sec. 4.5).
- NER (App. F): 0.82 % token errors, and 3.52 % strict / 2.93 % lenient entity errors.

## Observations
- naive, retokenize, mlm and propagate match the paper's plotted values essentially exactly, to about 0.01 points, for every edition and hash length.
- The **case** strategy, and **pipe** which includes it, differ slightly and consistently from the paper:
  - At h ≥ 2 ours has lower error: pipe on MD-1988 is 1.11 vs 1.29, on MD-1851-UK 3.52 vs 3.63, and the h=64 mean is 0.50 vs 0.57.
  - At h=1 ours has higher error: case 86.15 vs 84.44, pipe 85.73 vs 83.36.
  - The released case plugin probably differs a little from the version used for the paper. Every difference is within tolerance, and every conclusion holds:
    - pipe is best in every Fig. 6 and Fig. 7 setting.
    - Close editions stay ≤ 1.3 %.
    - Errors fall as hash length grows.
    - Identical text gives 0 errors.
- The paper contradicts itself on the OCR number. Its text says 6.66 % for pipe at OCR (0.2, 0.05), but its own Fig. 7 plots 5.83 %. We get 5.83 %, matching the figure.
- Synthetic errors and OCR noise are sampled without a fixed seed (python/numpy `random` inside joblib workers), so those values vary a little between runs. We still land within ≤ 0.5 points of the plot.
