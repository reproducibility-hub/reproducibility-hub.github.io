# Reproduction report — BioHiCL (ACL 2026 short, 2026.acl-short.62)

**Verdict: PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED**. Result match: **MIXED** (as released: **MIXED**). Coverage: **SUBSET**. Codebase completeness: **MISSING_CORE**. Flag: `BASELINE_STRONGER_THAN_REPORTED`.

## Summary
- I evaluated the released HF checkpoints (`LunaLan07/BioHiCL-Base`, `LunaLan07/BioHiCL-Large`) and the bge-base/large backbones on every Table 1 benchmark. All 32 Table 1 numbers I re-ran fall within tolerance. The BEIR IR numbers come out essentially exact (e.g. BioHiCL-base IR Avg 0.5433 vs 0.543), using the authors' own BEIR evaluator. The paper itself says (App. B.4) that Table 1 comes from these HF checkpoints.
- **The improvement the paper reports is a pooling artifact.** Training (and checkpoint selection) uses the CLS embedding. The saved/released checkpoints have no sentence-transformers config, so the released `SentenceBERT` evaluator falls back to **mean pooling** for BioHiCL. The bge baselines are loaded from BAAI with their official **CLS pooling**. Controlled runs:
  - untuned bge-base-en-v1.5 with mean pooling: **IR Avg 0.543**, the same as BioHiCL-base. It also ties or beats BioHiCL-base on BIOSSES (0.878 vs 0.878), SciFact-sent (0.357 vs 0.358) and PubMedQA (0.895 vs 0.897).
  - released BioHiCL-base with CLS pooling: **IR Avg 0.530**, the same as bge-base (0.530).
  - untuned bge-large with mean pooling: 0.539, versus BioHiCL-large at 0.536. BioHiCL-large with CLS pooling: 0.503.

  So under a like-for-like protocol, MeSH fine-tuning adds essentially nothing. The paper's Table 1 numbers reproduce, but the effect it attributes to the method (bge 0.529 → 0.543, Table 3) does not hold. Hence MIXED.
- PubMedQA baselines are stronger than reported: bge-base gets 0.891 (paper 0.856) and bge-large 0.901 (paper 0.871). Both are still within the 5% relative tolerance, but the gap the paper shows shrinks to under 1 point. With our numbers, BioHiCL-base (0.897) falls *below* bge-large (0.901).
- Training cannot run as released. `mesh_parse.py` (the MeSH hierarchy, ancestor expansion and depth lookup) is missing, and so are the training/validation data files. I re-implemented `mesh_parse` and trained on substitute public data (details below). The re-trained models are NOT_COMPARABLE with the paper. Under the same mean-pooling protocol they do not beat the untuned backbone either: base IR Avg 0.525 vs untuned 0.543, large 0.538 vs untuned 0.539.

## What I ran
Environment: conda env `env/` (Python 3.10). Pip pins are from the authors' (Windows, UTF-16) environment.yml: torch 2.9.1+cu126, transformers 4.57.3, sentence-transformers 5.2.0, beir 2.2.0, peft 0.18.0. GPU: 1×H200.

1. **Released evaluator, unmodified** except for the hard-coded `model_name` line: `work/asreleased/run_scifact_base.py` gives SciFact nDCG@10 = 0.75738 for LunaLan07/BioHiCL-base.
2. **`work/eval_all.py`** (my script). The IR part is identical to the released evaluator (beir `SentenceBERT`, `DenseRetrievalExactSearch`, cos_sim, test split). Batch 128 instead of 16 for speed; this does not change results. The other tasks are implemented from Appendix A:
   - BIOSSES (mteb/biosses-sts, 100 pairs): cosine similarity vs gold, Spearman.
   - SciFact-sentence: every claim in claims_train+dev paired with every sentence of each of its evidence abstracts; label 1 if the sentence is an evidence sentence; Spearman over all pairs. Dev-only values are also in results/.
   - PubMedQA: the 1,000 pqa_labeled questions retrieve from a corpus of pqa_labeled + pqa_artificial contexts (212,269 abstracts, the joined `contexts`); Recall@1.
   - Commands: `work/run_evals.sh`, `work/run_trained_evals.sh`, `work/run_extra.sh`. `--pooling cls|mean` overrides the pooling for the controlled runs.
3. **Training (substitute data)**: `PYTHONPATH=codebase python codebase/topic_model_contrast_only.py`, run in `work/train_base`. Large: a sed copy with `model_pretrained_name = BAAI/bge-large-en-v1.5`. Paper-hyperparameter variant: bs 32, lr 1e-5, wd 0. I used the code's own hyperparameters: 1 epoch, bs 16, lr 1e-4, wd 0.01, λ=0.1, β=0.3, LoRA r=8/α=16 on query/value, validation every 10 steps.
   - Substitute training data: 13 PubMed 2026 baseline XML files, keeping MEDLINE English abstracts with MeSH and year > 2000 (254K docs, 12.2 MeSH/doc; the paper reports 12.68). The code samples 80K of these with its own seed.
   - `predictions.csv` substitute: keep the full abstract. All five standard sections are in the code's target set.
   - MeSH hierarchy: `mtrees2022.bin`.
   - TREC-CT validation: 3,000 sampled TREC 2022 CT qrels, with topics and ClinicalTrials.gov brief titles.
   - MedNLI: unavailable (PhysioNet credential), so validation is TREC-CT only, as the paper describes.
   - Best checkpoints were selected at very early steps: base at step 60, large at step 30, paper-hparam variant at step 430.

Settings that differ from the paper: substitute training data (above); only the bge-base/large baselines were re-run. The other 11 baselines are not run. Only the two bge baselines were needed to test the claims about BioHiCL's gain over its backbone; the rest are off-the-shelf models whose prompting/pooling setups the paper doesn't give. Not run: the Table 2 ablations, the rest of Table 3 (e5/BMRetriever/BiCA fine-tuning), the Table 4 grid and the Table 5 seeds.

## Discrepancies
Code vs paper:
1. **(major) Evaluation pooling differs between method and baselines.** Training uses CLS. Released and saved BioHiCL checkpoints are evaluated with mean pooling (no ST config), while bge baselines use CLS. This explains the full reported gain.
2. **(major) `label_aware_cosine_loss`** regresses `cos/0.1` (temperature 0.1) onto SimL, not `cos`.
3. **(major) `contrastive_loss_fast`** uses a weighted dot product divided by the batch maximum as label similarity, not the cosine SimL of the paper.
4. (minor) Loss is λ·L_con + (1−λ)·L_mse, vs the paper's L_mse + λ·L_con.
5. (minor) Depth weight is log(max_depth), vs the paper's log(d+1).
6. (minor) lr 1e-4 vs 1e-5, weight decay 0.01 vs 0, batch 16 vs 32.
7. (minor) Not described in the paper: training text is only the classifier-selected section sentences; labels are restricted to hierarchy levels 3–10 and to labels in ≤10% of docs.
8. (minor) Validation uses MedNLI dev + a private TREC-CT subset with a margin loss; the paper describes TREC-CT only.
9. (minor) The paper claims 8192-token inputs; the code and model are 512.
10. (minor) The paper's "TREC-COVID" is BEIR `trec-covid-v2`. Standard `trec-covid` gives 0.706 / 0.672 for BioHiCL-base / bge-base.

Paper-internal:
- App. B.4 reports three runs but a t-distribution CI with df = 4 (three runs give df = 2).
