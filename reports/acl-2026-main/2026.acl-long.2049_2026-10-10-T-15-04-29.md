## Interpretation
- **Qwen2.5-VL (7B/32B):** on the 299 pairs, 3 to 13 points below the paper. The paper's strong second-image bias reproduces (SA ~65-77 vs FA ~16-25). On the clean subset the numbers come close to the paper:
  - 32B: AA 54.3 vs 57.6, CA 27.0 vs 31.0.
  - 7B: AA 47.0 vs 50.4, CA 15.0 vs 12.9.
  - Most of the gap comes from the 72 identical-image pairs. On those, the models often answer "Variation", "B" or "Neither". Those answers count as wrong, and the images can leak the winner.
- **InternVL-2.5-38B:** clean subset AA 53.5 / CA 28.8 vs paper 57.8 / 34.6. It still has the highest CA among the open models (claim holds, narrowly). On all 299 pairs it drops to AA 43.3 / CA 22.0, with 21% unparsed answers.
- **InternVL-2.5-8B:** CA matches (21.2 vs 21.3), but the bias direction is flipped (ours favours the second image, FA 35 / SA 55; the paper has FA 58 / SA 46).
- **LLaVA-NeXT-7B / LLaVA-OneVision-7B:** in ~70% of queries the model never writes the required `More effective: First/Second` line, even after the code's 6 retries. The code then stores an empty answer, giving AA 15.6 / 13.9 vs the paper's 47.5 / 50.7. I used the paper's own vLLM/transformers versions and the HF models' stock chat templates. I found no code or config difference that explains this; it may be due to image differences (unreleased manual edits, composite images). No model outputs were released to compare against.
- **Qualitative claims (on the clean subset, for the runnable models):**
  - Hold: AA only modestly above 50%; CA near random; strong position bias; larger Qwen > smaller Qwen; InternVL-38B best CA.
  - CLS hardest: holds for Qwen, not for InternVL.
  - LLaVA being worst: holds, but far more extremely than reported.
- **Overall: MIXED.**
