1. **Data:** `bash data/download_and_preprocess.sh` as released. All splits matched the expected sizes.
2. **Generation:** the released `generation.py`, instruct mode, 5-shot, greedy, `max_new_token` 512.

3. **Correctness labels:**

### Settings that differ from the paper or the release defaults
- **vLLM version:** 0.8.5.post1 instead of 0.8. 0.8 is uninstallable because its xgrammar pin was yanked.
- **Generation batch size:** 64 instead of 16. This can change greedy outputs slightly through padding numerics: on 256 test items, 242 were identical.
- **Olmo2 MMLU generation:** vLLM instead of HF `generate`.
- **TF32 matmuls** for the fp32 hidden-state extraction in `basecal.py`.
- **Judge sampling:** the judge uses the model's default sampling, exactly as in the release, so labels are stochastic. The paper does not say which seed or temperature it used.
- **Only the needed train/dev items** were generated and judged; this does not change the results.

## Why it is not fully reproduced
- **Unreleased baselines:** P(true), Verbalization, Semantic Entropy (with sampling), DACA and Temperature Scaling are not in the release. Every claim relative to them, including the abstract's 42.90%, is NOT_COMPARABLE. Coverage is SUBSET.
- **Remaining numeric gaps:** 7 of 60 BaseCal numbers fall outside tolerance, most notably BaseCal-Proj on Qwen2.5 WebQ.
  - I found no bug explaining it. Projection training converged normally: validation cosine 0.94, early stopping at epoch 288.
  - Plausible sources:
    - stochastic judge labels;
    - generation differences from the batch size / numerics;
    - WebQ's small training set (3,022 questions).
- **Vanilla gap:** the Vanilla that the released pipeline outputs does not match Table 1 (see Discrepancies). The paper's Vanilla is reproduced only by my out-of-release diagnostic.

## Discrepancies
1. **code_vs_paper, major (affects results): the Vanilla baseline.**
   - Paper: Vanilla is the PoLLM's average token probability for its own generated response (Eq. 3).
   - Release: `basecal.py` computes `instruct_conf` on a re-built chat whose assistant turn is `"Answer: " + response`, over a user prompt without the trailing "Answer:". The PoLLM never generated that prefix.
   - Effect: the released Vanilla is markedly lower, with mean ECE 0.318 vs 0.370. My generation-context recomputation gives 0.368 and matches Table 1 Vanilla in most cells.
   - Consequence: the baseline the release reports is stronger than the paper's, so the gain over the release's own Vanilla is smaller. The gain is still positive in all settings.
2. **code_vs_paper, minor (BaseCal hidden-state context).**
   - BaseCal hidden states and probabilities are extracted with an "Answer: " prefix before the response: in the assistant turn for the PoLLM, and as `prompt + "\nAnswer: " + response` for the base LLM.
   - Eqs. 5–6 do not mention this formatting.
   - The released code reproduces the paper's BaseCal numbers, so the paper presumably used it as is.
3. **code_vs_paper, minor (MMLU split sizes).**
   - Table 5: MMLU train 98,843 / dev 10,000.
   - Release: 20,000 / 10,000, sampled from the 30,000-item `auxiliary_train` pool.
   - In both cases only the first 10,000 / 2,000 are used.
4. **code_vs_paper, minor (judge).**
   - The judge runs with the server's default sampling (Qwen generation_config temperature 0.7), so labels are not deterministic. The paper gives no judge sampling settings.
   - The README's pinned vllm 0.8 cannot be installed.

No paper-internal inconsistencies found. Checked:
- the Table 1 averages 0.1641 / 0.1859;
- the 55.65% / 49.78% reductions vs Vanilla;
- 42.90% / 35.32%, which equal the reduction of mean ECE relative to Verbalization, the best baseline on average.
