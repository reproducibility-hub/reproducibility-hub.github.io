# Reproduction report: Hierarchical Token Prepending (HTP), ACL 2026 (2026.acl-long.870)

## Verdict

- **Verdict: PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED**
- **Result match (after fixes): MIXED**. Mistral-7B HTP matches the paper closely on Table 1 (5/5 small BEIR sets within 1 NDCG point) and on most of Table 2 (LongEmbed, 512 and 8192). NarrativeQA (≈5 points low at both lengths) and TREC-COVID (−7.6) do not match. Gemma2-9B HTP is 1–5 points below the paper at 512/BEIR but matches at 8192 on 3 of 4 LongEmbed tasks. Qwen2-1.5B HTP scatters by ±1–6 points. Several of the paper's comparative claims (HTP > TP w. Mean; HTP best at 8192) do not hold in our runs.
- **Result match as released: MIXED**. Only the Mistral BEIR (Table 1) path runs unmodified; its numbers are the same as above. TP w. Mean, all Qwen runs and all of Table 2 (LongEmbed) crash. The released Gemma class is Gemma-1 and gives garbage numbers for Gemma2-9B.
- **Coverage: SUBSET**. Table 1 was run for 3 models × 6 methods on ArguAna, SciFact, FiQA2018, NFCorpus and SCIDOCS. HotpotQA was skipped (5.2M docs, ~7h per method in fp32). Not run: Table 3 (general MTEB; no scripts for classification/clustering/reranking), Table 8 (NV-Embed + HTP; no code), and the ablations (Tables 4–7, Figs. 5–8).
- **Codebase completeness: MAJOR_FIXES**

## What the paper claims (targets)

HTP is a training-free method. It inserts a `<PST>` before every sentence and a block of `<B-PST>` tokens at the start of the input, and in layers 1..K it copies each sentence's last-token state into these positions. Combined with mean pooling and an early exit, the paper claims it improves decoder-LLM embeddings, especially for long documents. The main evidence is:

- Table 1: BEIR NDCG@10 for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, Gemma2-9B and Qwen2-1.5B(-Instruct), comparing Vanilla Mean, Vanilla Last, Echo Mean, TP w. PromptEOL, TP w. Mean and HTP.
- Table 2: LongEmbed (QMSum, 2WikiMQA, SummScreenFD, NarrativeQA) at context lengths 512 and 8192.

### Settings that differ from the paper / the release

- **Numerics**: the release loads models in fp32 (no dtype given). I kept fp32 but enabled TF32 matmuls (`HTP_TF32=1`) for speed. Check: Mistral HTP SciFact gives 0.46311 with the unmodified release (pure fp32, batch 4) and 0.46311 with TF32 at batch 32.
- **Batching**: mteb_retrieval_tasks encodes in descending-length order and restores the original order afterwards. This only reduces padding; with right padding the embeddings do not depend on batch composition. Check: Qwen HTP NFCorpus gives 4.891 sorted vs 4.89 unsorted. Batch size is 32 (config default 16); Qwen Echo uses 8.
- **HotpotQA was not run** (compute). TREC-COVID: Qwen Echo not run.
- **Qwen model**: the text/Table 1 say Qwen2-1.5B-Instruct, Table 9 says Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. I ran Qwen2-1.5B-Instruct for all methods and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct for HTP and Vanilla Mean as a cross-check (below).
- **Gemma2/Qwen configs are not in the release.** config.yaml only has Mistral. I added architecture entries with the Table 9 values: Gemma TP layers 1–6 with output layer −2; Qwen TP layers 1–7 with output layer −2. For Gemma I made copies of the method templates with `<s>`/`</s>` replaced by `<bos>`/`<eos>`, because Gemma needs a BOS and `<s>` is plain text to its tokenizer. This was decided before seeing results. Gemma Vanilla/PromptEOL/Echo then match the paper almost exactly (see Table 2), which suggests the authors did the same. For Qwen the released templates are used unchanged.
- **Gemma attention**: I used `attn_implementation='eager'`, the HF-recommended setting for Gemma2 soft-capping.
- **LongEmbed + spaCy**: HTP texts are cut to 12×max_length characters before spaCy sentence splitting. NarrativeQA documents (up to 1.9M characters) otherwise crash spaCy (1M-character limit), and each takes minutes to parse. Only the first max_length tokens are kept anyway, so this does not change the input.
- Instructions: as released, the BEIR query template is the generic "Retrieve relevant document. Text:{text}", not the task-specific instructions of the paper's Table 10.

## Assessment of the paper's claims

1. **HTP numbers, Mistral-7B (Table 1, Table 2): reproduced.**
   - All 5 small BEIR sets are within 1 point: ArguAna 46.80/47.06, SciFact 46.31/46.67, FiQA 7.92/8.77, NFCorpus 16.19/15.51, SCIDOCS 5.68/6.08 (ours/paper).
   - LongEmbed QMSum, 2WikiMQA and SummFD at 512 and 8192 are within ~1.6 points.
   - Exceptions: NarrativeQA is ~5 points low at both lengths (3.14 vs 8.09, 4.18 vs 9.07), and TREC-COVID is 26.09 vs 33.65.
   - The Mistral baselines Vanilla Mean, Vanilla Last, TP w. PromptEOL, PromptEOL and Echo@512 also mostly match (TP w. PromptEOL within 0.1). This confirms the pipeline is the paper's.
2. **"HTP outperforms Vanilla Mean" holds broadly.**
   - Mistral: holds on all 6 BEIR sets run, and on most LongEmbed tasks.
   - Qwen: holds on all 6 BEIR sets run.
   - Gemma2: the paper's direction holds on most BEIR sets, but on LongEmbed our Gemma2 HTP is *below* our Gemma2 Vanilla Mean at 512 and at 8192. The paper reports HTP ≥ Vanilla at 8192.
3. **"HTP consistently outperforms TP (w. PromptEOL / w. Mean)": does not hold against TP w. Mean in our runs.**
   - As released, TP w. Mean crashes; it runs after the minimal parser fix (left-padding last-token index).
   - Mistral TP w. Mean then gives SciFact 45.36, NFCorpus 15.77, SCIDOCS 7.21 and TREC-COVID 28.20. That is far above the paper's 36.71 / 8.13 / 3.30 / 25.67, and ≥ HTP on NFCorpus (≈), SCIDOCS and TREC-COVID. HTP stays ahead on ArguAna, SciFact and FiQA.
   - Neither padding variant reproduces the paper's TP w. Mean numbers. → flag `BASELINE_STRONGER_THAN_REPORTED`.
   - Gemma2 TP w. Mean is not comparable: the released template has no BOS and Gemma collapses without one.
4. **"HTP best at long context (8192)": partially.**
   - Mistral@8192: HTP beats Vanilla/TP-Mean on 2WikiMQA (27.87 vs 24.15/22.10), but Echo is higher (29.48; the paper also has Echo > HTP there). Vanilla/TP-Mean are better on SummFD (54.13/55.41 vs 51.93). QMSum is a tie (24.35 vs 24.27). On NarrativeQA, HTP (4.18) is above Vanilla (3.40) and TP-Mean (3.02) but far below Echo (8.74); the paper has HTP 9.07 > Echo 8.77.
   - All Mistral@8192 baselines (Vanilla, Echo, PromptEOL, TP-EOL, TP-Mean) match the paper within ~2 points.
   - Gemma2@8192: HTP is not better than Vanilla Mean on any task, but its numbers match the paper on 3/4 tasks; our Vanilla is slightly stronger.
5. **TREC-COVID: the HTP gain is not reproduced.**
   - Mistral: HTP 26.09 vs 33.65. Vanilla Mean 21.32 vs 26.02 and Echo 28.14 vs 39.13 are also low, but TP-EOL (20.79/20.35) and Vanilla Last (2.60/1.96) match. HTP still > Vanilla Mean, but < TP-Mean (28.20) and Echo.
   - Gemma2: our Vanilla Mean is 22.19 vs the paper's 10.69 and TP-EOL is 41.35 vs 33.65, while HTP is 22.40 vs 25.16. So the paper's large Gemma2 HTP gain on TREC-COVID (25.16 vs 10.69) disappears (`BASELINE_STRONGER_THAN_REPORTED`).
6. **Gemma2-9B / Qwen2-1.5B HTP numbers: mostly not reproduced (TREND or NO match).**
   - Gemma2 HTP is 1–5 points low on BEIR.
   - All Gemma2 TP-based methods (TP-EOL, TP-Mean, HTP) are low at 512, while Gemma2 non-TP methods (Vanilla, PromptEOL, Echo@512) match almost exactly. This points to the authors' (unreleased) Gemma2 TP implementation differing from a direct port of their Mistral logic.
   - Qwen numbers scatter by ±1–8 points, partly because the paper is ambiguous about which Qwen model it used.
