# Reproduction report — UERLens: Understanding Event Relations in Large Language Models (2026.acl-short.38)

## Verdict
- **Verdict: PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED**
- **Result match (after fixes): NOT_COMPARABLE.** The paper's dataset (UERBench) is not released, so every experiment ran on a substitute we built ourselves.
  - Trend checks are mixed:

## What the paper claims
UERLens trains no new model. It uses the public OpenSAE top-k SAEs (262,144 features, k=128) on Llama-3.1-8B.

1. **Fig. 3 — feature identification.** On counterfactual pairs (s+, s−) from UERBench, PS/PN/FRC scores identify "relation-sensitive" SAE features.
   - Layers analysed: 0, 6, 8, 15, 24, 30.
   - Scores are lowest at the embedding layer and highest in the middle layers.
   - PN is greater than PS.
   - Relation ordering: temporal > causal > sub-event.
2. **Fig. 4 and abstract — intervention.**
   - Setting relation-sensitive features to 0 (ablation) lowers event-relation accuracy, by "over 22%".
   - Setting them to 2 (enhancement) raises it, by "up to 7%".
   - Non-target features have weaker effects (App. C).
3. **Table 2 — classifier.** A linear SVM trained on the 120 relation-sensitive features (RSFT) clearly beats 120 random features (RFB).
   - F1: causal 72.05 vs 47.65, sub-event 67.33 vs 48.70, temporal 62.72 vs 49.66.
4. **Fig. 5 — feature overlap.** Overlap between relations is high at the embedding layer, low in the middle layers, and partially returns in higher layers. Ranking: S–T > C–T > C–S.

## What the release contains
Repository: https://github.com/ncepu-eai/UERLens (linked in the paper).

The method's building blocks are there:
- PS/PN/FRC computation (`uerlens/metrics.py`, `analyzer.py`)
- SAE splicing and intervention (bundled `OpenSAE/`, `uerlens/intervener.py`)
- an SVM wrapper (`uerlens/svm.py`)
- an overlap script (`uerlens/bilingual.py`, left over from LinguaLens)
- a visualizer

What is missing:
- **UERBench itself.** Only 5 example pairs each for cause, subevent and contains are included.
- **Selected feature lists.** `data/vectors` is absent.
- **The LLM-based filtering step** that keeps 20 of the top-50 features per layer.
- **Everything needed to evaluate the intervention:** the event-relation prompts, the evaluation set, and answer scoring. The intervener example prompt is an empty string.
- **The RSFT/RFB construction, train/test protocol and P/R/F1 evaluation.** `svm.py` has placeholder feature ids `[1],[2],…`, empty training data and a hard-coded `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES="4,5,6,7"`.
- **The ICL baseline.**

### Settings that differ from the paper
- **Data.** Our UERBench substitute comes from Qwen3-32B, not GPT-5, and has about 2.5k pairs per relation instead of 6.8k–25k.
  - The sub-relation mix is rebalanced toward roughly equal shares, with Begins-on and Ends-on taken in full.
  - Our counterfactual edits are much more templated (e.g. "caused" → "was followed by"), which makes the task easier. The ICL baseline reaches about 93 F1 on causal and sub-event here, against 67 and 59 in the paper.
- **No LLM filtering of features.** We used the top-20 by FRC directly.
- **Intervention prompts are our own.** The paper's prompts are unreleased. We report expected accuracy, not a 10-sample estimate.
- **The meaning of "FRC Avg" in Fig. 3 is not defined in the paper.** We used the mean over the top-50 vectors.
- **Classifier task and split are our assumptions.** The paper does not define the classification task or the split; we used s+ vs s− with an 80/20 split.

## Why it is not fully reproduced
1. **The data is unavailable.** UERBench, the paper's own GPT-5-generated dataset, is not released, and we have no GPT-5 key to regenerate it the same way.
   - Every number therefore depends on our substitute data, whose counterfactuals are more templated and easier.
   - This most likely explains the inflated Table 2 numbers and the reversed relation ordering.
2. **The intervention evaluation is unreleased.**
   - The prompts, evaluation set and scoring are missing.
   - Whether the 20 vectors were intervened one at a time or jointly is ambiguous: App. B says each vector is evaluated separately, while Fig. 4 shows one bar per layer.
   - Under both readings, the released intervention code does not produce the claimed ablation drops on our data.
   - Enhancement with value 2 applied to every token mostly breaks early layers.
3. **The LLM feature-filtering step is missing.**
4. **Table 3 (Qwen 1.7B)** was not attempted: no Qwen SAE is specified.

- **(major)** App. B describes an LLM-based filtering step (top-50 → 20 vectors per layer). It is not in the code.

- **(minor)** The intervention "control" is described as leaving activations unchanged. In the code, the layer's residual stream is still replaced by the SAE reconstruction. On our data this lowers accuracy: causal 0.807 without the SAE vs 0.774 for the L0 control.
- **(minor)** `bilingual.py` (the overlap analysis) always compares `cause.txt` with `subevent.txt`, whatever pair is requested.

**Paper-internal**
- Fig. 3(d), "Average of each relation", is identical to Fig. 3(c), "Sub-event". It cannot be the average, since (a) and (b) are higher.
- The abstract says enhancement gives improvements "up to 7%". Fig. 4 shows much larger enhancement gains (read off the plot): causal L6 ~0.63 → ~0.85, sub-event L6 ~0.67 → ~0.93.
