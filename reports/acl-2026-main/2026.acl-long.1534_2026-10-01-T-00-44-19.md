## Verdict
- **Verdict: PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED**
- **Result match (after fixes / our pipeline): TREND_MATCH**
- **Result match as released: DOES_NOT_RUN.** The release has no regression pipeline, so the paper's ΔLL numbers can't be regenerated from it alone. Its *precomputed* per-fold log-likelihoods (`loocv_data/`) do reproduce every Fig. 2/3/4/5 value and every significance marker exactly.

The paper's central claims all hold in our independent re-run:
1. Transforming cloze probability into surprisal helps, and S(w)² with V≈200 fits best (Table 1).
2. GPT2 surprisal subsumes cloze surprisal on the eye-tracking measures (Fig. 2).
3. All three manipulations (H1 resolution, H2 semantic clusters, H3 frequency) significantly reduce GPT2's fit, aggregated over 60 folds (p<1e-4 each) (Fig. 3).
4. The correlations of GPT2-based probabilities with cloze match (Fig. 3 bottom).

Per-measure ΔLL values often fall outside the 5% tolerance, e.g. Provo FP cloze 16.3 vs 11.0 and UCL GP GPT2 11.8 vs 19.6. In each of Figs. 2 and 4, one of the 12 significance markers differs. Exp. 3 (SA surprisal) depends on a bug in the released script (details below).

### Settings that differ from the paper
- **Unigram surprisal (baseline predictor).** The paper uses a KenLM unigram model on about 6.5B words of OpenWebText, which is not released. I substituted add-one smoothed unigram counts over all of OpenWebText (`Skylion007/openwebtext`, 80 parquet shards, about 8.0B regex/PTB-like tokens; `myscripts/owt_unigram.py`). Word surprisal is the sum over its tokens.
- **Regression pipeline.** Modelblocks' exact data-processing, partitioning and LME specification are not in the release. My version follows the paper text, so the absolute baseline log-likelihoods differ from the released ones. Observation counts differ by up to 5% (Provo ET: the exact line-boundary rule is not recoverable). The fold assignment differs in detail, since the exact cycling order is unknown.
- **H1 sampling.** New random samples. The paper's 5 runs can't be re-seeded.
- **H2 clustering.** New k-means runs (scikit-learn 1.7, `n_init='auto'`). The authors' sklearn version and seeds are unknown.

## Discrepancies
1. **code_vs_paper (major)**, `scripts/similarity_adjusted_surprisal.py::compute_word_prob` (Exp. 3, Fig. 5).
   - Paper: P(w′ | w₁…t₋₁) from GPT2.
   - Code: it feeds context + w′ and reads `probs[:, -len(w'):, word_ids]`. That is the distribution at the response's *own* positions, i.e. P(w′ | context, w′). For multi-token w′ it multiplies an n×n block. (It also inserts a double space before the context.)
   - Effect: the released SA predictors are nearly uncorrelated with GPT2 surprisal (r=0.07 on Provo; 0.39 after correction). Corrected SA-GPT2 rises from about 1 to 3.2 (Provo FP: 7.9). "Both SA versions are poor relative to cloze" still holds. "Inconclusive between cloze and GPT2 alternatives" becomes SA-GPT2 > SA-Cloze on most measures.
2. **code_vs_paper (major)**, `scripts/sample_completions.py` (H1).
   - Paper: GPT2 word samples are compared with w_t.
   - Code: the sampled word excludes the following punctuation token, while the target `word` keeps attached punctuation and case (e.g. `laughed.`). Every punctuated target gets count 0, i.e. the floor probability 1/(N+V). That is 7% of Provo FP observations.
   - Effect: this weakens GPT2-H1 and so inflates the support for H1. Aggregate H1 ΔLL is 4.6 with the bug and 6.75 with matching fixed; it stays significantly below GPT2.
3. **code_vs_paper (minor)**, `sample_completions.py` (hard-coded `V = 50`) and the released `output/*.gpt2_h1_{0..4}.itemmeasures`, which all use V=50 (e.g. 1/90 for Provo).
   - Paper §3.1 (and README): V=200.
   - Effect: small (aggregate 4.50 vs 4.61).
4. **code_vs_paper (minor)**, `scripts/get_llm_surprisal.py` (H2/H3).
   - Paper §2.2: word probabilities include the following-whitespace correction.
   - Code: the manipulated H2/H3 surprisals use plain summed token surprisal, with no whitespace correction. Per the README, the base `gpt2_wt` surprisal came from a separate, unreleased script with WT decoding. So manipulated and unmanipulated GPT2 differ in decoding as well as in the manipulation.
5. **code_vs_paper (minor)**, README and missing `scripts/smoothed_clozesurp.py`.
   - Paper: k=1, V∈{50,…,2000}.
   - README: k∈{0,0.1,0.5,1,2,5}, V∈{50,100,200}, generated by a script that is not in the repo. The `process_*` scripts with arguments suffice.
6. **Naming (no effect).** Code calls the paper's H2 "h3" (`gpt2_h3_*_clusters`) and the paper's H3 "h4" (`gpt2_h4_above_*`).

No internal inconsistencies were found in the paper. Table 2 fold sums equal the stated totals, and the released LL files match every figure.
