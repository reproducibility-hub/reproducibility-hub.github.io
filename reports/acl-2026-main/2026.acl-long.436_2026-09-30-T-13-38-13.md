## What was attempted

Paper claims (abstract/conclusion): GRADIEND models trained for one German gender-case article transition
(i) learn an encoding that separates the two transition directions and often generalizes to other cells with the
same surface articles (Table 3: Pearson correlations, ≥90% for German encoder models);
(ii) when applied under a 99%-LMS constraint, shift article probabilities significantly *beyond* the trained cell
(spill-over to other cells with the same source article; Table 4);
(iii) have strongly overlapping Top-1000 decoder weights within article groups, much less in a disjoint-article
control group (Table 5).

Scope chosen (compute: 1 H200 shared by up to 11 of our processes, ~47 h wall-clock):
* **GermanBERT (`bert-base-german-cased`), full paper protocol**: all 19 GRADIEND variants × 3 seeds
  (seed 42/43/44, best seed selected on validation correlation), 5,000 steps, lr 1e-5, batch 32, eval every 1,000
  steps — exactly the repo's `shell_scripts/train_all_interesting_gradiends_bert.sh` settings (that script misses
  GD_M and GD_N, which are in Table 3/5; we trained them with the same command).

  Table 5 row GermanBERT (8 numbers).

  paper's choice; we did not re-run the 1..7 pooling sweep), then 8 GRADIENDs (der↔die group + control group),
  **1 seed** each (paper: best of 3) → partial Table 3 row and Table 5 der↔die/control.
* Not run: GBERT, ModernGBERT, EuroBERT, LLaMA (each needs 19×3 GRADIEND trainings + ~4 h of encoder analysis
  per variant; LLaMA needs 3×A100 per run in the paper), SuperGLEBer evaluation (separate fork, explicitly

## Why not fully reproduced
* **Compute/time**: one GermanBERT variant needs 3×~45–90 min training plus ~3–4 h of test-set encoder analysis (41k + 9.6k + 2×25k + 6k per-example gradient encodings), and each Table 4 sweep took 2–3.5 h on the shared GPU. All 6 base models × 19 variants × 3 seeds (LLaMA needs 3×A100 per run in the paper) is far beyond 48 h on one GPU. We therefore ran GermanBERT completely and GermanGPT-2 partially. The SuperGLEBer column needs a separate benchmark fork and was not run.
* **Table 4 numbers**: α* selection in the released code depends on unseeded random masking (see Discrepancies), so the exact α* (and the α-dependent ΔP) cannot be reproduced even with identical models.

## Discrepancies
1. **code_vs_paper (major, affects results) — Table 3 column G^MASC_{NOM,DAT}.** `gradiend/export/de_encoder_table.py` lists `(NOMINATIVE, DATIVE, FEMALE)` (ND_F) twice instead of ND_M. In the paper's Table 3 that column is identical to the G^FEM_{NOM,DAT} column for **all six models** (95.7/95.7, 97.8/97.8, 93.9/93.9, 61.3/61.3, 64.3/64.3, 62.1/62.1), so the published values are not the ND_M GRADIEND's (ours: ND_M 97.1 vs. ND_F 97.0 for GermanBERT). Also listed as a paper_internal inconsistency.
2. **code_vs_paper (major, affects results) — unseeded neutral masking in the LMS constraint.** `DeDecoderAnalysis.evaluate_on_neutral` masks one random token per D_NEUTRAL sentence with an unseeded `random.choice`, separately for the base model and for every α. The neutral accuracy fluctuates by ±0.6 points from masking alone (GermanBERT, ~0.44), while the 99% threshold is ~0.44 points. α* selection is therefore partly random (e.g. N_MF: α=0.002 "violates" while α=0.01–0.1 pass), and the D_NEUTRAL ΔP/d/significance compare different masked positions. With identical masks, α* changes in 5/7 rows, and D_NEUTRAL becomes *** (d 0.08–0.21) in all rows (paper: n.s. in 5/7). At the same α=0.5 (AD_F, NG_M), the as-released analysis gives n.s. with d≈0.00, while paired masks give *** with d≈0.11–0.13. This suggests the paper's "no significant change on D_NEUTRAL" is at least partly an artifact.
3. **code_vs_paper (minor, affects seed choice)** — seed selection uses the full validation split in code vs. "100-example subset per gender-case dataset" in App. C (we followed the paper).
4. **code_vs_paper (minor, no effect here)** — `determine_best_gf_alpha` uses a 90% LMS (perplexity) threshold for decoder-only models; the paper states 99%.
5. **code_vs_paper (minor)** — none of `shell_scripts/train_all_interesting_gradiends_*.sh` trains GD_M / GD_N (dem↔des), which are reported in Tables 3 and 5.
6. **code_vs_paper (minor)** — Table 5 column order in `max_overlap.py` (der_die, der_des, der_dem) vs. the paper's header (der↔die, der↔dem, der↔des); our values suggest a label swap.
7. **paper_internal (minor)** — Table 5 header "das ↔ das" should be das↔dem.
