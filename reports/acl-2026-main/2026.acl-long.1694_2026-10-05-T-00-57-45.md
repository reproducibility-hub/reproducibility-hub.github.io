## What was run
I followed the README for every experiment in the paper:
* **Data.** SAP benchmark `ClassicGardenPathSet.csv`, `Fillers.csv` and `RelativeClauseSet.csv` from the README's Google Drive folder (via `gdown`). Natural Stories (git submodule), UCL (Springer supplementary zip), and Smith & Levy 2013 `data.pkl` (md5 matched the README). I ran the README preprocessing commands unchanged: RT 100–3000 ms, 24 folds requested, NPZ item 14 dropped as a data error, giving 23 GP folds and 24 RC folds.
* **Main experiment (Sec. 5, Fig. 1/2, Table 2).** `train_all_valid_folds.py` with the `garden_path_template.yaml` hyperparameters, for GPT-2 small, medium and large (23 LOO folds each, 500 steps, seed 42). Then `evaluate_all_folds.py` (GP effects with filler-fit coefficients, plus ΔLLH on NS/Brown/UCL with WT-decoding) and `evaluate_baseline.py all` (pre-FT). Then `evaluate_perplexity.py` and `scripts/evaluate_blimp_gp.sh` (lm-eval 0.4.9.1 BLiMP).
* **Cross-construction transfer (Sec. 6.1, Fig. 3/6).** `garden_path_template_{mvrr,nps,npz}.yaml` × 3 sizes × 23 folds, evaluated on all three constructions.
* **SRC/ORC (Sec. 6.2, Fig. 4/5, Table 2).** `relative_clause_template.yaml` × 3 sizes × 24 folds, with the RC evaluation, baseline, perplexity and BLiMP scripts.
* **Running it.** `lane.sh` runs the README commands per model size. For medium and large, the RC and transfer stages were run per fold by `fold_lane.sh` because the shared scratch volume had only about 40–120 GB free. Each fold goes train → `evaluate_fold(_rc).py` → perplexity → BLiMP; the weights are then replaced by an empty placeholder, and the released `evaluate_all_folds(_rc).py --skip-existing` and `aggregate_blimp.py` do the aggregation. The commands and arguments are the same as the released orchestrators'.
* **Settings vs. paper.** No differences: same models, data, folds, hyperparameters (template values), seed and evaluation. The only config edits are `model:` per size (as the README instructs) and `save_checkpoints: false` (intermediate checkpoints are never used).

### How paper numbers were obtained
Figures 1–6 report results only as bar charts. I extracted the figure images with `pdfimages` and read each bar height from the pixels, calibrating against the gridlines (`analysis/digitize.py`, `dig_fig1.py`, `dig_rest.py`, `dig_fig6.py`). I checked the digitization against every number quoted in the text and it agrees to within 0.2 ms or 1 percentage point. Examples: small ROI1 captures 73/83/73% (digitized 73.7/81.7/73.0%); Fig. 3 MVRR→NPS 51.5 ms (51.3), baseline 9.6 (9.8), MVRR→NPZ 48.9 (48.7), baseline 18.1 (17.9). Table 2 values are taken from the text.

**Tolerance.** A row is NUMBERS_MATCH if it is within 5% relative, or within our SE across folds (the paper's own error bars are SE across folds and are similar in size), or within the read-off precision of the digitized figure (1 ms for Fig. 1/3/6, 0.5 ms for Fig. 4, 1e-4 for ΔLLH). ### Claims
1. **After fine-tuning, LMs capture garden-path slowdowns on held-out items (Fig. 1). Reproduced.** Pre-FT effects match the paper almost exactly. Post-FT effects match within tolerance in 23/27 cells; the other 4 are GPT-2 large/small NPZ, which come out 7–20% *larger* than in the paper. GPT-2 small is still the best-aligned size. The ROI1 ordering matches the paper: MVRR > NPZ > NPS post-FT, versus NPZ > MVRR > NPS pre-FT.
2. **Fine-tuning improves ΔLLH on all three naturalistic corpora for all sizes (Fig. 2). Reproduced numerically:** all 18 values match.
3. **General LM capability is largely preserved (Table 2). Reproduced.** Pre-FT perplexities match exactly. Post-FT small and medium match, and so does BLiMP. For GPT-2 large, our post-FT GP perplexity is much lower (54.9 ± 1.1 vs 76.72 ± 14.25). The paper's large SE implies a few outlier folds in its run; our 23 folds are all stable (49–71). This supports the claim even more strongly.
4. **Cross-construction transfer (Fig. 3/6). Reproduced:** 23/27 cells NUMBERS_MATCH and the rest TREND. Every transfer beats the pre-FT baseline.
5. **SRC/ORC is not learnable to the same degree (Fig. 4/5).** Fig. 4 is reproduced: post-FT ORC>SRC at the verb (ROI0) is small, and GPT-2 large captures 15% of the human effect vs the paper's 22%. Fig. 5 only partially reproduces. The released evaluation gives pre-FT Brown/UCL ΔLLH baselines that differ from the paper by up to 2×. The "RC fine-tuning degrades ΔLLH" direction holds in 8/9 cells. The paper's exceptions were GPT-2 large on Brown and UCL: we see the large-UCL improvement but not the large-Brown one.

## Why not REPRODUCED
* The Figure 5 RC ΔLLH values (Brown/UCL) do not match. The released code keeps frequency and length spillover in the naturalistic regression even when the config disables spillover, and a truly spillover-free run crashes. So the protocol the paper describes ("without spillover variables") cannot be run with the release, and the cause of the gap is unconfirmed.
* A few GP numbers fall outside the 5% tolerance: GPT-2 large NPZ effects, GPT-2 large post-GP perplexity, and four medium/large transfer cells.
* GPT-2 large RC training diverged in 4/24 folds (11, 16, 20, 22) vs 2/24 (20, 22) in the paper. I excluded all non-converged folds, which is the paper's own criterion. The release hard-codes {20, 22}, so its appendix table generator outputs perplexities of about 10⁹ on our run.
