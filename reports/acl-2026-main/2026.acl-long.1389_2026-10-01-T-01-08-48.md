## 1. What the paper claims
1. **Fig. 2 / §5.1.** Phrasebook (PB) agents built from CSAR-induced morphemes can talk with the online neural agents of the EL. Their median test accuracy is 100%, and they "seldom fall below 80%". Online and offline neural agents score close to 100%.
2. **§5.2 / Figs. 4, 10, 11 (environment ablation).**
   - Low/low settings: n_attr=2 gives 0%, n_val=2 gives 0%, test-prop 75% gives [30%, 45%].
   - High-online / low-PB settings: non-GRU cells give [90–100%] vs [15–35%]; cross-entropy loss gives 95% vs [5–15%]; vocab=4 gives 100% vs 5%.
3. **§5.3 / Figs. 12, 13 (phrasebook ablation).**
   - Without morpheme repetition, the PB sender's median drops to 60% (from 100%).
   - Not ablating meanings, changing morpheme order, and the search/IP senders all hurt.
   - Receiver variants have little effect.
   - Max meaning size 1→2 helps slightly; max form length >1 hurts slightly.
4. **§5.4 / Figs. 6, 14.** Morpheme bijectivity (prevalence-weighted NPMI) predicts PB-agent accuracy better than other metrics: R² = 0.85, vs. toposim 0.57, bosdis 0.29 and morpheme MI 0.54.

## 2. What I did
- **Cloned the repo into `./codebase`.** Of the two submodules:
  - `.egg-repo` (brendon-boldt/EGG@a0067a4) is available.
  - `.csar-repo` is pinned to `brendon-boldt/morph-algo@3103deb`. That repo is **not public** (the clone asks for credentials; the GitHub API returns 404). I substituted the public `brendon-boldt/csar` (commit 0d209f9) and patched it (see §5).
- **Environment.** conda env at `./env` with Python 3.13, torch 2.14.1+cu130, numpy 2.5, numba 0.68, scipy 1.18, polars, pydantic 2.13, cvxpy, pyscipopt, morfessor, and so on. `env.yml` uses micromamba and is unpinned except numpy, numba and scipy.
- **Runs.** I ran exactly the experiment set in `reproduce.sh`, with the same CLI commands, through a 10-way parallel launcher (`run_task.sh`, `tasks.txt`, `xargs -P 10`):
  - `python -m src base` ×1200
  - `python -m src agent-ablation i` for i = 0..99
  - `python -m src env-ablation <param> i` for all 14 sweeps with the run counts in `reproduce.sh` (2700 runs)
  - then the repo collectors `base-analysis`, `env-ablation-analysis` and `agent-ablation-analysis`. All three ran and wrote `codebase/export/*.parquet`.
- **Analysis.** `paper.py` is missing, so I wrote `analyze.py`. Its outputs are in `results/` (`summary_base_env_ag.json`, `env_ab_summary.csv`, `ag_ab_summary.csv`, `base_runs.csv`, `env_ab_runs.csv`).
- **R² analysis (reconstructed from §4.4).**
  - Data: env-ablation corpora, excluding n_values, n_attributes and test_prop (fn. 3).
  - x = metric; y = PB-sender and PB-receiver test exact-match accuracy, pooled.
  - Stratified sampling: 5 equal-width buckets on x, with as many points drawn per bucket as the smallest bucket holds. R² is the squared Pearson correlation, averaged over 200 draws.
  - The paper does not give the exact bucket or sample-size rule, so these choices are mine.
- **Completeness of runs.**
  - Base: all 1200 runs completed. Two runs crashed before I added the try/except and were resumed from cache.
  - Offline sender: 64/1200 runs have no result because offline-sender training diverged to NaN.
  - Env ablation: some settings converge in fewer than 50 runs, as the paper also notes: vocab=4 in 26 runs, vocab=1024 in 37, LSTM in 28, max_len=3 in 41.

### Settings that differ from the paper
- **Seeds per env-ablation setting.** The paper says 100; the release's run counts give **50 per setting value**. I used the release's counts.
- **Main-experiment max epochs.** The paper says 300; the code default is 10,000. I left the code as is: all base runs converge within about 20 epochs, so this has no practical effect.
- **CSAR.** I used the public CSAR plus my reimplementation of NPMI, p(f|m), p(m|f) and the `npmi` weight method, instead of the authors' private pinned version.

### Qualitative claims from Fig. 13 (phrasebook ablation, 100 seeds; PB-S median / mean test accuracy)

| Variant | Median | Mean |
|---|---|---|
| Base | 1.00 | 0.956 |
| Ablate-meaning off | 0.68 | 0.647 |
| Shuffle order | 0.96 | 0.874 |
| Insertion order | 1.00 | 0.914 |
| Search sender | 0.56 | 0.547 |
| IP sender | 0.84 | 0.797 |
| Max meaning size 1 | 1.00 | 0.931 (vs. 0.956 for size ≥ 2) |

| PB-R, ablate form | 1.00 | 0.976 (vs. base 0.986) |
| PB-R, idempotent | 1.00 | 0.985 |

All directions match the paper:
- repetition matters;
- order matters;
- search/IP are worse than greedy;
- receiver variants barely matter;
- max meaning size 2 is slightly better than 1.

The "max form length >1 slightly worse" effect is tiny but in the same direction (receiver 0.996 → 0.986).

**Other env-ablation settings** (in `results/env_ab_summary.csv`):
- Most settings are high/high, as the paper says.
- Sender resets (`s`, `ss`, `sss`) lower PB performance.
- max_len=3 and max_len=20 lower PB-S slightly.

### Not compared numerically
- **Fig. 8 (Appendix E), inventory statistics.** Our morpheme bijectivity distribution is similar (median 0.94, 5–95% range 0.86–0.99). Our inventories are smaller and less synonymous than the histograms suggest: inventory size median 21 (95th percentile 43), and synonymy entropy median 0.07, against a paper mass of roughly 0.1–0.6. This may reflect the substituted public CSAR version. These figures are an appendix description, not a claim.

### Conclusion
- The paper's central claims reproduce:
  - PB agents communicate successfully (median 100%).
  - The ablation findings on repetition, ordering and environment hold.
  - Morpheme bijectivity is a much better predictor of PB-agent accuracy than toposim or bosdis.
- R² for bijectivity matches (0.87 vs 0.85).
- The baselines' R² values differ by 5–18%.
  - Our morpheme-MI R² (0.64) is now slightly higher than toposim (0.53), whereas the paper has 0.54 < 0.57. The paper makes no claim about this ordering.
  - The R² values also depend on my reconstruction of the stratified sampling. Without stratification the ordering is the same: bijectivity 0.76, MI 0.58, bosdis 0.28, toposim 0.27.

## 4. Why not fully reproduced / as-released status
- **The released code does not run as is.**
  1. `git submodule update` fails, because the pinned CSAR repo `brendon-boldt/morph-algo` is not public.
  2. With the public `brendon-boldt/csar`, every pydantic model fails with `PydanticSchemaGenerationError` (no schema for `sfrozenset`).
  3. `InductionMorpheme` lacks `npmi`, `p_f_m` and `p_m_f`, which the bijectivity metric needs, and there is no `npmi` weight method.
- **I reimplemented the NPMI statistics myself**, from the paper's definition (pre-ablation p(f,m), p(f), p(m)). The bijectivity numbers therefore rest on that reimplementation.
- **`paper.py` (figures and R²) is missing**, so the stratified-sampling R² procedure is my reconstruction.
- **The offline agents in Fig. 2 are disabled in the released `ReconExperiment.run`.** I had to enable them.

   This is a reimplementation of missing core functionality: the proposed metric.
