**Code:** https://github.com/Simon-leong/TED-TTS (linked from the paper/README; built on IndexTTS-2). It ships inference code only, with a 5-sample demo manifest per language.

## What the paper claims (main experiments)

- **Table 1 (emotion control).** TED-TTS is compared with IndexTTS-2 (segments synthesised independently and concatenated) and other TTS systems, under speech and text emotion prompts, in EN and ZH. The paper claims "consistent gains on DNSM and SSIM" and baseline-level WER/CER and emotion scores.
- **Table 2 (duration control).** Five per-segment duration scaling factors are used. TED-TTS "attains the best DNSM, NISQA and OVRL scores in both languages."
- **Table 3 / Table 4 (ablations).** These cover "w/o full-text access", "w/o alignment", "w/o local steering" and "w/o global EOS". The semantic-token-count error is reported as 1.7–3.4% for TED-TTS, against 5.8–12.0% for the baseline. Local steering is said to give "the largest" gain.

## What was done

1. **Environment.**
   - Python 3.10 conda env, torch/torchaudio 2.8.0+cu128 (versions taken from `uv.lock`).
   - `requirements.txt` installed, with transformers 4.52.1 (the `pyproject` pin).
   - `einops` added, because it is missing from `requirements.txt`.
   - Evaluation tools: funasr, onnxruntime, jiwer, zhconv, s3prl, gin-config.
   - protobuf pinned back to 3.19.6. funasr had upgraded it to 7.x, which breaks loading the model via tensorboard.
2. **Data and models.**
   - IndexTTS-2 weights from HF (`IndexTeam/IndexTTS-2`).
   - ESD from the README's Google Drive link.
   - MED-TTS from HF (`Chanson-0803/MED-TTS`; 15,591 EN / 15,465 ZH samples, a single `train` split).
3. **Test set (our substitute; `scripts/build_testset.py`).**
   - The paper's "500 randomly held-out samples per language" are not identified anywhere. The released dump also includes the 5 shipped demo samples.
   - We drew a seeded random 500 per language from the samples whose segment emotions are all covered by ESD (Angry/Happy/Neutral/Sad/Surprise). The paper scores emotion over "5 discrete labels". We then used the first 200 per language.
   - Speakers were cycled as in the shipped demo JSON: EN uses ESD 0011–0020, ZH uses 0001–0010, and the speaker prompt is `<spk>/Neutral/<spk>_000001.wav`.
   - Each segment's speech emotion reference is a random ESD utterance of the same speaker and emotion.
   - Duration targets are `duration_sec × factor`, with the factor cycling through {0.75, 0.875, 1, 1.125, 1.25}, i.e. 40 utterances per factor per language. Tokens are taken at 50/s, as in `infer_batch.py`.
4. **Generation (`scripts/gen.py`).** This is our runner around the released `IndexTTS2.infer`, using the `infer_batch.py` decoding defaults (beam 3, top-p 0.8, top-k 30, T 0.8, rep-pen 10).

   | System | Paper row | How it was run |
   |---|---|---|
   | `ted_speech` | Ours, speech prompt (Table 1) | `method=hmm`, per-segment ESD emotion audio as a list (`emo_audio_prompt`) |
   | `ted_text` | Ours, text prompt (Table 1) | Mode 3 of `infer_batch.py`: per-segment `emotion_description` |
   | `idx_speech` / `idx_text` | IndexTTS2 baseline | Each segment synthesised alone with the same model and code (one segment), then the waveforms are concatenated, as in paper App. E.1 |
   | `ted_speech_eos` | w/o full-text access (Table 3) | `method="eos"` (the code's local-text variant) |
   | `ted_speech_woalign` | w/o alignment (Table 3) | `method="wo_align"` |
   | `dur_ted` | Ours, duration (Tables 2–4) | `duration_mode=both`, neutral emotion: the speaker's neutral prompt is the per-segment emotion reference |
   | `dur_global` | w/o local steering | `duration_mode=global` |
   | `dur_intra` | w/o global EOS | `duration_mode=intra` |
   | `dur_none` | Table-4 baseline without duration control | no duration targets |
   | `dur_idx` | IndexTTS2 with duration (Table 2) | Per-segment single-segment synthesis with the target duration embedding, then concatenation |
   | `dur_ted_fix` / `dur_intra_fix` | (after-fix variants) | As `dur_ted` / `dur_intra`, with our fix to local steering (below) |

5. **Evaluation (`scripts/evaluate.py` and `scripts/aggregate.py`, written by us).** The release has no evaluation code; we followed paper App. E.3.
   - **WER:** Whisper-large-v3.
   - **CER:** paraformer-zh (HF).
   - **SSIM:** UniSpeech WavLM-Large ECAPA SV checkpoint. It is the mean of two parts: similarity to the speaker prompt, and the mean pairwise similarity of the ASR-segmented segments.
   - **DNSM:** DNSMOS Pro (the NISQA-trained checkpoint; the paper does not say which), averaged over 2 s windows with a 1 s stride.
   - **OVRL:** DNSMOS P.835 ONNX.
   - **NISQA:** v2 `nisqa.tar`.
   - **Emo2v:** emotion2vec_plus_large on ASR-segmented segments. For speech prompts we use cosine similarity to the segment's emotion reference. For text prompts we use 5-way accuracy from emotion2vec+'s own classifier head. The paper used an unreleased "fine-tuned emotion2vec classifier".
   - **ASR segmentation:** Whisper word timestamps (EN) or ModelScope paraformer-zh character timestamps (ZH). Segment boundaries are placed proportionally to the reference segment lengths.
   - **Table 4 token error:** the per-segment semantic token counts that the released MSA tracker reports (`seg_lens`). We also report ASR-segmentation-based counts.

Commands: `scripts/gen.py --systems ... --n 200` (run from `codebase/`), `scripts/eval_loop.sh`, NISQA `run_predict.py --mode predict_dir`, then `scripts/aggregate.py`. Logs are in `logs/`, per-sample metrics in `evals/`, tables in `results_tables.txt` and `results_summary.json`.

### Settings that differ from the paper

- **Test samples and size.** Our own seeded 200/lang sample replaces the unreleased 500/lang split. Speaker and emotion-reference assignment is ours.
- **Evaluation metrics.** All implementations are ours. The DNSMOS-Pro checkpoint choice is ours. The text-prompt emotion classifier is emotion2vec+'s head instead of the authors' fine-tuned one.
- **Whisper decoding.** We used greedy decoding (`num_beams=1`), because word timestamps fail with beam search in transformers 4.52.
- **Table 4 for `dur_global` / `dur_intra_fix`.** Only 100 per language (20 per factor) were run for these two. `dur_global` was verified to produce bit-identical token sequences to `dur_ted` on all 200 samples checked (100 EN + 100 ZH).
- **Other baselines.** MaskGCT, F5-TTS, SparkTTS and CosyVoice2 were not run: the release has no code or configs for them. The subjective MOS tests (SMOS/NMOS/EMOS/SPMOS) were not run.

## Why it is not reproduced

1. **Missing data.** The 500/lang held-out evaluation split, the speaker and emotion-reference assignment, and the fine-tuned emotion2vec text classifier are not released. The HF MED-TTS dump has a single `train` split, and its sizes (15,591 / 15,465) differ from the paper's (15,092 / 14,965).
2. **Missing scripts.** There is no evaluation code at all. There is also no script for the speech-emotion-prompt intra-utterance setting (`infer_batch.py` mode 1 takes only one global emotion reference) and none for the baseline or ablation runs. We wrote all of these.
3. **Code bug in a core component.** The local duration-embedding steering (paper Sec. 3.3, Eq. 3) has no effect in the released code. Fixing it (our patch) did not move the duration error meaningfully: EN 16.3 → 17.2, ZH 26.4 → 25.5. The paper's 1.7–3.4% token errors are far from what the released method produces on MED-TTS targets.
4. **Results.** The main quality and duration claims (Table 2, Table 4) do not hold on our substitute set. The emotion-control quality gains (Table 1) hold weakly for speech prompts and not for Chinese text prompts.

- **Missing scripts.** We wrote the following ourselves:

  - `scripts/gen.py`: runner for all systems, including the per-segment speech-emotion setting, the baselines and the ablations.

  - In `inference_speech`, store `cond_len` on the inference model.
- **Runner-level workaround (in our script, not in the release).** The released prompt-caching path in `IndexTTS2.infer` breaks for list-valued prompts on a cache hit: `spk_cond_emb_list` is left empty and `emo_cond_embs` is undefined. Our runner resets the caches before every call.

  - protobuf has to stay at 3.19.6.

## Discrepancies

1. **code_vs_paper, major.** Local duration embedding steering (Sec. 3.3 / App. C) is described as re-querying `W_dur` for the active segment during decoding.
   - The code (`model_v2.py`, `update_duration_embedding` at `duration_emb_position=-1`) writes to the text stop-token slot of `cached_mel_emb`.
   - With `kv_cache=True` the prefix is never re-encoded, so the update has no effect.
   - `duration_mode=both` and `global` produce identical tokens (verified on 200 samples).
   - It affects the reported results: the "w/o local steering" ablation in Tables 3/4.
2. **code_vs_paper, major.** App. C describes "conservative and emergency regimes" at 1.2× and 1.5× of the segment budget. They are not implemented in `IntraSegmentDurationController`.
3. **code_vs_paper, minor.** The EOS-steering neutral band differs across sources:
   - Paper App. C: [0.8, 1.1], bias in [−5, +15].
   - Code: `neutral_ratio=(0.7, 1.0)`, `max_ratio=1.2`, bias in [−5, +15].
   - README: (0.8, 1.2), max_ratio 2.0, +10.
4. **code_vs_paper, major (ablation).** The paper defines "w/o full-text access" as restricting attention to the segment's local text. The code variant (`method="eos"`) additionally replaces MSA switching with EOS-triggered switching. This truncates outputs (WER 64% vs 2.4% in the paper).
5. **code_vs_paper, minor (data).** The paper reports 15,092 EN / 14,965 ZH samples ("15,000 each"). The HF release has 15,591 / 15,465 and no held-out split.
6. **paper_internal.** Table 3 "Ours" (duration block) reports SSIM 0.460. Table 2 (English Ours) reports SSIM 0.532 with the same WER (3.227), DNSM (3.988) and NISQA (4.766).
