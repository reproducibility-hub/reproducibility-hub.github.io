## Key finding: released LLaMA3/Qwen2 results were produced in fp32, not the fp16 in the script
- The released `direction.pt` files for LLaMA3 and Qwen2 are float32 tensors; those for Gemma2 and OLMo are float16. `steering.py` loads every model with `torch_dtype=torch.float16`.
- Re-running LLaMA3 and Qwen2 in **fp32** reproduces the released CSVs bit-for-bit: cosine similarities within 4e-7, and 99.98–100% of per-instance preferences identical. That gives exactly the paper's Table 2/3 numbers.
- Re-running in fp16 as the script is written changes per-instance cosines by up to about 1.4e-3 (median 2.5e-4). Pragmatic and logical similarities differ by only about 7e-4 (median), so 2–17% of the binary preferences flip.
- The steering directions agree with the released ones (cos ≥ 0.9999994) in both precisions, so the differences come from the per-sentence embeddings.
- For Gemma2 and OLMo, the fp16 rerun reproduces the released CSVs: 98.2–99.98% of preferences identical, and the statistics match.

## Precision sensitivity (robustness caveat)

| Model | Uniform ρ fp16 | Uniform ρ fp32 | Graded ρ fp16 | Graded ρ fp32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LLaMA3 | 0.33 (p<.001) | 0.02 (p .79) | 0.46 | 0.39 |
| Qwen2 | 0.25 (p .006) | -0.01 (p .92) | 0.36 | 0.27 |
| Gemma2 | 0.06 (p .48) | 0.06 (p .48) | 0.39 | 0.39 |
| OLMo | -0.02 (p .82) | -0.01 (p .89) | 0.38 | 0.39 |

- Gemma2 and OLMo are robust to precision.
- For LLaMA3 and Qwen2, the claim that "uniform steering collapses item-level variation (non-significant ρ)" holds only in fp32. In fp16, uniform steering saturates less (rate 0.76 vs 0.91 for LLaMA3), and the baseline item ordering partly survives.
- The cause is that preferences are decided by cosine-similarity differences of about 1e-3 between embeddings whose similarities are about 0.999.
- The graded-steering claim (significant ρ) holds in every setting.

## Interpretive note (not a code/paper discrepancy)
In graded steering, α is assigned from each item's GraSD grade (A = largest α, E = smallest). The finding that "the magnitude of item-level responses increases along the A–E hierarchy" (Appendix F, Section 6.4) is therefore largely built in by design rather than an emergent property of the model.
