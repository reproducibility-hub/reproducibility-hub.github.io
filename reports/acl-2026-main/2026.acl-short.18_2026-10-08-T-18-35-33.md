**In short:** the paper's qualitative central claim does reproduce with the released code. ES fine-tuning on Countdown steadily erodes
HellaSwag (prior-task) accuracy, GRPO does not, and ES updates are far larger and denser than GRPO's. The quantitative claims do not:
- Our ES runs reach only 24–28% Countdown accuracy, against the paper's 53.0%.

### Settings that differ from the paper
- Hardware: 1×H200 shared by ES (4 vLLM engines on one GPU) and GRPO, versus 4 GPUs (ES) and RTX A6000s (GRPO) in the paper.
  - With several engines on one GPU, NCCL broadcast between engines is impossible. Engine 0 applies the ES update (exactly as
    released) and the other engines copy engine 0's weights through `/dev/shm`. This is numerically the same as the released NCCL broadcast.
  - Checked: per-engine mean rewards agree (0.331–0.336 over iterations > 150).
- GRPO: dynamic micro-batching (`use_dynamic_bsz`) instead of micro-batch 4. This changes only how micro-batch losses are weighted
  inside a mini-batch, not the optimizer batch. The rollout memory fraction was also changed. GRPO ran the full 500 steps.
- ES replicate: 300 instead of 500 iterations (time).

## 4. Why the numbers differ
1. **ES under-performs when re-run (`RERUN_WEAKER_THAN_RELEASED`).**
   - The authors' released ES checkpoints score 40.2% at iter 100 and 53.8% at peak on exactly our evaluation. Our two re-runs of the released command score 18–22% at iter 100 and 24–28% at peak.
   - Our runs are faithful to the released code. Two re-runs with the same seed have update cosine similarity 0.54 at iter 50, and the update norms equal the authors' to 3 digits (noise-dominated, as theory predicts).
   - The authors' checkpoints, however, have cosine **0.000** with our same-seed runs. So they were produced with a different perturbation-seed sequence (a different seed or code version) than the released code generates with `initial_seed=33`.
   - We found no config or code difference that explains a 2× gap in learning speed. Candidates the release leaves open:
     - `countdown_run_vllm_template.py`'s default reward file `countdown_reward_micah.py` is not released, and the paper's reward weights (0.1·format + 0.9·answer) differ from the released `countdown_reward.py` (0.1·format + 1.0·answer).
     - The ES README mentions `--chat_template_file <path_to_template>`, which is not provided. The checkpoints' chat template is identical to the tokenizer's, though.
     - Run-to-run variance beyond what we measured with two runs.
2. **GRPO is also far below the paper (25.2 vs 56.4).**
   - We used the released verl config. The policy quickly converges to short (~73-token) answers: train reward ≈0.60, val answer accuracy ≈21–25%.
   - Possible causes: verl version (the release does not pin one; we used 0.6.1), the micro-batching change, and the fact that the GRPO Countdown scores the authors' evaluation code pulls from W&B are mean *reward* (incl. the format bonus), not accuracy.
   - The authors released no GRPO checkpoints, so this cannot be checked.
3. **Norm gap: the released Frobenius code under-estimates GRPO norms.** `evaluation_script.compute_frobenius_norm` computes `(base_fp16 - model_fp16) ** 2` in fp16, so per-element differences below ≈2.4e-4 underflow to 0. These are almost all of GRPO's updates.
   - As released, GRPO norms are 0.000 (step 30), 0.0028 (60), 0.0092 (90), 0.016 (150) and 0.021 (210).
   - With fp32 they are 0.66–0.88. ES norms change by <0.1% under the fix.
   - With fp32 the GRPO norm is 1.31 at step 500, against 0.082 as released.
   - At 500/500 steps the ES/GRPO ratio is **1076× with the bug** (matching the paper's "three orders of magnitude") and **67× without it**.

## 6. Discrepancies
- **code_vs_paper (major)**: `evaluation_script.py::compute_frobenius_norm`.
  - Paper: the ES update norm is "three orders of magnitude" larger than GRPO's.
  - Code: the fp16 squared difference underflows, giving GRPO norms 16–40× too small (0.082 vs 1.31 at step 500). The ratio is 1076× as released and 67× correct.
  - Affects Fig 3 / A2 and the abstract/conclusion claim.
- **code_vs_paper (minor)**: Countdown reward. Paper (A.2.3): 0.1·Format + 0.9·Answer. `countdown_reward.py` and GRPO `reward_function.py`: 0.1·format + 1.0·answer.
- **code_vs_paper (minor)**: `grpo_experiments_implementation/gsm8k/grpo_qwen_gsm8k_experiment.sh`.
  - Paper: batch 200, mini-batch 32 for all GRPO runs.
  - Script: train_batch_size=1, ppo_mini_batch_size=1, max_prompt_length=100.
- **code_vs_paper (minor)**: `evaluation_script.fetch_wandb_metrics`.
  - Paper: Table 1/Fig 1 report "accuracy".
  - Code: for GRPO it reads `val-core/countdown_json/reward/mean@1`, the mean reward including the 0.1 format bonus, while ES uses accuracy. The override that would use it is commented out, so impact is unknown.
- **paper_internal**: Sec 3.1 says "for both models, ES is within 3–4 percentage points of GRPO", but Table 1 Llama/Countdown is ES 15.2 vs GRPO 37.6 (22.4 points).
