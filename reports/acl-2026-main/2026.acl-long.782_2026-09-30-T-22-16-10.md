After we reconstructed the missing pieces and trained all featurizers ourselves (ReLU/Top-K/SpADE/SSAE × 6 correlation levels × 3 seeds × 2 models = 144 featurizers), the paper's claims came out as follows:

**Holds:**
- **Probes ≫ SAEs on MCC.** Probe MCC is 0.92–0.94 (paper 0.88–0.92); SAE MCC is 0.27–0.58 (3-seed means).
- **SAE MCC stays roughly flat as ρ grows** from 0 to 1.
- **SSAE is the best of our trained SAEs on Pythia.** SSAE 0.58 > Top-K 0.47 > ReLU ≈ SpADE 0.44 (paper: 0.645 > 0.58 > 0.55 > 0.49).
- **I and E are high, C is low.**
  - I ≈ 0.91–0.995.
  - E ≈ 0.93–0.99, but only with the Eastwood et al. normalization the paper cites. The released formula gives E ≈ −3.1…0.5; see Discrepancies.
  - C ≈ 0.09–0.56, so concepts are spread over many features.
- **Steering is entangled.** Independence and selectivity are mostly well below 1, and their maxima are well above their means (Table 1, at the trend level).
- **Disjointness holds (Table 2):** R² 0.95–1.00 for every SAE and model. This **only** holds after fixing how `max(f_i)` is computed in `run_multi_intervention.py`. The released script gives R² down to −1.06 (Pythia Top-K), because the token-summed max over-steers into saturation.

**Does not hold:**
- **D is not "high".** Ours is 0.24–0.64 (Pythia; SSAE 0.24, Top-K 0.39) and 0.50–0.78 (Gemma), vs. paper ≈0.88–0.98.
- **Gemma MCC ranking differs.** Paper: Top-K 0.68 > SSAE 0.61 > SpADE 0.58 > ReLU 0.51. Ours: SSAE 0.57 > Top-K 0.52 > ReLU 0.50 ≫ SpADE 0.27. Our Gemma Top-K and ReLU fall below the Neuron baseline (0.55).

## What we did (commands)

All runs are in `./env` (Python 3.10, torch 2.7.1, nnsight 0.3.0, transformers 4.53.0, sklearn 1.6.0, xgboost 3.1.1, cooper-optim 1.0.1; pins from `requirements_full.txt`). Logs are in `logs/` and `logs/eval/`; parsed results are in `results/`.

1. **Correlated data.** `repro_scripts/make_correlated_data.py` builds `data/labeled_sentences_corr_ds-sp_{ρ}.jsonl` for ρ ∈ {0, .1, .2, .5, .9, 1}, using the released configs (domain=science ↔ sentiment=positive). The achieved Pearson ρ equals the target exactly.
2. **Activation cache.** `repro_scripts/cache_acts.py` caches all real-token activations at `layers[L//2]` and the last-token activation at `layers[L-1]` for the 382,459 training sentences:
   - Pythia-70M-deduped: layers 3 and 5.
   - Gemma-2-2B: layers 13 and 25.
3. **SAE training.** `repro_scripts/train_sae.py` trains ReLU, Top-K (k=128) and SpADE (`sparsemax_dist`) SAEs. It uses the release's `sae_utils/saes.py` and a port of the spadeFormalGrammars training loop with the App. B.2 hyperparameters:
   - 10,000 steps, batch of 128 sentences (64 for Gemma SpADE);
   - AdamW, lr 1e-3, 200 warm-up steps, betas (0.9, 0.95);
   - 8× dictionary, middle layer.
   - Runs: 3 seeds (42/43/44) × 6 ρ × 2 models.
4. **SSAE training.** `repro_scripts/train_ssae.py` uses the released `ssae/ssae.py` (DictLinearAE, the SSAE cooper problem, ExtraAdam, decoder renorm/projection). It trains on last-token final-layer differences of random pairs within each correlated set (pairing as in `ssae/data_utils.py`): 10,000 steps, batch 128, latent size 8×d. Runs: 3 seeds (1/2/3) × 6 ρ × 2 models.
5. **Probes**, with the released scripts:
   - `probe_experiments/train_probes.py --layer_num {3|13} --data_file labeled_sentences_corr_ds-sp_{ρ}.jsonl`
   - `probe_experiments/train_multiclass_probes.py --layer_num {5|25}`
   - `probe_experiments/run_mcc.py`
6. **Fig. 2 (MCC):** `sae_experiments/test_sae.py --model_name {pythia,gemma} --sae <path> --id-metric mcc`, plus `--identity-baseline` and `--randomize-sae`. Scripts: `repro_scripts/eval_mcc_pythia.sh`, `eval_gemma.sh`, `eval_gemma_seeds.sh`.
7. **Fig. 3 (DCI-ES):** same script with `--id-metric dci` and `--id-metric exp`. Scripts: `repro_scripts/eval_dci_pythia.sh`, `eval_dci_gemma.sh`.
8. **Table 1:** `sae_experiments/build_multiclass_heatmap.py --model-type {pythia,gemma} --layer_num {5|25} --exp-name {relu,topk}_corr_{ρ} --sae-method corr --seed 42`. ### Settings that differ from the paper / scale-downs

- **Gemma weights.** `google/gemma-2-2b` is gated for the available HF token, so we used the ungated mirror **`unsloth/gemma-2-2b`** (same base weights). The hard-coded model ID was made configurable through `GEMMA_ID`.
- **DCI and E: 1 seed only** (seed 42 SAEs / seed 1 SSAEs), on a subset of the training set.
  - Full-set XGBoost needs 32–46 GB per job and was OOM-killed repeatedly under the 119 GB job limit.
  - Pythia: D, C, I use the first 100k training sentences; E uses 50k.
  - Gemma (18,432-dim features): D, C, I use 30k; E uses 20k.
  - Full-set Pythia D/C/I for ReLU and Top-K at ρ=0 and Neuron (`logs/eval/dcifull_*`) agree with the subset values to within 0.01–0.02.
  - E could **not** be computed for Gemma SSAE and Gemma Random: XGBoost at depth 15 on dense 18,432-dim features runs out of the 141 GB GPU memory even as a single job.
- **Gemma train-side feature selection uses a subset.** For MCC, the steering heatmaps and disjointness, f̂ is selected on the first 100k training sentences instead of all 382k: full-set feature matrices (382k × 18,432 floats, several copies) don't fit in host RAM. Pythia uses the full training set throughout.
- **Steering experiments use 1 SAE per setting.** The paper doesn't say how many seeds it used for Tables 1 and 2.
- **MCC uses 3 seeds** everywhere, as in the paper. Probes use 1 seed (42).
- **Not run:**
  - the "Natural" baselines (Marks et al. Pythia SAEs, GemmaScope), which need the external `dictionary_learning` repo plus SAE downloads;
  - the gradient-attribution feature selection (App. I.2);
  - the sparse-probe experiments (App. E/G).

### Settings we had to choose (unspecified in the paper and release)

- **SAE dictionary size: 8×.** Inferred from Table 4/5 Top-K sparsity (1 − 128/4096 = 96.9%; paper 97.6%) and a code comment (`f_orig: [1, 4096]`).
- **L1 / distance-regularizer weight: 0.1** (spadeFormalGrammars "default"). This gives ReLU sparsity of 54% (Pythia) and 47% (Gemma), vs. Table 4/5's 54–59% and 50%.
- **SAE training data: all real tokens.** BOS and pad are dropped.
  - A last-token-only variant reproduces the Table 4 reconstruction stats better (Top-K VE 94.9% vs paper 94.2%; ReLU 99.70% vs 99.7%).
  - But its MCC was worse for ReLU (0.29 vs 0.47) and similar for Top-K (0.48 vs 0.46), so we kept all-token training. Logs: `logs/eval/lt_mcc_*`.
- **SpADE inputs are scaled by 1/64**, to match the evaluation code (`test_sae.get_activations`).
- **SSAE latent size: 8×d.**
  - The release's CLI default `--oc 10` gives NMSE 0.92 (tested at ρ = 0, 0.1, 0.9), far from Table 4's 0.004.
  - Our 8× SSAEs reach NMSE 0.05 (Pythia) and 0.14 (Gemma), still worse than the paper's 0.004 and 0.07.
  - The SSAE sparsity schedule (warm-up 2000, schedule 1000) is ticked per step, not per epoch: 15,000 epochs over ~7×10⁵ pairs is infeasible.
- **Correlated sets are built by sub-sampling the uniform set**, keeping all (science, positive) and (other, non-positive) examples. The paper describes "upsampling" but releases neither the sets nor the code.

## Why it is not fully reproduced

1. **The featurizers had to be trained by us, with unspecified details.** No trained SAEs/SSAEs and no SAE training script are released, so we chose dictionary size, regularizer weight, training tokens, input scaling and SSAE latent size (see above). The paper's MCC/DCI/steering numbers depend strongly on these. Gemma SpADE in particular came out poor (MCC 0.27, D 0.50).
2. **Metric-code differences:**
   - the explicitness formula (as released) differs from the cited definition;
   - the disjointness steering magnitude uses the token-summed max (see Discrepancies);
   - the Random baseline is uniform noise rather than a randomly initialized SAE.
3. **Correlated datasets are not released.** Our sub-sampled construction keeps several thousand decorrelated examples at ρ=0.9, which likely explains why our probes don't degrade at ρ=0.9 while the paper's do.
4. **Compute/RAM scale-downs** for DCI and Gemma feature selection (see above).

## Discrepancies

**Code vs. paper:**

1. **Explicitness formula** — `test_sae.compute_explicitness` (major, affects results).
   - Paper (App. D.2): E is one minus the normalized area under the loss–capacity curve, per Eastwood et al. 2023; Fig. 3 shows E ≈ 0.97 for all methods.
   - Code: E = 1 − AULCC / (0.5·(c_max − c_min)·(L_max − L_min)), normalized by the loss *range* rather than a baseline loss. It gives E ≈ −3.1 … 0.5 for our featurizers.
   - With an Eastwood-style normalization (baseline = majority-class error), the same logged losses give 0.93–0.99.
2. **Steering magnitude in disjointness** — `run_multi_intervention.py` (major, affects results).
   - Paper Eq. 3: do(f_i = α · max f_i), the max of the feature's activation on D. `build_multiclass_heatmap.py` uses the per-token (last-token) max.
   - `run_multi_intervention.py` instead uses the max of the **token-summed** activation (≈ sequence length × larger). This drives the model into saturation at α ≥ 2 and lowers R² to as little as −1.06.
   - With the per-token max, R² = 0.95–1.00, matching Table 2.
3. **Random baseline** — `test_sae.py --randomize-sae` (major, affects results).
   - Paper: "a randomly initialized SAE (Random)".
   - Code: zeroes all SAE parameters (`_reset_parameters`), then replaces the features with `torch.rand` uniform noise. MCC is ≈0.02–0.04 vs the paper's ≈0.13.
4. **Neuron baseline** — `test_sae.py --identity-baseline` (major by definition, small effect).
   - Paper: the original activation vector h (identity featurizer).
   - Code: `dictionary` is overwritten by the loaded SAE, so the "identity" path uses `dictionary(x)`, i.e. the SAE *reconstruction* x̂. That is close to h only for well-reconstructing SAEs; ours reconstruct 99.99% of variance.
5. **SSAE training crashes** — `ssae/ssae.py` (major, blocks SSAE results). See the SSAE training bug above.
6. **SSAE embedding extraction** — `ssae/store_embeddings.py` (major, effect unknown).
   - Paper: frozen Pythia-70M / Gemma-2-2B.
   - Code: loads Pythia-70M-deduped at `revision="step3000"` (an early training checkpoint) and only `google/gemma-2-2b-it`, while all evaluation uses the final base models. It uses `hidden_states[layer]` (the output of layer−1) rather than `layers[layer]`.
7. **MCC feature selection set** — `test_sae.py` (minor, possibly affects results).
   - Paper App. D.1: f̂ is selected on the training dataset D, which carries the correlations.
   - Code: `--train_dataset` defaults to the uniform `labeled_sentences_train.jsonl` for every SAE, regardless of the correlation level it was trained at.
8. **SSAE hyperparameters** — `ssae/ssae.py` (minor, affects results).
   - Paper: "similar hyperparameters as when training SAEs" (batch 128, 10,000 steps).
   - Code defaults: batch 32, lr 5e-4, latent `oc=10`, 15,000 epochs, sparsity warm-up/schedule in epochs. `oc=10` cannot reach the reconstruction in Table 4.
9. **SSAE DCI/E crash** — `test_sae.compute_importance_gbt` / `compute_explicitness` on SSAE features (major, blocks results).
   - Paper: reports DCI-ES for SSAEs (Fig. 3).
   - Code: SSAE labels (`PairedDataset.labels_diff_binary`) are signed differences in {−1, 0, 1}, and xgboost rejects them, so SSAE DCI/E crash. A commented-out unsigned variant `diff = int(l1 != l2)` exists in `PairedDataset`.
   - We fixed this by label encoding.
10. **Pooling is inconsistent across experiments** — test_sae / heatmap / probes (minor).
   - Paper: not specified.
   - Code: MCC/DCI use last-token SAE features; steering feature selection uses token-summed features; probes are trained on token-summed activations; the disjointness readout uses last-token activations while its probes were trained on summed activations.

**Paper-internal:**

11. **Table 2 header.** It reads "Domain=sentiment"; the text and Fig. 5 say domain=science.
