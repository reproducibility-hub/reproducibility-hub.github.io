# Reproduction report — 2026.acl-long.2079
**One Persona, Many Cues, Different Results: How Sociodemographic Cues Impact LLM Personalization** (Weeber, Neplenbroek, Batzner, Padó; ACL 2026)

## Verdict
- **Verdict: PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED**
- **Result match (after fixes): MIXED.** The paper's headline conclusions hold:
  - Cues are highly correlated, with Spearman ρ between cues of .933–.969 (paper .91–.96).
  - Every correlation is significantly below 1.
  - Explicit cues in the user prompt produce the most persona disparities and names in the system prompt the fewest.
  - Explicit mentions drive the age, gender and race effects.
  - Human conversation histories lead to different conclusions than the other cues.
  - Many counts and effect sizes still fall outside tolerance (direction preserved), and four AITA-specific statements were **not** reproduced.
- **Result match as released: DOES_NOT_RUN.**
  - The camera-ready analysis notebook contains unresolved git merge-conflict markers, so it is invalid JSON.
  - The released HF/bitsandbytes generation and HF-pipeline judge cannot process the ~10M generations within the time budget, so I ported both to vLLM.
- **Coverage: SCALED_DOWN.**
  - 6 of 7 models: GPT-4o-mini needs an OpenAI key, which was not available.
  - MMMD uses the public sample of 298 claims instead of 500.
  - Everything else ran at the paper's full scale: 6 cues × 10 personas × 5 variants × 3 samples, all four datasets, plus the Llama-3.3-70B stance judge over all IssueBench responses.
- **Codebase completeness: MAJOR_FIXES.** Generation and judge loops were rewritten (vLLM ports); the analysis notebook needed a merge-conflict fix and a logic fix; the AITA subset had to be reconstructed. Details below.

## What I ran (exact pipeline)
All commands were run inside the conda env `env/` (Python 3.12, vllm 0.30.0, torch 2.13 cu130, pandas 2.3.3, scipy, statsmodels) on 4×H200.
1. **Data**
   - SBB: `git clone github.com/MatthewTKearney/sociolinguistic-bias-benchmark` → `data/sociolinguistic-bias-benchmark`.
   - AITA: authors' Drive folder (bit.ly/AITA_Dataset) → `AITA_Dataset_Raw.csv`. **Subset2.csv is not there**, so `scripts/make_aita_subset2.py` rebuilds a balanced asshole / not-the-asshole subset (13,792 each, deleted/removed posts dropped).
   - MMMD: `sample_data/claims.csv` from github.com/kinit-sk/medical-misinformation-dataset. The full dataset is access-restricted on Zenodo.
   - IssueBench and PRISM: HF datasets. PersonaMem-v2: `data/raw_data` (999 persona files).
   - NC voter file: Sep-2026 statewide snapshot, then `python prep_names.py`.
   - Then `scripts/prep_data_evaluation.py` and `scripts/prep_data_demographics.py` (nbconvert of the released notebooks, minimal edits listed below).
   - Resulting eval set: SBB 500 (100 per domain), AITA 500 (250/250), IB 166 neutral (of 500), MMMD 298 (56 true / 242 false).
2. **Generation**: `scripts/run_all_generation.sh` → `scripts/get_responses_vllm.py --model_name M --tp 4` for
   - Llama-3.1-8B/70B-Instruct
   - Gemma-3-12B/27B-it (unsloth mirrors)
   - Qwen2.5-14B/72B-Instruct

   Each model runs all 7 conditions (no-demographics plus 6 cues) on all four datasets, ≈1.47M responses per model. Prompts come from the released `add_demographic_bias`; sampling uses temperature 1.0, top_p 0.9, n=3, 10 new tokens (IB: 256). Wall-clock was 25 min (8B) to 2.3 h (70B/72B) per model.
3. **Post-processing and evaluation**: `scripts/run_postgen.sh`:
   - released `generation/postprocess_responses.py`
   - `scripts/judge_ib_vllm.py` (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, released template, greedy, 10 tokens; 42 files, ≈0.9M judge calls, 14 h)
   - released `evaluation/eval_responses.py -rf responses_closed -ibf ib_judged -af answers`
4. **Analysis**: `scripts/visualizations_cr.py`, the camera-ready `visualizations.ipynb` with conflict markers removed (patches listed below). It writes figures/, results/*.csv (Tukey tables 5–7 equivalents) and `results_summary.json`. Then `scripts/make_comparisons.py` → `comparisons.json` / `comparisons.md`.

### Summary of the comparison
- **Fig. 3a (cue correlations)** reproduces closely: .933–.969 vs .91–.96. All pairs are significantly < 1 (bootstrap B=1000, α=.01). Name and explicit cues correlate more with each other than with histories, and the two history cues correlate highly.
- **Fig. 3b (model correlations)**: .72–.89 vs .68–.88. The two Qwen models are the most correlated pair, as in the paper.
- **Fig. 4**: SBB-salary correlations are all >.98 (ours ≥.985). MMMD has the lowest correlations (.73–.87 vs .73–.90). In every task, explicit-user is the cue least correlated with history-human.
- **Significance counts (Sec. 5.2)**:
  - name-system 0/24 (paper 1), explicit-user 17/24 (20), history-human 6/24 (9). The ranking is the same, but the counts are lower.
  - By task: SBB-medical 11 (10), political 3 (3), IB 3 (3), MMMD 4 (6), **AITA 4 (11)**.
  - By variable: gender 13 and race 16 fit 15±3; **age 9 does not**.
- **Explicit-mention effects (Tables 5–7)**: all checked explicit-cue Tukey differences have the same sign and significance and similar magnitude. Examples:
  - 55+ vs 18-34 told to seek medical attention 17.6 pts more often (paper 13.1).
  - Salary +8.6k (paper +7.8k).
  - IB White–Black −0.123 (paper −0.104).
  - The race-direction inversion between history-human and explicit-user holds on IB and AITA.
- **Not reproduced (NO_MATCH)**:
  - AITA is not the most persona-sensitive task: 4/18 vs 11/18; in our run SBB-medical is.
  - The two history cues do not lower pooled AITA accuracy below the no-persona baseline (paper: every cue does). Our largest per-model drop is 5.3 pts (paper "up to 7").
  - AITA Male–Non-binary differences for explicit-system and history-human are not significant (paper 0.028*, 0.017*). explicit-user is significant but smaller (0.029* vs 0.071*).

  Likely causes: our AITA posts are a different sample (Subset2 had to be reconstructed), GPT-4o-mini is missing, and generation used bf16 instead of 8-bit.
- **Invalid-response rates**: SBB 0.08% (0.08), IB 3.54% (3.49), MMMD 0.48% (0.93), AITA 0.02% (0.16).

## Why not fully reproduced
1. GPT-4o-mini needs an OpenAI API key; none is configured.
2. The MMMD full dataset is gated behind a Zenodo access request; only the public sample (56 true claims) was usable.
3. AITA Subset2.csv is missing from the linked data folder and was reconstructed. NC voter and PersonaMem data are newer versions, so the sampled names and conversations differ.
4. The released inference code (HF generate with batch 8 and 8-bit quantization; HF-pipeline judge) is far too slow for the ~10.5M generations within ~48 h, so it was ported to vLLM bf16 with identical prompts and sampling parameters.

Items 1–3 change the data and model pool, which plausibly explains the count differences, especially on AITA.

## Discrepancies
**code_vs_paper**
1. *(minor, affects results)* `generate_hf.py` loads every open model in 8-bit bitsandbytes; the paper never mentions quantization.
2. *(minor, affects results)* Sampling uses top_p=0.9 in addition to temperature 1.0; the paper states only temperature 1.0.
3. *(minor, affects results)* IB responses are capped at 256 new tokens for open models but 2000 for GPT-4o-mini.
4. *(minor, affects results)* For explicit-mention-system / age, 2 of 5 templates are first-person user templates ("I identify as N years old.", "My age is N") instead of Table 2's "The user identifies as…" / "The user's age is…".
5. *(minor, affects results)* The MMMD prompt is missing a backslash ("…{claim}nAnswer with…"), so a literal "n" replaces the newline.
6. *(minor, no effect if run per dataset)* `get_responses.py` builds output filenames from the CLI `eval_set` (None by default), so files overwrite each other.
7. *(minor)* In the analysis notebook, invalid-response exclusion sits inside a loop over GPT ids, and the file has merge-conflict markers.
8. *(minor, affects Fig. 3 slightly)* The correlation pivot averages the SBB "yes" indicator (0 for salary questions) with the salary values for the same ids.
9. *(minor, no effect)* The stale `generation/visualizations.ipynb` uses bootstrap B=1 and α=.05; the camera-ready notebook matches the paper (B=1000, α=.01).

**paper_internal**
1. The text says 11/18 significant combinations for AITA, while Fig. 5 marks 10.
2. The text says older groups get "up to 5,000$ higher salaries", while Table 6 shows 7,849$ (explicit-user, 18-34 vs 55+).
3. The captions of Tables 5/6 say "0.01% level", while the method uses α=.01.
4. Appendix C says "sizes from 8B to 71B", but the largest model is Qwen2.5-72B.
