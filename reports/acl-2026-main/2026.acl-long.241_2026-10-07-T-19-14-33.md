## Summary
The released code and data are sufficient to re-run the whole study (fine-tuning of BERT/GPT‑2/TurnGPT/LLaMA‑3‑8B/Qwen‑3‑8B, embedding extraction + k-means silhouette analysis, NLG evaluation), but three bugs had to be fixed, one of which silently changes the 8B results.

**Main claim — fine-tuning increases the silhouette score of backchannel/filler embeddings (Fig. 4, Tables 6–14).**
* **Reproduced in direction for no-context and one-context inputs for all four model families** (BERT‑MASK, GPT‑2‑NTP, LLaMA‑3‑NTP, Qwen‑3‑NTP, English and Japanese, PCA‑100 space): every one of the 16 (model × language × {no,one}-ctx) PCA comparisons shows an increase after fine-tuning, as in the paper. Many values match the paper numerically (all BERT and Qwen‑3 no‑ft values, most GPT‑2 PCA values, BERT/GPT‑2 fine-tuned values within ≈0.01–0.05). The Table 6 bootstrap means (ours n=30) are reproduced within the paper's CI for BERT (except the two mis-printed BERT‑EN one-ctx entries, see Discrepancies), GPT‑2‑EN and all Qwen‑3 no-ft settings; GPT‑2‑JP and TurnGPT‑JP come out higher, LLaMA‑3 no-ft lower.
* **Not reproduced for LLaMA‑3 English with full context**: fine-tuning *decreases* the silhouette (point estimate PCA 0.258→0.199, bootstrap 0.259→0.204; paper 0.205→0.299 / Table 6 0.210→0.301). **LLaMA‑3 Japanese full context is ambiguous**: point estimate decreases (0.312→0.216; paper 0.330→0.344), but the bootstrap mean increases (0.311→0.413±0.075; paper 0.318→0.408). Qwen‑3 full context reproduces (EN 0.167→0.264, JP 0.168→0.181; paper 0.176→0.289, 0.169→0.178).
* **Qwen‑3 fine-tuning diverges with the released config**: the released fp16 AMP setting (lr 1e‑4, bs 1, LoRA + fully-trained embed/lm_head) blew up at ≈24 % of the first epoch (grad-norm 30+, then loss stuck at ≈5 with zero gradients, NaN weights from 50 % on); the resulting adapter produces NaN logits. We re-trained both Qwen‑3 runs with bf16 mixed precision (only change), which trained smoothly (final loss 1.97 EN / 1.74 JP).
* **TurnGPT (TTP)**: improves over the GPT‑2 baseline for Japanese (no-ctx 0.189→0.394, one-ctx 0.151→0.308) and English one-ctx (0.167→0.212), but *not* for English no-ctx (0.248→0.210; paper 0.245→0.263). TurnGPT checkpoints are not released; we trained them with the paper's hyper-parameters.
* **Original-dimension ("orig.") columns** agree less well (GPT‑2 orig values are mostly 0.03–0.15 above the paper (EN ft no-ctx 0.05 below), LLaMA‑3 orig no-ft values ≈0.2 below; Qwen‑3 EN no-ctx orig decreases after fine-tuning while the paper reports an increase).
* **LLaMA‑3 baselines differ strongly from the paper** (no-ft no-ctx PCA 0.227 vs 0.424; orig 0.207 vs 0.407) although Qwen‑3, BERT and GPT‑2 baselines match: the suzume LLaMA‑3 tokenizer adds no BOS token while the code's LLaMA-specific `[1:]` slicing assumes one; prepending BOS raises the value to 0.297 (diagnostic), explaining part of the gap.

**Secondary claim — NLG (Fig. 6, Tables 4/5)**: after fine-tuning, BC/filler frequency increases and the (code's) BC/filler perplexity drops for all 6 model×language pairs, BERTScore increases in 6/6 and BLEU in 3/3 English pairs — as in the paper. BLEU *decreases* for all three Japanese pairs (paper: increases), and diversity decreases for GPT‑2‑EN and Qwen‑3‑EN (paper: increases). The released `infer.py` crashes as released, evaluates on the whole corpus (incl. the 80 % training split) instead of the 20 % held-out split, and computes "perplexity" as exp(entropy) of the predictive distribution rather than the paper's formula.

## What was attempted

**Paper's main claim** (abstract, §4–§7, Fig. 4, Tables 6–14): fine-tuning (MASK for BERT, NTP for GPT‑2 / LLaMA‑3‑8B / Qwen‑3‑8B, TTP = TurnGPT for GPT‑2) increases the silhouette score of k‑means clustering of the contextual embeddings of the top‑15 backchannels/fillers (English: Switchboard+MapTask; Japanese: BTSJ), for no‑/one‑/full‑context inputs. **Secondary claim** (§6, Fig. 6, Tables 4/5): fine‑tuned LMs generate dialogue continuations with more / more diverse backchannels/fillers, lower BC/filler perplexity, higher BERTScore and BLEU.

**Code / data**: https://github.com/colalao/discourse_markers (linked in the paper). The repository ships the pre-processed English and Japanese CSVs (all data + the 80/20 NTP/MASK split + TurnGPT train/val/test split), fine-tuning (`NTP_and_MASK/train.py`, `TurnGPT/turngpt/train.py`), embedding+clustering (`test.py`), NLG evaluation (`infer.py`) and the bootstrap notebook. No checkpoints are released (TurnGPT's `test_config.py` points to un-released `finetune_model/*.ckpt`).

**Environment**: one conda env (python 3.10, torch 2.5.1+cu124, transformers 4.51.3, peft 0.15.2, scikit‑learn 1.7.2, cuML 24.12 (only imported), pytorch‑lightning 1.9.5 for TurnGPT, bert_score 0.3.13, nltk, fugashi+unidic‑lite, sentencepiece). 1× H200, 14 CPU cores.

### Settings that differ from the paper / released config
* **Scaled down**: LLaMA‑3 JP, Qwen‑3 EN and Qwen‑3 JP LoRA fine-tuning for **1 epoch instead of 3** (time budget: the released config trains with batch size 1 in fp32+AMP; 3 epochs of all four 8B runs would have taken ≈35 GPU-hours on the H200). LLaMA‑3 EN uses the full 3 epochs.
* **Bootstrap (Table 6, Fig. 4 error bars) not run**: the call is commented out in the released `test.py`; with cuML it would cost several GPU-hours per setting. We compare with the point estimates of Tables 7–14 (same procedure as the released code) and list Table 6 only for reference.
* **NLG sample size**: the released `infer.py` draws every `infer_ratio`‑th example of the *whole* corpus; we used ratios 20 (EN) / 25 (JP) → 4 406 / 5 039 examples ("around 4000 utterances", App. D.3).
* **Speed-only changes in our driver** (`scripts/run_embed_cluster.py`, not in the repo): (i) hidden states are extracted once and both the raw ("orig.") and the PCA‑100 embeddings are derived from them with the repo's own `batch_embedding_pca`; (ii) `--batched`: right-padded, length-sorted batches with attention mask instead of batch size 1, and a list-based re-implementation of `find_token_index` with identical logic (verified on GPT‑2 EN ft_no: identical label counts, every vector has a counterpart within L2 0.4 for vectors of norm ~10²); (iii) TF32 matmuls for the 8B extraction runs (except LLaMA‑3 EN no_ft no/one ctx, which ran in full fp32); (iv) only the last layer is clustered and t‑SNE plots are skipped (qualitative only). The k‑means / silhouette procedure (`utils/k_means.py`, k=2..15, max silhouette per word, average over the 15 words) is the released code.
* **Qwen‑3 mixed precision**: bf16 instead of the released fp16 (the fp16 run diverged to NaN, see Summary).
* GPU/CPU contention: many jobs shared the single GPU; this only affects wall-clock.

### Non-determinism of the released pipeline (important for judging "numbers match")
* `KMeans(n_clusters=k, random_state=42)` uses a single k-means++ initialisation (sklearn ≥1.4 default `n_init='auto'`), so the per-word *maximum* silhouette is sensitive to tiny input perturbations. Re-running the identical GPT‑2 EN ft_no PCA embeddings with k‑means seeds 1,2,3 gives 0.301, 0.299, 0.281 (seed 42: 0.339; an unbatched extraction of the same model gave 0.280). For GPT‑2 EN no_ft_no: 0.243/0.242/0.248 (seed 42: 0.248). So differences of ≈±0.03 between our numbers and the paper's are within the pipeline's own run-to-run noise (the paper's Table 6 bootstrap half-widths are also 0.01–0.07).
* `no_ft` runs add 4 special tokens whose embeddings are randomly initialised by `resize_token_embeddings` (no seed); `args.interjection` is built from a `set()` (order varies per process); sklearn PCA on 4096‑d inputs uses the randomized solver without a seed.

### As released vs. after fixes (8B fine-tuned rows; everything else is identical as released)
The released `utils/get_embedding.py` loads the base model with `AutoModel` and then the LoRA adapter (saved from an `AutoModelForCausalLM` with `modules_to_save=[embed_tokens, lm_head]`). With `peft` 0.15.2 this raises `KeyError: 'base_model.model.embed_tokens.weight'` (log: `logs/ext_English_llama3_ft_no.failed_asreleased.log`). With `peft` 0.14.0 (installed side-by-side in `./peft014`, used only for this test) the unmodified loader runs but prints *"Found missing adapter keys …"* for **every** LoRA matrix, i.e. the "fine-tuned" embeddings come from the **base weights** (only the tokenizer differs). ### Diagnostics
* **k-means initialisation noise** (repo's KM code, same PCA embeddings, k-means seeds 1–3 vs. 42): GPT‑2 EN no_ft_no 0.242–0.248; GPT‑2 EN ft_no 0.281–0.339 (two extractions); LLaMA‑3 EN no_ft_no 0.228–0.232; LLaMA‑3 EN ft_no 0.453–0.461 (`results/recluster.jsonl`).
* **LLaMA BOS** (`diag_bos`): the code strips the first token of each LLaMA pattern (`input_ids[0][1:]`), assuming a BOS token, but the suzume tokenizer (all HF revisions) adds none, so `<ds>` is dropped from the search pattern and the model never sees a BOS. Forcing a Meta-style BOS raises LLaMA‑3 EN no_ft no-ctx from 0.227 to 0.297 (PCA) / 0.207 to 0.285 (orig) — part, but not all, of the gap to the paper's 0.424/0.407.
* Bootstrap means are systematically above the point estimates (resampling with replacement creates duplicate points), consistent with Table 6 vs Tables 7–14 in the paper.

## Why not fully reproduced
* Time budget (48 h, 1 GPU): 3 of the 4 8B fine-tunings were run for 1 epoch instead of 3; bootstrap with n=30 instead of 1000.
* Bugs in the release (adapter loading, `infer.py` crash, TurnGPT imports) and an fp16 divergence of Qwen‑3 with the released config (fixed by bf16).
* Some of the paper's numbers are not reproduced even after fixes: LLaMA‑3 no-ft baselines (≈0.2 lower), LLaMA‑3 full-context fine-tuning effect (EN reversed; JP ambiguous), TurnGPT EN no-ctx, Qwen‑3 EN orig-space no-ctx, Japanese BLEU and English diversity trends in the NLG evaluation. The pipeline is also quite noisy (single-init k-means, unseeded random init of new special-token embeddings), which limits numerical agreement to roughly ±0.03.
* No blocker from data/licensing: all three corpora are shipped pre-processed in the repository.
