**Summary.**
1. **Acceptance length τ at T=0 matches Table 1.** Using the released `jakiro/` code and checkpoints, mean τ is within 0–5% of the paper on all 5 models: 4.69/4.72, 4.51/4.51, 4.57/4.57, 4.69/4.60, 4.14/4.34 (ours/paper). The EAGLE-2 baseline, run with the official EAGLE code and weights, also matches Table 1 (mean τ within 2% everywhere). So the setup is sound.
2. **The claimed speedup over EAGLE-2 does not reproduce.** On our H200, Jakiro is slower than or tied with EAGLE-2 in all 10 model×temperature settings, and in both independent runs. The mean-S gap (Jakiro − EAGLE-2) ranges from 0.00 to −0.22. Our τ also never exceeds EAGLE-2's. In the paper's own Table 1, Jakiro's τ is already *below* Eagle2's at T=0 for 4 of the 5 models, so the T=0 speed win in the paper depends entirely on draft-cost/hardware effects. Those effects do not carry over to H200. Absolute S is not directly comparable to A100-40G.
3. **The large non-greedy (T=1) gains in Table 1 come from a sampler that is not lossless.**
   - The main `jakiro/` code at T=1 (EAGLE-2-style verification, which our probe confirms is distribution-preserving) gives τ ≈ EAGLE-2 or below: V7B mean τ 4.14 vs the paper's 5.45.
   - The paper's T=1 Jakiro numbers are reproduced only by the separate `jakiro_star/` ("Jakiro*") code path: V7B mean τ 5.39–5.43 vs the paper's 5.45; L2-7B 4.94–4.96 vs 5.09. The authors' own A40 output files shipped in the repo also show this.
   - That code's T>0 acceptance rule uses a wrong draft probability `q`: `sample_moe` samples from a softmax over the concatenated 2×vocab logits of both experts, then always reads `q` from the *second* half (`gather(..., sampled_indices + vocab_size)`). Its residual distribution is `softmax(mean(probabilities))`, a softmax of probabilities. `q` is therefore underestimated and `p/q` inflated.
   - Our losslessness probe re-scores every T=1 output under the target model. For vanilla sampling, EAGLE-2 and `jakiro/`, the per-token target-probability distributions agree (KS statistic ≤ 0.014; mean target-token probability within 0.005). For `jakiro_star/` they differ drastically: V7B mean target probability 0.37 vs 0.79, argmax rate 0.45 vs 0.85, KS statistic 0.45, p≈0. The **authors' own shipped A40 Jakiro* outputs** show the same signature (0.37 vs 0.79; KS 0.46).
   - So the T=1 numbers in Table 1 do not come from lossless speculative sampling. This contradicts the paper's statement that it verified statistical equivalence with a KS test (p > 0.05). Even with the inflated τ, Jakiro* is no faster than EAGLE-2 on H200: V7B 2.38–2.42× vs 2.35×; L2-7B 2.33–2.37× vs 2.62–2.64×; L2-13B 2.14× vs 2.87–2.90×.

The baselines and the T=0 τ reproduce, but the paper's central claim does not hold under a valid (lossless) comparison: Jakiro beats EAGLE-2, "with particularly notable improvements in non-greedy decoding". Result match is therefore **NO_MATCH** and the verdict is **FAILED**.

## What was run

- **Environment:** conda env at `./env` (Python 3.10, torch 2.1.2+cu121, transformers 4.36.2, accelerate 0.26.1, fschat 0.2.36, openai 0.28.0, numpy<2, scipy). The repo's `requirements.txt` is unpinned; these versions follow EAGLE's pins.
- **Hardware:** 1× NVIDIA H200 141 GB (the paper uses A100-40G). The node's CPUs were shared with another user's job, which can affect wall-clock time slightly.
- **Models:**
  - Vicuna-7B/13B-v1.3 (`lmsys/*`).
  - LLaMA2-Chat-7B/13B. The Meta repos are gated (access pending), so we used the byte-identical public re-uploads `NousResearch/Llama-2-{7,13}b-chat-hf`.
  - Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct (`meta-llama`, already cached).
  - Draft heads: `Hhaiduo/Jakiro-*`, `Hhaiduo/Jakiro_star-*`, and the official EAGLE-2 heads `yuhuili/EAGLE-*`.
- **Driver:** `run_all2.sh` (phase A per model: Jakiro T=0/T=1, Jakiro* T=1, EAGLE-2 T=0/T=1, vanilla T=0; phase S: Jakiro* T=0; phase B: vanilla T=1) and `run_rep2.sh` (second run of Jakiro, EAGLE-2 and Jakiro*). The driver only calls the repos' own scripts:
  - `python -m jakiro.evaluation.gen_ea_answer_{vicuna,llama2chat,llama3chat} --ea_model_path <Hhaiduo/Jakiro-*> --base_model_path <base> --bench_name <b> --temperature {0.0,1.0} --total_token 60 --depth 5 --top_k 10` (the repo defaults, as in `eval_metric/*.sh`)
  - `python -m jakiro_star.evaluation.gen_ea_answer_{vicuna,llama2chat} --ea_model_path <Hhaiduo/Jakiro_star-*> ... --temperature 1.0` (mc_sim_7b_63 static tree, the repo default)
  - `python -m jakiro.evaluation.gen_baseline_answer_{vicuna,llama2chat,llama3chat}` for vanilla autoregressive decoding (speedup denominator; same temperature as the method)
  - EAGLE-2: `python -m eagle.evaluation.gen_ea_answer_{vicuna,llama2chat,llama3chat} --total-token 60 --depth 5 --top-k 10` from `SafeAILab/EAGLE` at commit 3653144, the EAGLE-2-era code with transformers 4.36.2 (cloned into `./eagle_code`, unmodified). Its benchmark question files are byte-identical to Jakiro's.
- **Metrics:** `analyze.py` reimplements `jakiro/evaluation/speed_accept_len.py` exactly. S = mean over questions of SD tokens/s ÷ mean over questions of vanilla tokens/s. τ = mean over questions of new_tokens / #target forward passes. The 6-benchmark mean is the arithmetic mean, as in `eval_metric/*.sh`. With 2 runs, the reported values are averages over the runs.
- **Losslessness probe (our addition):** `probe_lossless.py`. For every T=1 answer, it rebuilds the exact generation prompt and re-scores the first 128 response tokens with the target model. It reports mean target probability, mean log-probability, target-argmax rate, and a two-sample KS test of per-token target probabilities against vanilla T=1 sampling.

### Differences from the paper's setup
- GPU: H200 instead of A100-40G, so absolute speedups differ. Jakiro and EAGLE-2 were compared on the same GPU with identical settings.
- 2 runs instead of 4 seeds (42/123/456/789). The scripts expose no seed argument: they use `torch.manual_seed(choice_index)`, and the T>0 acceptance uses Python's unseeded `random`. The LLaMA3 scripts call `set_seed(0)`, so their two runs are identical in tokens and differ only in timing.
- Models: Vicuna-33B, LLaMA2-Chat-70B and LLaMA3-70B were not run because no Jakiro checkpoints for them are released. The README says they are "being organized".
- Medusa, Hydra and Eagle1 baselines were not run; only EAGLE-2 was reproduced (official code and weights). Claims against Medusa, Hydra and Eagle1 are NOT_COMPARABLE.
- LLaMA2-Chat base weights come from the NousResearch mirror (same weights).
- Tables 2–4 (Eagle-3†, Flash Decoding, ablations) were not attempted: they need retrained or unreleased models or code.

## Codebase completeness: MINOR_FIXES

Changes to the released code (left uncommitted in `./codebase`, see `git diff`):
1. `jakiro/evaluation/gen_ea_answer_vicuna.py:11`: commented out the hard-coded `os.environ["CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES"] = "3"`. Slurm assigns the GPU.
2. `jakiro_star/evaluation/gen_ea_answer_vicuna.py:11`: commented out the hard-coded `os.environ["CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES"] = "4"`.

No other code edits. In particular, we did **not** fix the Jakiro* sampling bug; we ran and measured it as released. The EAGLE code (`./eagle_code`) is unmodified.

Scripts we wrote (outside the repo):
- `run_all2.sh` / `run_rep2.sh`: sequential drivers over models, benchmarks and temperatures. They are thin wrappers around the repo's own eval scripts; `eval_metric/*.sh` does the same with hard-coded author paths.
- `analyze.py`: the S/τ computation, a copy of `speed_accept_len.py` logic over all runs.
- `compare.py` + `paper_values.py`: paper-vs-ours tables.
- `probe_lossless.py`: the distribution-preservation probe. This is our addition and is not needed to reproduce Table 1.
- `download*.py`: model downloads.

Release issues found:
- The paper's T=1 Jakiro numbers come from `jakiro_star/`. Its speculative-sampling acceptance uses a mis-indexed draft probability and a wrong residual (`softmax` of probabilities), so it is **not lossless**. This bug affects the headline results.
- The HF checkpoint `Jakiro_star-Vicuna-13B-v1.3` is a byte-for-byte duplicate of `Jakiro_star-LLaMA2-Chat-13B`.
- `jakiro_star` crashes at T=0 (there is no greedy path in `sample_moe`).
- There are no checkpoints for Vicuna-33B, LLaMA2-70B, LLaMA3-70B or Jakiro*-LLaMA3.
- The Medusa, Hydra and Eagle1 baselines have no code in the release. EAGLE-2 comes from the separate official repo.
- The README's evaluation instructions say to *uncomment* `MixtralSparseMoeBlock_train` for evaluation, the opposite of the code's comments. We kept the code default, the inference block, which loads the released checkpoints with only `embed_tokens` missing; that is expected, since it is loaded from the base model.
- The vanilla baseline scripts never increment `new_token`, so the 1024-token cap is never applied; they stop at EOS or 1960 total tokens. The SD scripts cap at 512 (`eagenerate`) or 1024. Speed is computed per question as tokens/second, so this has only a small effect. It is inherited from EAGLE.
- `requirements.txt` is unpinned. `fschat` needs `openai<1` (`fastchat.llm_judge.common` imports `openai`).

## Why not fully reproduced

- **Hardware:** H200 instead of A100-40G. Absolute S is not comparable. The Jakiro vs EAGLE-2 comparison on the same hardware still contradicts the paper's ranking.
- **Missing checkpoints:** 33B/70B models, the Vicuna-13B Jakiro* head, and Jakiro* for LLaMA3.
- **Baselines:** Medusa, Hydra and Eagle1 not run (no code or configuration in the release).
- **Release bug:** the paper's non-greedy (T=1) results are produced by a non-lossless sampler (`jakiro_star/`). With the lossless `jakiro/` implementation, the non-greedy advantage disappears.
