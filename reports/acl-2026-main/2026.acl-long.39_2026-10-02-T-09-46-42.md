### Summary
- **Hardware-independent results reproduce numerically.** These are the RRC rows of Tables 1/4/5 (capacity, entropy, entropy utilization at 128 bits on GPT-2, OPT-1.3b and Llama-2-7b) and the utilization rows of the scalability Tables 2/6/7 (32 to 8192 bits). All 33 such numbers are within 5% relative. Every utilization comes out at about 100% (96.9 to 100.7%), so the paper's central claim (~100% entropy utilization, stable across message lengths) holds.
- **The headline speed reproduces.** GPT-2 at 128 bits gives 1483.6 bits/s against the paper's 1554.66 bits/s (−4.6%).
- **Other speeds depend on hardware and implementation.** They are H200 vs. the paper's RTX 6000 Ada, and the release is a re-implementation with binary-search candidate selection (see CHANGELOG). Speeds are the same order of magnitude or faster than the paper's. Unlike the paper, they do *not* fall at long message lengths: for example, Llama-2 at 8192 bits runs at 283 bits/s against the paper's 46.1. I label these `TREND_MATCH` / `NOT_COMPARABLE` rather than `NUMBERS_MATCH`, which is why the overall label is TREND_MATCH and not NUMBERS_MATCH.
- **Baselines can't be compared.** AC, ADG, Meteor, iMEC, Discop and SparSamp are not in the release, so "outperforms all baselines" (utilization; fastest on GPT-2) is **NOT_COMPARABLE**. The same applies to vanilla RC (Table 10), the steganalysis tables (Table 8, which needs SparSamp texts plus fine-tuned discriminators), human evaluation (Table 3) and perplexity (Table 9; the release writes "-" for PPL). None of these were run.
- **Extraction works after a fix.** Stegotexts round-trip at 100% when the receiver gets the token ids (200/200 at 128 bits for each model; 30/30 at 8192 bits for GPT-2; 30/30 at 2048 bits for Llama-2). This took a one-line fix in `RRC_extract.py`: as released, the checker reports about 56% because it parses the secret bit-string as an integer (details below).

## What was run
Environment: conda env `env/` with Python 3.12, torch 2.6.0+cu124, transformers 4.57.6, pandas and accelerate, on 1× NVIDIA H200 with 14 logical CPUs. The paper used Python 3.12.7, Torch 2.5.0 and an RTX 6000 Ada. Torch had to be ≥2.6 because transformers refuses `torch.load` of OPT-1.3b's `.bin` weights on torch<2.6.

Models: `openai-community/gpt2`, `facebook/opt-1.3b`, and **`NousResearch/Llama-2-7b-hf`**. The last is an ungated mirror of `meta-llama/Llama-2-7b-hf`, because our access request to the gated repo was still "awaiting review". Data: the release's `0.Prompts.tsv` (1000 C4 prompts, as in the paper). Settings: full vocabulary (`--top_k -1`), temperature 1, key 42, secret-message seed 0.

All embedding runs are **unmodified `RRC_embed.py`** invocations:
1. **Phase 1, main tables (Tables 1/4/5), 128 bits, all 1000 prompts.** Run one process at a time on an otherwise idle GPU, so the speeds are clean (`run_phase1.sh`):
   `python RRC_embed.py --language_model <m> --bit_length 128 --key 42 --top_k -1 --seed 0 --output ../results/<m>_bit128.tsv`
2. **Phase 2, scalability (Tables 2/6/7), l ∈ {32, 64, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192}, all 1000 prompts** (except Llama-2 at 8192, see below). Sharded with `--part/--part_max` and run up to 12 at a time (`scheduler.py` → `scheduler2.py` → `scheduler3.py`). These runs were used **only for utilization/capacity/entropy**, since timings under contention are not meaningful. Arguments: `--max_ctx_len 1000` (GPT-2), `2000` (OPT), `3900` (Llama-2, the default). `--max_tokens` was capped at 2048 for GPT-2/4096, 2500 for GPT-2/8192, 4096 for OPT/8192 and 6000 for Llama/8192; the default is 2·l.
3. **Phase 3, clean speed/running time for the scalability tables.** Single process on an otherwise idle GPU, using the first 200 prompts for l ≤ 1024 and the first 30 for l ≥ 2048 (`run_phase3.sh`, `--limit`).
4. **Extraction check.** `RRC_extract.py --mode both --limit 200` on the 128-bit outputs, plus `--mode ids` on the clean GPT-2/8192 and Llama-2/2048 outputs (`run_extract.sh`, `run_extract2.sh`).
5. **Aggregation.** `analyze.py` averages the per-sample TSV columns. Capacity is mean BPT, entropy is mean per-sample entropy, and utilization is the mean of the per-sample `Utilization` column the code writes (the ratio-of-means variant is also given in the notes). Speed is l / mean running time; this reproduces the paper's own speed/time pairs, e.g. 128/0.082 = 1561 ≈ 1554.66. Comparisons are built by `build_comparisons.py` and saved to `comparisons.json` and `results/*_summary.csv`.

### Differences from the paper / scale-downs
- **Llama-2-7b at 8192 bits: 506 of 1000 prompts** (every prompt with idx mod 44 < 22). About 49 GPU/CPU-hours at the paper's speed would not fit the 48 h budget alongside everything else.
- **Speed and running time for l ≠ 128** were measured on 200 prompts (l ≤ 1024) or 30 prompts (l ≥ 2048), not 1000.
- **Skipped prompts.** The release skips any prompt whose embedding exceeds the token budget, so some cells have fewer than 1000 samples. Counts: GPT-2 128-bit 999, Llama 128-bit 996, Llama 32-bit 989, Llama 64-bit 993, OPT 8192-bit 998, and one or two missing in several other cells.
- **GPT-2 at 8192 bits: only 681/1000 prompts embed.** GPT-2 has 1024 positions, and an 8192-bit message needs about 1400 tokens. The release's sliding KV-cache window (`--max_ctx_len`; the original code used a fixed 1000) drops the earliest tokens, and generation then collapses: per-step entropy falls from about 6 to about 0.07 bits (measured with `probe_gpt2_trim.py 1000`). Those prompts can never terminate, and 319 hit the 2500-token cap and were skipped. The paper reports 1000 GPT-2 samples at 8192 bits; the 681 that do embed show 99.98% utilization.
- Hardware: H200 instead of RTX 6000 Ada. Llama-2 weights come from an exact mirror repo.
- Baselines, vanilla RC (Table 10), steganalysis (Table 8), human evaluation (Table 3) and perplexity (Table 9) were not run, because no code is released for them.
- KL divergence was not measured. The code writes a constant 0 in the `KL` column, and the paper's 0/0 for RRC comes from its proof (§5.1), not from measurement.

## Discrepancies
**Code vs. paper**
1. *(minor; affects results slightly)* `rrc_core.embed_message` adds a sender-side "verified termination" replay that Algorithm 3 does not have. When the termination test fires but the receiver would decode the wrong value, the sender keeps generating. Per the README this adds 0.095% tokens at 128 bits.
2. *(minor; does not affect reported metrics)* The paper's security argument (Appendix proof) requires a *cryptographically secure* PRNG. The code uses Python's `random.Random(key)` (Mersenne Twister), reseeded with the same key for every message, and offsets are 53-bit floats (`Decimal(prng.random())`).
3. *(minor; affects the sample set)* The token budget defaults to 2·l, and prompts that exceed it are skipped. Together with the sliding KV window, this keeps GPT-2 at 8192 bits from embedding 32% of prompts, whereas the paper reports 1000 samples per length.
4. *(minor; no effect)* The `KL` column is hard-coded to 0 and `PPL` to "-". The paper's KLD of 0/0 is theoretical, and its PPL table (Table 9) cannot be produced with the release.
5. *(minor)* The CLI default `--language_model meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B` is not one of the paper's models. The model was passed explicitly.

**Paper-internal**
6. Table 7 (Llama-2, 128 bits) gives running time 1.143 s with speed 146.24 bits/s, but 128 / 146.24 = 0.875 s. Every other column of Tables 2/6/7 satisfies speed ≈ l / time (e.g. 64/0.443 = 144.5).
7. §6.2 defines embedding speed as "the average seconds required to embed a single secret bit", while every table reports it in bits/s, higher being better.

Note on history: the earlier version of the code (before commit f69fce8) used a fixed Decimal precision of 50 digits. The current README states that this gives 157 to 4766% "utilization" for l ≥ 256, which is unlike the paper's Tables 2/6/7. The paper's long-message numbers therefore cannot have come from that exact code. The current release (derived precision) does reproduce them.

## Codebase completeness: MINOR_FIXES
- **Environment.** torch had to be ≥2.6 (`transformers` 4.57 refuses `.bin` weights on torch<2.6; OPT-1.3b ships only `.bin`), and `requirements.txt` has no upper or lower torch pin for this. The gated `meta-llama/Llama-2-7b-hf` was replaced by its exact mirror `NousResearch/Llama-2-7b-hf`.
- **Code edit (the only one), `codebase/RRC_extract.py`.** `pd.read_csv(..., dtype=str)` replaces `pd.read_csv(...)`. Without it, the `Secret` bit-string is parsed as an integer, its leading zeros are lost, and every secret starting with 0 is scored as a failed recovery (reported recovery ≈56% instead of 100%). This affects only the extraction checker's reported recovery rate, which the paper does not report. It does not affect the embedding metrics.
- **Configuration.** `--max_ctx_len` must be set below the model's position limit (GPT-2 1000, OPT 2000) for long messages. `--max_tokens` was capped to bound the cost of prompts that degenerate after the window slides.
- **Scripts I wrote (outside the release).** `run_phase1.sh`, `scheduler*.py` (sharded parallel runner of unmodified `RRC_embed.py`), `run_phase3.sh`, `run_extract*.sh`, `analyze.py` (averages the per-sample TSV columns into table metrics; the release has no aggregation script), `build_comparisons.py`, `progress.sh`, `probe_gpt2_trim.py` (KV-window collapse probe).

## Operational notes
- Five Llama-2 8192-bit shards (parts 13, 16, 17, 18, 19) crashed with **GPU OOM** when 8 Llama processes with long KV caches shared the 141 GB GPU. The scheduler was restarted with at most 5 concurrent Llama processes, and the crashed shards were resumed with `--resume` (part 19 relaunched by hand). There are no duplicate rows, and `analyze.py` also de-duplicates by Idx.
- Under 12-way CPU contention, per-sample times were 2 to 7× slower than in single-process runs. That is why speeds were measured separately.
