### Settings that differ from the paper
- **Table 1 KL used bfloat16** instead of float32 (the README says bf16 is fine for KL). fp32 at this scale would have taken about 100 GPU-hours. I checked this on the first 20 prompts in fp32: values were within 2–4% of bf16 (e.g. Baseline 1.416 vs 1.472; "[CONTEXT TRUNCATED]" 1.192 vs 1.219). So bf16 does not explain the gap to the paper.
- **Soft-bridge training used TF32 matmuls** (fp32 storage) for speed: about 3× faster on H200. Embedding and extraction were strict fp32.
- Hardware was H200 rather than RTX 6000 Ada, and 4–8 processes shared each GPU. **Time (s) is therefore not comparable** and is not scored.
- The released `Baseline` strategy ("WinStega") is plain truncation to the latest w tokens. It has no WinStega entropy-threshold mechanism. That likely explains its higher capacity (2.99 vs 1.995). Robustness for WinStega uses the script's modelled dependency width of 2w.

**What holds:**
- BLEU, ROUGE-L and BERTScore match within ±0.005 for every method.
- The paper's headline improvements over WinStega reproduce: ROUGE-L 0.100 → 0.204 (paper 0.098 → 0.200), BLEU 0.085 → 0.279, BERTScore 0.700 → 0.827.
- The forward-KL soft bridge is best on BLEU and ΔPPL, and has the unusually high capacity (1.64 vs 1.61) noted in the paper's Limitations section.
- ΔPPL keeps the paper's ordering (WinStega ≫ ASW variants > soft-forward > …), but the individual values are noisy. ΔPPL is an absolute difference of mean PPLs, and the windowed stegotexts have lower PPL (≈10–11) than the covertexts (≈16) because they are repetitive.

**What does not hold:**
- Steganalysis accuracy. With the released discriminator, every w=10 method is detected at 0.95–0.97. The paper reports 0.745–0.885 for ASW versus 0.955 for WinStega.
- The ASW methods are still on average slightly less detectable than WinStega (e.g. soft-forward 0.948 vs 0.972). However, the gap (≈2 points) is inside seed-to-seed variation (test set = 200 texts). So the claimed imperceptibility improvement (−21.99%) is not reproduced.
- The windowed stegotexts almost always run to the 512-token cap (≥99% vs 81% for covertexts). They show visible topic drift and repetition, which a fine-tuned BERT picks up easily.

## Why not fully reproduced
- **Steganalysis:** the claimed imperceptibility gain disappears with the released discriminator. No bug was found: the stegotexts extract exactly and the text-similarity metrics match the paper. The likely reason is that the BERT steganalyzer in the release is stronger than the one the paper used, or is set up differently.
- **Table 1:** absolute values are systematically lower than the paper's. The cause is unknown; it is not the bf16 precision.
- **Coverage:** the secondary variant experiments were not run, to fit the time budget. The main tables took about 25 h of GPU time on 2×H200.
