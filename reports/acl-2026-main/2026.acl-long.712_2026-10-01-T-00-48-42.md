## What the paper claims / what we targeted
The paper introduces a VQA benchmark of paired synthetic images (FLUX.1-dev) and probes LLaVA-1.6-7B, Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct and DeepSeek-VL2 on four paradigms: factuality, perception, stereotype (SSCM traits) and decision making. Answers are constrained to the options with Outlines. The main quantitative artefacts are:
- **Fig. 4**: per-axis, per-model normalized scores for the four paradigms. The repo ships the exact numbers in `codebase/figures/spider_plots/*_normalized_scores.csv`.
- **Per-identity scores** behind Figs. 5/15–17 (`codebase/figures/intermediate/<model>/*.csv`).
- **Fig. 13**: decision-making selection frequency on cooking, per identity.
- **Tables 3/4**: polarity scores for Christian and Mafia.
- The identity-level claims in Section 6.

Proprietary-model experiments (A.4/A.5: GPT-5.2, Gemini) are out of scope: there are no API keys and they are not the central claims. Also not run:
- PATA real-image experiments (A.6): data not provided.
- Positional responses (A.7): no code released.
- LVLM-Interpret (A.8): no code released.
- Text-only vs. vision (Sec. 7.4, `llama_decisionmaking_text.py`): about 400k Llama queries as released, which is out of budget.

## What we did (exact pipeline)
1. **Environment.** `conda create -p ./env python=3.10`, with torch 2.5.1+cu124, transformers 4.46.3, outlines 0.1.11, diffusers 0.31.0, bitsandbytes, flash-attn 2.7.4.post1, xformers 0.0.28.post3 and datasets. `deepseek_vl2` was installed from github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-VL2 with `--no-deps`: it pins transformers 4.38.2, but it works with 4.46.3. The repo has no requirements file.
2. **Images (`repro/gen_subset.py`).**
   - Same prompt templates, descriptor lists (`maledes.csv`/`femaledes.csv`) and filename scheme as `src/gen_image.py`/`gen_image_noactivity.py`.
   - Model: `unsloth/FLUX.1-schnell` (Apache-2.0 mirror), bf16, 4 steps, 1024², guidance 0.
   - Activities: Cooking, Cleaning, Doing laundry, Programming, Teaching, Giving a presentation, Babysitting, Repairing a car, Gardening, Playing chess, Playing basketball, Playing guitar (3 per time-use category).
   - Output: 3,948 activity images (166 male + 161 female identities × 12) and 329 portraits.
3. **Pairs (`repro/make_pairs.py`).** Blending uses the released `merge.py:merge_and_save_images`, following the intra-axis pairing rules. Instead of all combinations we sampled:
   - identity contrast: each identity in ~1 pair per activity and gender → 1,956 pairs;
   - identity-activity contrast: 163 pairs;
   - activity contrast: 329 pairs;
   - portraits: each identity in ~2 pairs → 328 pairs.
   - Pairs are written into the directory layout the released VQA scripts read (`flux2/test_merged_images/{same_activity,different_activity,same_descriptor}/...`, `noactivity_images/merged_images/...`).
   - The gender axis exists only in the male file. The scripts crash on a missing female dir, so the female dir is a symlink to the male one, and analysis keeps only the male rows.
4. **VQA: the released scripts, run unchanged in logic.**
   - Scripts: `factuality.py --setting 1/2`, `perception.py --setting 1/2/3`, `stereotype.py`, `decisionmaking.py`, plus their `llama_*` and `ds_*` counterparts.
   - Each script is run for all 8 axes (`VIG_AXIS` env var) from `codebase/src/_run`, driven by `repro/runner.py` with several concurrent workers.
   - Llama weights: `unsloth/Llama-3.2-11B-Vision-Instruct` (identical public mirror; the meta-llama repo is gated for us). DeepSeek: `deepseek-ai/deepseek-vl2`. LLaVA: `llava-hf/llava-v1.6-vicuna-7b-hf`.
   - Settings are the scripts' defaults: 4-bit NF4 for LLaVA and Llama, temperature 0.7, 672×336 resize; greedy decoding for DeepSeek.
5. **Extra runs.**
   - (a) Factuality setting 1 with the distractor fix (`VIG_FIX_DISTRACTORS=1`), all 3 models, same pairs.
   - (b) Black-image ablation: 126 sampled identity-contrast pairs run through the released factuality-1 and decision-making scripts with the real image and with an all-black image of the same name.
6. **Metrics (`repro/analyze.py`).**
   - A faithful re-implementation of `src/visualization/correlate.py`: `compute_{factuality,perception,stereotype,decisionmaking}_scores` and the `plot_cross_model_spiderplots` normalization. That is, per-axis median over identities; perception = 100 − "struggle" selection %; stereotype = min–max over all models × axes. We re-implemented rather than imported because `correlate.py` executes plotting at import time and hard-codes DeepSeek file names.
   - Fig. 13: cooking rows of decision making.
   - Tables 3/4: S_high − S_low per SSCM pair.
   - The comparison table is built by `repro/build_comparisons.py`.

## Why it is not fully reproduced
1. **Data not released.** The 30M+ images and ~36 GB of outputs are behind placeholder links in the README. The paper's generator (FLUX.1-dev) is license-gated, and our token has no access. So we regenerated a subset with FLUX.1-schnell. These are different images; some "with their face visible" prompts come out as close-up faces without a clearly visible activity, which lowers factuality. Our sampled pairs are about 1–5% of the benchmark.
2. **Compute.** The released scripts issue one constrained generation per question and re-encode the image every time. At ~0.4–1.6 s per question, the full benchmark is infeasible in 48 h on one GPU. Sampled per-identity estimates use only ~24 identity-contrast pairs (factuality/perception/decision) or 4 portrait pairs (stereotype) per identity. The stereotype/polarity numbers (Fig. 4 stereotype, Tables 3/4) are therefore very noisy, in steps of 25 points for Tables 3/4.
3. **A results-affecting bug in factuality** (see Discrepancies). The paper's factuality ranking holds only with the bug in place.
4. **Validity issue with the constrained-decoding protocol (not a code bug, but it affects interpretation).**
   - With the released prompts ("... ASSISTANT:" followed immediately by an Outlines regex over the bare option strings), every allowed first token has a log-prob of about −25, while the model's preferred continuations ("The", "In") are at about −0.6.
   - For a clear cooking image, the model's own likelihood ranks "cooking" first (−27 vs ≤ −30), yet Outlines greedy decoding returns "walking a dog". It returns the same answer for an all-black image.
   - In the black-image ablation, 62–74% of decision-making choices are unchanged when the image is replaced by an all-black one.
   - For comparison, real-vs-real retest agreement is 71–75% for the sampled LLaVA/Llama runs and 97% for greedy DeepSeek.
   - Decisions are therefore driven mostly by the identity words in the options. The image moves ~5–12% of LLaVA/Llama decisions beyond sampling noise, and ~24% of DeepSeek's.
   - This likely explains why identity-level decision/perception patterns reproduce so well (ρ≈0.9) despite completely different images.

## Discrepancies
**Code vs. paper:**
1. **Factuality distractors** (`factuality.py`/`llama_factuality.py`/`ds_factuality.py:get_random_activities`). **Major; affects results.**
   - Paper (§4.1, A.3): the options are the ground truth plus "three random activities" as distractors.
   - Code: always returns the first three entries of `activities.json` ("Grocery shopping", "Cooking", "Sleeping"), Capitalized. The ground-truth option is lowercase from the filename, so it is never excluded, and on cooking images it appears twice ("cooking" and "Cooking"). Scoring is case-sensitive, so "Cooking" counts as wrong.
   - Effect: Llama, which prefers the capitalized options, scores 1–4% (paper and ours), and the Fig. 4 / Insight 7 factuality ranking depends on it.
2. **Decision-making questions** (`*decisionmaking.py` + `correlate.py:compute_decisionmaking_scores`). Minor; affects results.
   - Paper (§4.4): one question per activity for each of six SSCM dimensions.
   - Code: 9 questions per pair, including 3 `ability_irr*` questions about randomly drawn roles from other activity categories. All 9 are pooled into the decision score.
3. **Quantization / decoding.** Minor; affects results.
   - Paper (A.3): T=0.7, top_p=0.95, max_tokens 200; A100 80GB; no quantization mentioned.
   - Code: LLaVA and Llama are loaded in 4-bit NF4 (bitsandbytes), with no top_p. DeepSeek uses greedy decoding (`do_sample=False`, `max_new_tokens=128`) even though a temperature-0.7 sampler is passed.
4. **Fig. 4 aggregation** (`correlate.py:plot_cross_model_spiderplots`). Minor; affects results.
   - Paper (§7.2): "scores are normalized and aggregated".
   - Code: per-axis *median* over identities. Perception uses only the "struggle" question (100 − %). Stereotype is min–max over all models × axes. Factuality credits both identities of a pair for every question.
5. **Activity coverage of decision making** (`data/activities/roles.json`). Minor.
   - Paper: 75 activities, with a role question for each.
   - Data: roles.json has 74 activities ("Smoking a cigar" is missing).
6. **Perception wording** (`perception.py`). Minor.
   - Paper: "Who is better at {activity}?"
   - Code: "Who is capable of {activity} better?"
7. **Identity counts** (`data/descriptors/maledes.csv`/`femaledes.csv`). Minor.
   - Paper (Table 8): 167 male / 162 female identities.
   - Data: 166 / 161 visible identities.

**Paper internal:**
1. §A.4 says three proprietary models plus Qwen3-VL. The same section later says "Figure 13 shows ... both proprietary models", and A.3 says API credits were spent across GPT-5.2 and Gemini-3-Flash only.
2. §7.2 says "LLaMA-3.2 and DeepSeek-VL2 perform lower, with DeepSeek-VL2 showing the weakest grounding". The released Fig. 4 data (`spider_plots/factuality_*_scores.csv`) has Llama far lowest on every axis (1.1–4.0) and DeepSeek at 21.3–35.3.
3. §7.2 says perception and decision-making scores "all fall roughly in the 40%–50% range". The released Fig. 4 data (`spider_plots/*_normalized_scores.csv`) range 41.6–58.0 for perception and 42.7–54.7 for decision making.
