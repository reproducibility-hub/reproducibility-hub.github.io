# Reproduction report: 2026.acl-long.711
**"To Think or Not to Think: The Hidden Cost of Meta-Training with Excessive CoT Examples"** (Kothapalli et al., ACL 2026)

## Verdict
- **Verdict: PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED**
- **Result match (after fixes): TREND_MATCH.** The Figure 2a (TF-4) conclusions all hold. Under the paper's metric, 41 of 60 numbers fall within tolerance (11 more match in trend). The α=0 baseline is seed-sensitive: with the released seed it never left the pre-transition plateau (see below).
- **Result match as released: DOES_NOT_RUN.** The unmodified `train.py` crashes at start-up, and the unmodified vLLM evaluator crashes on model load.
- **Coverage: SUBSET.** Only TF-4 (Figure 2a) was run, but at the paper's full scale: T = 6.4M sequences, 50k steps, batch 128, 10^4 eval prompts, all 5 α, K' ∈ {0,10,20,30,39}, Force Think / Force Answer. TF-8, TF-12, Section 6 (length generalization) and Section 7 (Qwen2.5 / CIL-LangSym SFT) were not run.

## What the paper claims (targets)
Section 5 / Figure 2 shows meta-training on CoT-ICL Lab-2.0 (|V|=1024, d=10, N=M=C={4}, K=40, T=64×10^5) with CoT-Recipe α ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 2, ∞}.
1. **α=0** (all-CoT training) is over-reliant on CoT examples. Force-Think accuracy collapses as K' (the number of CoT examples stripped from the prompt) grows: ≈0.93 → ≈0.13–0.18.
2. **α=∞** (no CoT in training) cannot think: Force Think ≈ 0. Force Answer rises with K'.
3. **α ∈ {0.5, 1, 2}** keep Force-Think accuracy high until K'=30. At K'=39 (no CoT examples left), α=2 with Force Think is on par with α=∞ with Force Answer.
4. The gain over α=0 at K'=39 is large: "> 300 %" for TF-12 (0.18 → 0.78). The text gives numbers only for TF-12, so the TF-4 values below were read off Figure 2a (uncertainty ≈ ±0.02).

## What I ran
- Environment: conda env (python 3.12, torch 2.4.0+cu121, transformers 4.45.2, vllm 0.6.3.post1, datasets 3.2.0). The repo's `pixi` spec pins transformers 4.44.2 and torch 2.4.0+cu118 and does not include vLLM. vLLM 0.6.3 needs transformers ≥ 4.45, hence 4.45.2. The unit tests pass (102/102).
- Training, one GPU (H200). Command: `scripts/train_all.sh 0 1 2 3 4`, which runs `torchrun --nproc-per-node=1 -m tokenized_cot_icl.core.train --task_card_key k` with `REPRO_BF16=1`.
  - Task card `repro_section5_power_law_alpha` (added) is a copy of the released `hybrid_power_law_alpha` with these changes:
    - N=M=C=(4,) instead of (3,4), the Section 5 setting;
    - batch_size=128, equal to the paper's 16/rank × 8 GPUs;
    - eval every 5000 steps (10000 for α=0), checkpoints every 10000.
  - Unchanged from the release: lr 5e-5, AdamW, n_tasks=6.4M (→ 50,000 steps), `ablation_fixed_H=True, num_unique_H=1`, hybrid_special_token prompt, power-law recipe (a=1, b=0).
  - TF-4: Llama, 4 layers, hidden 2048, 32 heads. That is 243,288,064 parameters without embeddings, exactly Table 1.
- Extra α=0 run with a different model-init / data-order seed (`REPRO_INIT_SEED=7`; the task data seed stays 42, so the task and eval set are identical). Script: `scripts/queue_seed2.sh`.
- Evaluation: `scripts/eval_vllm.py`, a batched wrapper around the released components:
  - the released `InferenceEvaluator._load_eval_dataset` / `EvalArgs`, giving 10^4 all-CoT eval prompts generated with the training task's embeddings and MLP;
  - the released `drop_k_cot_tokens_fn(transformation="random")` for K' ∈ {0,10,20,30,39};
  - greedy vLLM decoding in fp32, max 100 tokens, forced `think_start` / `answer_start` suffix.
  - Two scores are reported per setting:
    - **strict**: the paper's §3.1 rule (pattern (t_ans_start, ŷ, t_ans_end) in the response). Used as "Ours".
    - **released rule**: the vLLM evaluator's `o_ids[-2]==ans_end → pred=o_ids[-3]`.
  - Cross-check: on the α=0 step-10k checkpoint, the in-training HF-generate eval gave 0.393 and my vLLM eval gave 0.389.
- Wall clock: about 38.5 h in total (2026-10-08 00:00 → 2026-10-09 ≈14:30 CDT). That includes ~2.5 h of diagnostic runs; each TF-4 run takes ≈5.3 h in bf16.

### Settings that differ from the paper / release
| Item | Paper / release | Here | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPUs | 8×H100 DDP, 16/rank | 1×H200, batch 128 | budget; same global batch |
| Precision | release: pure fp32 (no autocast); paper: Liger kernels | bf16 autocast, fp32 master weights | fp32 = 2.45 s/step (34 h/run) vs 0.37 s/step |
| Model sizes | TF-4, TF-8, TF-12 | TF-4 only | TF-12 ≈ 20 h/run on 1 GPU |
| Eval engine | release: per-prompt `LLM.generate` calls, rebuilding the engine for each K' | one engine, batched generation, `skip_tokenizer_init=True`, `gpu_memory_utilization` 0.06–0.3 | released evaluator cannot load (no tokenizer saved); must share the GPU with training |
| Extra seed for α=0 | – | seed 7 for init/data order | seed-42 α=0 run stuck on the pre-transition plateau |

**bf16 sanity checks.** The data stream is seeded, so runs with different precision see identical batches:
- pure fp32 (released code) vs bf16, through step 500: losses 0.7414 vs 0.7366 at step 500, 0.7921 vs 0.7907 at step 400;
- TF32 vs bf16, through step 7.5k: in-training accuracy 0.381 @7.5k (TF32) vs 0.393 @10k (bf16), with matching losses.

Precision is therefore not what caused the α=0 plateau.

**α=0 seed sensitivity.** Figure 8a shows α=0 TF-4 sitting at ≈0.37 for the first ≈5×10^5 sequences, then jumping to ≈0.85 by 15×10^5. With the released seed (42), our α=0 model never made that jump (0.39 for all 50k steps). Its per-step accuracy was y1 0.94, y2 0.56, y3 0.42: it fails at inferring the in-context DAG. With seed 7 the jump happened by step 5k, and the final accuracy matches the paper (0.935 at K'=0). All α>0 runs with seed 42 made the jump on schedule, and their in-training eval curves match Figure 8a (final in-training eval: α=0.5 0.853, α=1 0.783, α=2 0.738, α=∞ 0.682, α=0 seed 7 0.932, vs. Figure 8a ≈0.85, 0.78, 0.74, 0.67, 0.93).

## Codebase completeness: MISSING_SCRIPTS
### Changes to the released code (left uncommitted in `./codebase`)
1. `core/train.py`: `create_metric_logger()` was called with no arguments, causing a TypeError at Trainer init (both Trainer classes). Now it is passed `self.args, device_id`. Crash fix.
2. `core/train.py`: optional bf16 autocast around the training forward pass (`REPRO_BF16=1`; default off). Compute budget.
3. `core/train.py`: optional TF32 (`REPRO_TF32`), gradient checkpointing (`REPRO_GRAD_CKPT`, not used in the end), and model-init/data-order seed override (`REPRO_INIT_SEED`). All default off; used for diagnostics and the seed-7 run.
4. `core/task_card.py`: added `repro_section5_power_law_alpha()` and set `TASK_CARD` to it. The release has no Section 5 card: its `hybrid_power_law_alpha` is the Section 6 N=M=C={3,4} setup, and `TASK_CARD` defaults to `hybrid_sanity_check`.
### Scripts written outside the release (`./scripts`)
- `eval_vllm.py`: batched evaluator built on released components. It adds the paper's strict answer-pattern scoring and also reports the released rule. Needed because the released `inference/vllm/evaluator_special_token.py` crashes: `LLM(..., skip_tokenizer_init=False)` on a checkpoint with no tokenizer gives an OSError, which I verified. `inference/evaluate.py` also references the undefined `eval_args_str` (NameError after all evals), and its task card points to `/opt/...` paths of a C=2 bs=32 model.
- `train_all.sh`, `eval_all.sh`, `rescore_all.sh`, `queue_seed2.sh`: run queues. `make_tables.py`, `aggregate.py`: comparison tables. `step_acc.py`, `bench.py`: per-chain-step accuracy and throughput diagnostics.
### Missing from the release
- No SFT script and no evaluation script for the Qwen2.5 / CIL-LangSym experiments (Section 7, Figures 5–7, the abstract's "130 %"). Only data generation (`core/data/symbolic/prepare.py`) and a chat-formatting `SymbolicDataModule` exist. Not attempted: the scripts are missing, the GPU was fully used by the Section 5 runs, and 7B full SFT plus about 500k long-context generations would not fit the budget.
- No released checkpoints or eval outputs.

## Discrepancies
1. **code_vs_paper (major):** answer scoring in `inference/vllm/evaluator_special_token.py::evaluate`.
   - Paper §3.1: search for the pattern (t_ans_start, ŷ, t_ans_end).
   - Code: only checks `o_ids[-2]==answer_end` and takes `o_ids[-3]`, without requiring `answer_start`. The in-training `SpecialTokenTrainer.cot_evaluate` does check `[-4]==answer_start`.
   - Effect: with Force Think, the α=∞ model emits `y, ans_end, eos` straight after the forced `think_start`. The released rule then reports 0.04/0.04/0.15/0.24/0.31 where the paper reports ≈0; the strict rule gives 0.000. For α=0 the released rule also inflates the K'≥20 numbers. For α ∈ {0.5,1,2} the two rules give identical scores.
2. **code_vs_paper (minor):** precision and kernels.
   - Paper App. C: trained with Liger kernels.
   - Code: `train.py` has no Liger integration and trains in pure fp32 without autocast. The README's `Args(use_liger_kernels=True)` field does not exist.
   - Affects speed only.
3. **code_vs_paper (minor):** the task's MLP cache.
   - Paper §3.2: sample C MLPs per sequence from a finite `TokenProcessorCache`.
   - Code: released configs and the evaluator default to `ablation_fixed_H=True, num_unique_H=1`, so one MLP is shared by every chain position and every sequence.
   - I used the released setting, and numbers match, so it presumably matches what the authors ran.
4. **code_vs_paper (minor):** drop strategy default.
   - Paper: CoT examples are dropped i.i.d. at random.
   - Code: the `evaluator_special_token.py` CLI default `--drop_strategy first_k`. The bulk `evaluate.py` uses `random`, which is what I used.
5. **code_vs_paper (minor):** missing Section 5 config. No task card exists for N=M=C={4}, and the shipped default card is a sanity check (see change 4).
6. **paper_internal:** §7.2 text says "out of the 3428 prompts with wrong final answers". Figure 6(b) says 3248, and 6752 + 3248 = 10^4.
7. **paper_internal:** Algorithm 5 lines 18–21 assign `a = fmt_a(y, CoT=True)` inside the if, then unconditionally overwrite it with `fmt_a(y, CoT=False)`, so no example would ever be CoT. This contradicts the text; the code (`SymbolicDataModule`) correctly uses if/else.
