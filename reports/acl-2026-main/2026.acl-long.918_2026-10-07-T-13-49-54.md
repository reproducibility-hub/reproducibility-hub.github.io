# Reproduction report — CAKE: Causal-Guided Adaptive Knowledge Editing for LLMs (ACL 2026, 2026.acl-long.918)

## Verdict
- **Verdict: PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED**
- **Result match (after fixes): MIXED**
- **Result match as released: DOES_NOT_RUN.** `Cake/Cake_main.py` imports a module that isn't in the release (`from notebooks.util import hparams`), so `experiments/evaluate.py` crashes on import. Removing that one unused import is the only change to CAKE's code path, and it doesn't affect results.
- **Coverage: FULL.** All Table 1 CAKE and AlphaEdit cells were run at the paper's scale: 3 models × 2 datasets, 2000 sequential edits in batches of 100, one run each.
- **Codebase completeness: MINOR_FIXES**

**Summary.** The released CAKE code runs on the Table 1 protocol, and our AlphaEdit baseline reproduces the paper's AlphaEdit row closely (mostly within ~1 point). CAKE's own Table 1 numbers mostly do **not** reproduce:
- They come out below the paper in almost every cell, sometimes by a lot. LLaMA3 CounterFact Gen is 88.88 vs 97.92; GPT2-XL CounterFact Spe is 67.18 vs 72.68 and Flu is 590.8 vs 614.5.
- In our runs, CAKE's advantage over AlphaEdit is small or absent:
  - LLaMA3 CounterFact: +1.1 Gen (paper: +11.7)
  - LLaMA3 ZsRE: about ±0.2
  - GPT-J CounterFact and ZsRE: tie or slightly worse
  - GPT2-XL: +1.7 to +4.6 on ZsRE, +1.8 Spe on CounterFact

**The L2 confound.** The released CAKE configs for GPT2-XL and GPT-J also raise the ridge regularizer `L2` (40 and 30, vs AlphaEdit's 10). The paper doesn't mention this. We re-ran AlphaEdit with the same L2:
- GPT2-XL: matched-L2 AlphaEdit equals CAKE on CounterFact and is within about 1 point on ZsRE. The GPT2-XL "gain" comes almost entirely from the L2 change, not from the causal allocation.
- GPT-J ZsRE: matched-L2 AlphaEdit is mixed against CAKE (Gen 96.68 vs 95.76; Eff 99.44 vs 99.69).

The paper's direction ("CAKE ≥ AlphaEdit") holds weakly in some settings (LLaMA3 CounterFact, GPT2-XL) and not in others (GPT-J, LLaMA3 ZsRE). Hence MIXED.

## What was attempted

- **Code:** https://github.com/zjh-vinky/CAKE (linked in the paper), commit `0b37823`, cloned to `./codebase`.
- **Environment:** conda env with python 3.10. Packages pinned as in the README: torch 2.6.0, transformers 4.51.3, datasets 2.21.0, spacy 3.4.1, and so on. Also `numpy<2`, because spacy/thinc 3.4 binaries need numpy 1.x, and `accelerate`.
- **Commands.** Every run used the README's command, with downstream GLUE eval switched off (it isn't needed for Table 1):

  - Results were summarized with the repo's `experiments/summarize.py` through `scripts/collect.py`:
    - CounterFact: `post_{rewrite,paraphrase,neighborhood}_success`, `post_ngram_entropy`, `post_reference_score`
    - ZsRE: `post_{rewrite,paraphrase,neighborhood}_acc`
  - Wrappers: `scripts/launch.sh`, plus `scripts/sched.sh`, which runs jobs one at a time. Running 5 jobs concurrently on the single GPU slowed latency-bound evaluation about 17×.

  - CounterFact, ZsRE, attribute snippets and TF-IDF files: downloaded from the URLs the code points to (memit.baulab.info).

### Settings that differ from the paper
1. **LLaMA3 covariance statistics.** The code's dataset (`wikipedia` 20200501.en, built with Apache Beam, hard-coded cache dir) can no longer be built. We used 100k random articles from 6 of the 41 parquet shards of `wikimedia/wikipedia` 20231101.en (937,729 articles). Everything else matches the release: same seed (`random_sample=1`), max length, float32 and tally logic. They were computed in one pass over all 5 layers by `scripts/llama_stats_multilayer.py`, which reuses the repo's `TokenizedDataset`, `length_collation`, `make_loader` and `CombinedStat`. This may shift LLaMA3 results slightly for both CAKE and AlphaEdit.
2. **Instance selection.** The paper says it "randomly samples 2,000 editing instances". The code takes the **first** 2000 records (`size=2000` slicing, no seed). We used the code's behaviour.
3. **Runs.** The paper reports mean ± std, apparently over several runs. The code has no seed or multi-run handling, and context templates are sampled without a seed, so we ran one run per cell.
4. **Hardware.** We used an H200 instead of an A100, in full fp32 (no TF32), like the original.

### Causal-score check (LLaMA3-8B-Instruct)
The release has per-case tracing scripts (`experiments/causal_trace_llama3.py`) but not the step that aggregates them into the `causal_scores` stored in the hparams. We wrote that step ourselves (`scripts/causal_scores_llama.py`), reusing the release's own tracing functions. Settings: `rome.causal_trace.calculate_hidden_flow`, kind=mlp, noise=0.02, layers 4–8, last subject token, known_1000 facts with correct predictions (n=862).

| Layer | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shipped scores | 0.4812 | 0.4744 | 0.4656 | 0.4441 | 0.4335 |
| Our mean restored prob. | 0.4972 | 0.4922 | 0.4839 | 0.4647 | 0.4531 |
| Our AIE (restored − corrupted) | 0.4160 | 0.4109 | 0.4026 | 0.3834 | 0.3719 |

The ordering and the gaps between layers are reproduced. The softmax weights don't change when every score shifts by the same constant, so our scores would give essentially the same layer weights. The shipped values correspond to the mean *restored probability*, not to IE = P_restore − P_corrupt as defined in App. E, Eq. 67. Because of the shift invariance, this doesn't matter.

## Why it is not fully reproduced
- **CAKE's numbers are below the paper's** on 23 of 24 Table 1 cells (the 24th, GPT2-XL ZsRE Spe, is equal). Several gaps far exceed the paper's ± values: LLaMA3 CF Gen −9.0, GPT2-XL CF Flu −23.7, GPT2-XL CF Spe −5.5, LLaMA3 ZsRE Gen −4.1.
- **The baselines are not the cause.** Our AlphaEdit, run through the same pipeline, matches the paper's AlphaEdit row: |Δ| ≤ 1.51 everywhere except GPT2-XL CF Flu (−4.84). So the setup (data, stats, metrics) isn't what's wrong.
- **What we didn't find.** No bug or config difference explains the shortfall. The only extra ingredient we found in the release is the undisclosed L2 change, and the ablation shows it accounts for most of the GPT2-XL gain.
- **Possible contributing factors:**
  - Run-to-run variance from unseeded context-template sampling (we ran one run per cell).
  - The paper's "random 2000 instances" vs the code's first 2000.
  - For LLaMA3, our substitute covariance statistics. These affect AlphaEdit equally, and our AlphaEdit LLaMA3 numbers match the paper.

None of these plausibly explains a +11.7-point Gen gap on LLaMA3 CounterFact. We see +1.1.

## Discrepancies
1. **code_vs_paper, minor, affects results.** `hparams/Cake/gpt2-xl.json` uses `L2=40` and `hparams/Cake/EleutherAI_gpt-j-6B.json` uses `L2=30`. The AlphaEdit configs for the same models use `L2=10`. The paper presents CAKE as AlphaEdit with only the layer weighting and residual allocation changed, "using the official AlphaEdit implementations … to maintain consistency", and never mentions a different regularizer. Our ablation shows that most of CAKE's GPT2-XL gain over AlphaEdit comes from this.
2. **code_vs_paper, minor, affects results.** The paper samples 2000 editing instances at random (Sec. 5.2). The code takes the first 2000 records, with no seed or sampling.
3. **code_vs_paper, minor, no effect on results.** App. E defines the score as AIE = mean(P_restore − P_corrupt). The shipped `causal_scores` match the mean restored probability instead. This is invariant under the softmax.
4. **paper_internal, affects results.** For LLaMA3 the CAKE and AlphaEdit configs are identical apart from the weighting. The τ=100 setting (weights uniform to within 0.05%) should therefore equal the uniform "Dense (MEMIT-like)" ablation and also AlphaEdit, but the paper reports three different numbers for it on LLaMA3 ZsRE:
   - Table 6, τ=100: Eff 96.08, Gen 91.11, Spe 32.44
   - Table 2, uniform "Dense (MEMIT-like)" ablation: 94.24, 89.36, 32.68
   - Table 1, AlphaEdit: 94.38, 88.19, 32.72
5. **paper_internal, affects results.** Table 1 reports ±std (for example ±0.04 on CAKE LLaMA3 CF Eff), but the paper never says how many runs or seeds produced it. The released pipeline has no seed or multi-run support.
