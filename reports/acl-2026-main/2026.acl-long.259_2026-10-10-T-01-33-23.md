# Reproduction report: Demystifying Uncertainty in LLMs: Active Calibration between Concepts and Human Evaluations (2026.acl-long.259)

## Verdict
- **Verdict: PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED**
- **Result match (after fixes/reconstruction): MIXED.** As released: **DOES_NOT_RUN**
- **Coverage: SUBSET.** We reran Figure 4 (main ILS results), but not Figure 3 (AR-Bench) or Table 1 (TruthfulQA/ToolBench), because the release has no code for them.
- **Codebase completeness: MISSING_CORE.** The prompt module is absent and both LLM wrappers are stubs.
- Flags: `BASELINE_STRONGER_THAN_REPORTED`
- Wall-clock: about 1 h 50 min (01:33 to 03:23, 2026-10-10). The 36 runs together took about 75 min on one H200.

**Summary.** The repository (`zhouyeah215/Demystifying_Uncertainty`, a single commit) contains:
- the ILS selection logic (`estimate_cal_for_question`, the JS divergence between the PolicyLLM's simulated user answers and the ResponseLLM's "true user" answers);
- the metric code (semantic-cluster epistemic entropy, aleatoric error mass, and the diagnostic Cal);
- the two datasets (AmbigQA with 50 queries; ambiguous HotpotQA with 51).

It cannot run as released, for three reasons:
- `src/prompt/prompt_builder.py` is missing, so the code fails with an ImportError.
- `PolicyLLM` and `ResponseLLM` are `@ray.remote` stubs with only `__init__`.
- `main()` and the YAML configs contain only placeholders (`"data_path"`, `"model_path"`, all hyperparameters `None`).

I rebuilt the missing pieces:
- the prompt builder, verbatim from the paper's Appendix G prompts (Tables 9–15);
- vLLM-backed wrappers;
- a parallel runner.

I then reran Figure 4 three times per setting.

**What holds and what doesn't:**
- **AmbigQA:** ILS gives the lowest mean calibration error at turns 4–10 for both policy models (and at turn 2 for Qwen). The margin is much smaller than in the paper: at turn 10, Cal is 0.41 vs 0.43–0.44 for Qwen, against 0.27 vs 0.44–0.49 in the paper.
- **HotpotQA:** ILS beats SelfSelect, but the Random baseline matches or beats ILS. Our Random reaches 0.25 (Qwen) and 0.24 (LLaMA) at turn 10; the paper reports 0.36–0.37.
- **Epistemic uncertainty:** much higher than in the paper for every strategy, with no consistent ILS advantage.

## What was run
Environment:
- conda env at `./env`, Python 3.11, vllm 0.31.0, torch 2.13 (cu130);
- `VLLM_USE_FLASHINFER_SAMPLER=0`, because FlashInfer's JIT needs nvcc, which is not on the node.

Servers:
- Two vLLM OpenAI-compatible servers on one GPU: `Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct` (port 8001) and `meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3-8B-Instruct` (port 8002).
- bf16, `--generation-config vllm` (so the model's own generation_config doesn't override the sampling settings).

Command, for each policy ∈ {qwen (M=10), llama (M=15)}, dataset ∈ {ambigQA, hotpotqa}, strategy ∈ {random, self_select, ils_cal}, seed ∈ {0,1,2}:
```
env/bin/python -I scripts/run_ils.py --data codebase/datasets/<ds>.json --out runs[_s<seed>]/<policy>_<ds>_<strategy>.json \
   --policy_cfg configs/<policy>.yaml --response_cfg configs/qwen.yaml --strategy <strategy> --M <M> --turns 10 --seed <seed>
env/bin/python scripts/aggregate.py runs runs_s1 runs_s2   # -> results_summary.json, logs/aggregate_3seeds.txt
env/bin/python scripts/compare.py                          # -> comparisons.json, logs/compare_table.md
```

Other notes:
- vLLM sampling is unseeded; the seed only fixes Random's choice of question.
- **Not implemented, as in the release:** the β(t) curriculum schedule of Algorithm 1. ILS differs from the baselines only in its Cal-based choice of question.
- **Cal definition:** the figure-comparison Cal uses the released code's definition (reference = softmax(2·level)). Cal under the paper's text definition (reference = level/10) is in `results_summary.json` as `cal_paperref`. Under that definition, ILS's advantage shrinks further: at turn 10 it is within about ±0.02 of the baselines everywhere except on HotpotQA vs SelfSelect (ILS lower by 0.03 for Qwen and 0.04 for LLaMA).

### Interpretation
- **Interaction lowers Cal and Ale for every strategy** (e.g. Qwen-AmbigQA Random: 0.79 → 0.43). This reproduces, and baseline magnitudes at early turns are close to the paper's.
- **ILS faster than the baselines on AmbigQA:** holds for Cal at turns 4–10 for both models (and Ale at turns 4–8), with a smaller gap than reported. At turn 10 on Qwen it is within noise (0.407±0.017 vs 0.430±0.036 / 0.441±0.009).
- **ILS faster than the baselines on HotpotQA:** does not hold against Random. Random reaches lower Cal and Ale at turns 8–10 for Qwen and ties for LLaMA. Our Random is much stronger than the paper's, hence `BASELINE_STRONGER_THAN_REPORTED`.
- **Epistemic uncertainty:** ours is 0.41–0.68 at turn 10, against the paper's 0.25–0.51. ILS shows no consistent advantage (it is lowest only for LLaMA-AmbigQA). Our semantic clustering of free-form answers (by Qwen2.5-7B with the Table 13 prompt) splits answers more finely than the paper's apparently did.
- **"SelfSelect occasionally underperforms Random":** reproduced, clearly on HotpotQA.
- **Valid-answer rate (Fig. 5, not compared numerically):** ILS is highest on AmbigQA at most turns, but not on Qwen-HotpotQA at turns 8–10, where Random is highest.

**Caveats on comparability:**
- The prompt construction (history formatting, candidate parsing, self-select parsing) and all unreported sampling hyperparameters are my reconstruction.
- The β(t) schedule is absent in both the release and our run.
- Paper values come from a bar chart.

Because of these, a gap of a few hundredths may come from the setup rather than the method. The qualitative failures, however, are consistent across 3 runs: no ILS advantage over Random on HotpotQA, and no epistemic advantage.

## Why not fully reproduced
1. **Release incomplete (MISSING_CORE).** The prompt module is missing, the LLM interface is a stub, there is no entry-point configuration, and the β(t) curriculum is not implemented. As released, nothing runs.
2. **Hyperparameters missing.** The paper doesn't give the sampling temperatures, K_p, smoothing, or token limits.
3. **Results differ.** With the reconstruction, the ILS advantage appears only on AmbigQA. On HotpotQA, Random is as good as or better than ILS.
4. **Not run: Figure 3 (AR-Bench DC/SP) and Table 1 (TruthfulQA/ToolBench).** There is no code or data for them in the release. Fig. 3 also uses GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 as policies.

## Discrepancies
**Code vs paper:**
1. **Major. Diagnostic Cal reference distribution.** The paper (App. G.3) uses p(level) = level/10. The code (`compute_cal_over_score_bins`, beta=2.0 in main.py) uses softmax(2·level). This affects the results; see `cal_paperref`.
2. **Major. Aleatoric score.** The paper has s_j = level/10; the code uses level/4. The figure appears to use the code's version (see paper-internal item 3).
3. **Major. β(t) curriculum missing.** Algorithm 1, Sec 5.2, and App. B.3 sample candidates from q_t ∝ μ^β(t). The release has no scheduling: all strategies use one LLM call that produces M questions.
4. **Minor. Crash and stubs.** `run_single_sample` passes `None` arguments (crash), and the LLM wrappers are stubs.

**Paper-internal:**
1. **K is inconsistent.** Sec 4 says K=8 samples; App. G.3 says K=5 answers and K=5 simulated user answers.
2. **Identical bars across models in Figure 4.** The ILS AmbigQA Cal bars for Qwen2.5-7B (top) and LLaMA3-8B (bottom) are identical at all five turns (0.64/0.51/0.43/0.38/0.27). The Ale bars are identical at 4 of 5 turns. The Epi bars differ. Two different policy models are unlikely to produce identical numbers, which suggests a plotting or copy error.
3. **Ale definition vs reported values.** The App. G.3 definition (s_j = level/10) implies Ale ≥ 0.6, but Figure 4 reports Ale values down to about 0.14.
