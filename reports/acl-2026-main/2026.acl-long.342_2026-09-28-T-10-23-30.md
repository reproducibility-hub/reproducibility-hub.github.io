# Reproduction report: Parity-Aware BPE (ACL 2026, 2026.acl-long.342)

## Verdict
- **Verdict: PARTIALLY_REPRODUCED**
- **Result match: TREND_MATCH.** The central claim reproduces almost exactly. Parity-aware BPE sharply lowers the Gini token-inequality coefficient on FLORES (PA-BPE: 0.0565 -> 0.0061 = **-89%**; paper: 0.064 -> 0.007 = -89%), while global compression, Rényi entropy and the other intrinsic metrics stay close to Classical BPE. The ranking of Gini across all dev-set variants also matches (PA < window < hybrid < hybrid+window << BPE). The absolute values are off in a few places: fertility is ~7-10% higher than the paper, Rényi efficiency ~6% lower, and MorphScore ~0.1-0.18 lower in absolute terms. The ratios variant is *more* equitable than in the paper (Gini 0.017 vs 0.040).
- **Coverage: SCALED_DOWN.**
  - Only the intrinsic Table 1 experiment (all 6 tokenizers) was run.
  - The tokenizer training corpus is smaller than the paper's. The paper does not give its size; see "Differences" below.
  - The extrinsic 3B-parameter LM experiments (Table 2, Fig. 5: 3B LLaMA models on 100B tokens, 256 GPUs x 18 h each) are far beyond the 1-GPU / 48 h budget and were **not run**.
- **Codebase completeness: MISSING_SCRIPTS.** The released BPE-learning code (Classical + all Parity-aware variants) and the HF-tokenizer builder ran **unmodified**. The data-construction pipeline (FineWeb2 sampling, dev/ratio inputs) and the evaluation driver are not in the release, so I wrote them. The authors' separate TokEval suite (legacy version) was used as is.

## What the paper claims / what I targeted
Table 1 covers 128k tokenizers trained on the unbalanced 30-language FineWeb2 mix. They are evaluated on FLORES+ devtest for compression rate, TTR, vocabulary utilization, fertility, Rényi (alpha=2.5), Gini, and MorphScore P/R. The rows are Classical BPE, PA-BPE, PA-BPE (window), PA-BPE (hybrid), PA-BPE (hybrid+window) and PA-BPE (ratios). The abstract's headline "Gini reduced by up to 89% with negligible impact on global compression" rests on this table.

## What I did (exact pipeline)
1. **Code:** `codebase/` = github.com/swiss-ai/parity-aware-bpe @ c7395d4, no edits. **Evaluation:** `tokeval_legacy/` = github.com/cimeister/tokenizer-analysis-suite at tag `legacy-suite-final` (the suite the paper cites; the current master changed metric definitions), plus its `morphscore` submodule, with MorphScore data from HF `catherinearnett/morphscore`.
2. **Environment:** conda env `env/`, Python 3.11, `pip install -e codebase transformers datasets pyarrow huggingface_hub`, `pip install -e tokeval_legacy tokeval_legacy/morphscore`.
3. **Dev/eval data:** `scripts/get_flores.py` writes FLORES-200 dev (997 lines) and devtest (1012 lines) for the 30 languages of Table 9.
4. **Training data:** `scripts/prep_train.py --unit 20000` streams FineWeb2 (`HuggingFaceFW/fineweb-2`, train split, first shards) for 29 languages, and FineWeb `sample-10BT` for English (FineWeb2 has no English).
   - Per-language document counts follow temperature sampling (tau=3.3) over mC4 per-language sizes, as in paper Sec. 5.1. The mC4 token counts were transcribed approximately from the mT5 paper.
   - `scripts/subset.py` takes the first 1/10 (u2k) and 1/40 (u500) of each language's text *lines* from a 2.18M-document temperature-sampled pool (FineWeb documents contain newlines, so the cut is by lines, i.e. roughly 1/10 and 1/40 of the documents), keeping the same proportions:
     - **u2k:** ~0.22M docs / 4.35M lines (English ~22k docs ... Telugu ~2.2k docs), 7.08M word types, 128M pre-tokenized words.
     - **u500:** ~55k docs / 1.09M lines, 2.67M word types, 32M words.
5. `scripts/make_dicts.py` pre-computes word-count dictionaries using the code's own pre-tokenizer (Whitespace + ByteLevel(use_regex=False)). The runs then use the code's `--dict-input` mode, which gives exactly the same counts as the text-input mode and is much faster to load. `scripts/aux_inputs.py` writes:
   - the all-language merged dictionary (for `learn_bpe.py`, which takes one input);
   - the ratio targets for the ratios variant: FLORES dev bytes per line per language, as recommended in Sec. 4.3.
6. **Training** (`scripts/train_all.sh`, plus the u2k runs launched individually with identical arguments): 127,744 merges (= 128,000 vocab - 256 bytes). Hybrid = 63,872 global merges + the rest parity-aware (J=K=|m|/2). Window: W=100, alpha=2. Commands:
   - `learn_bpe.py --dict-input --symbols 127744 --input ALL_merged.dict`
   - `parity_aware_learn_bpe.py --dict-input --symbols 127744 --variant {base|window} [--window-size 100 --alpha 2] [--global-merges 63872] --input <30 train dicts> --dev <30 FLORES-dev dicts>`
   - ratios: `... --variant base --ratio <30 floats>` (no dev set)
7. **Evaluation** (`scripts/eval_run.sh`):
   - `HF_tokenizer.py` builds vocab.json/merges.txt.
   - `tokeval_legacy/scripts/run_tokenizer_analysis.py` runs on FLORES devtest with `class: custom_bpe` in three passes:
     - `lines` measurement: compression rate, Gini, TTR, vocab utilization, Rényi, plus MorphScore (defaults: freq_scale=True, exclude_single_tok=False, by_split=False);
     - whitespace-word measurement: fertility;
     - HF-whitespace-word measurement: alternative fertility.
   - `scripts/make_table.py`, `scripts/compare.py` and `scripts/extra_stats.py` collect the tables.
   - Outputs: `runs/u2k/` and `runs/u500/` (table.json, comparisons.json, compare.md, eval_*/analysis_results.json, merges, logs).

Additional qualitative checks (`scripts/extra_stats.py`, u2k):
- **Fig. 2 (per-language compression).** The max/min per-language tokens-per-line ratio is 1.54 for BPE (worst: Georgian, 47.7 tok/line; best: English, 31.1). It falls to 1.10 (PA), 1.12 (window), 1.20 (hybrid), 1.32 (hybrid+window) and 1.19 (ratios), so compression is much more uniform.

- **Utilization and TTR at u2k.** Dev-set PA variants have slightly higher utilization/TTR than BPE, as in the paper. At u500, PA-base was slightly lower on these two, so this minor trend is scale-sensitive.

## Differences from the paper's setup (all reductions/substitutions)
- **Training data size.** Not reported in the paper; its resource tiers imply >1M documents for high-resource languages, i.e. roughly 20-30M documents in total. The released learner is pure Python (~2-8 s per merge early on at 7M word types; each u2k PA run needed 7-8 h and ~26 GB RAM). So I used ~0.22M docs (u2k) and ~55k docs (u500), i.e. ~1% and ~0.25% of the likely paper scale, with the paper's tau=3.3 proportions.
  - Going from u500 to u2k changed the results very little (Gini within 0.002, CR within 0.0005), which suggests limited sensitivity to data scale. It does not prove that the paper's scale would give identical numbers.
- **mC4 proportions.** The mC4 sizes used for temperature sampling are approximate transcriptions of mT5-paper token counts, not the authors' exact per-language parameters. English comes from FineWeb (not in FineWeb2). Documents are the first N of each language's first FineWeb2 shards (not random), and Persian uses FineWeb2 `fas_Arab`.
- **FLORES.** FLORES+ on HF (`openlanguagedata/flores_plus`) is gated and our HF account is not authorized. I used the public FLORES-200 release (dl.fbaipublicfiles.com), which has the same dev (997) / devtest (1012) sentences, without FLORES+'s later corrections. Filipino = FLORES-200 `tgl_Latn`; Chinese = `zho_Hans`.
- **Metric conventions I had to infer:**
  - **CR** = mean over lines of lines/token under the `lines` measurement (it matches the paper's magnitude: BPE 0.0288 vs 0.0275).
  - **Fertility.** The paper says HF-Whitespace words, but the HF-whitespace config gives 2.31 for BPE, far from the paper's 3.99. Plain whitespace splitting gives 4.26, so I report that as primary and HF-whitespace in `table.json` (`fertility_hfws`).
  - **Rényi efficiency** = H_2.5 / log2(#observed token types). This is the legacy suite's normalization according to the TokEval CHANGELOG; the legacy suite itself only outputs raw entropy.
  - **MorphScore** = unweighted macro-average of `morphscore_precision` / `morphscore_recall` over the 27 languages with data (Persian, Telugu and Vietnamese yield no samples). The absolute MorphScore values are ~0.1-0.17 below the paper's for every tokenizer, including the baseline. This probably reflects a different MorphScore version, dataset or setting than the authors used, not the tokenizers. Differences *between* tokenizers are small (<=0.03) in both the paper and ours.
- **Not run:** the UnigramLM row (not in Table 1), the mC4 / 60-lang / balanced / 256k ablations (Tables 3-8), and the extrinsic LM training and benchmarks (Table 2, Fig. 5). The LM runs need ~4,600 GPU-hours per model.

- **Observations about the release:**
  1. The learner is pure-Python subword-nmt style and very slow and memory-hungry at the paper's likely data scale. There is no documented data-size or runtime information.
  2. The legacy TokEval custom_bpe loader drops merges.txt lines starting with "#", so one merge (e.g. a merge of the "#" token) is silently dropped in our u2k tokenizers (127,743 of 127,744 used). The effect is negligible.
  3. The legacy suite's custom_bpe post-processor adds <s> ... </s> to every line, so the token counts include 2 special tokens per line. That is presumably the same in the paper.
