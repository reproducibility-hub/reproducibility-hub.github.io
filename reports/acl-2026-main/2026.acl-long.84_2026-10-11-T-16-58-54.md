## 1. What the paper claims and what was targeted
The paper compares humans and 7 MLMs on a 4-way multiple-choice production task across three word types: Vocabulary, Possessives and Demonstratives. Each MLM task has 6144 image+prompt trials. The claims tested here:
1. **Fig. 2**: a difficulty hierarchy Vocabulary > Possessive > Demonstrative for most models. Models are near human level on vocabulary and far below humans on perspectival words.
2. **Fig. 3**: ablations with four cue-removal conditions (Perspectival-Linguistic, Spatial-Linguistic, Spatial-Visual, Spatial-Visual+Linguistic).
3. **Fig. 4A**: instruction-based prompting brings possessives close to human level but does not help demonstratives.

Per the user's note, only the open-weight models were run: **Gemma-3-27B-it, InternVL2.5-4B-MPO, InternVL2.5-8B-MPO, Ovis2-4B, Ovis2-8B**. GPT-4o and Llama-4-Scout (Together API) were not run. The paper does not report MiniCPM numbers, and its backend file is not in the release, so MiniCPM was not evaluated. Human data is not part of the code and was not re-collected; the human numbers come from Table 3.

The paper's numbers appear only as bar charts. I read them by measuring bar heights in the PDF's embedded bitmaps, calibrated against the values the paper states in text (human 99.84/93.44/83.0, GPT-4o demonstratives 70.2, Llama possessives 85.7). The calibration reproduces those stated values to within 0.3 points. Assume about ±0.5 points of reading error (`scripts/paper_numbers.json`).

## 2. What was run
* **Environment**: conda env (python 3.11), torch 2.6.0+cu124, transformers 4.51.3, flash-attn 2.7.4.post1, **sentencepiece 0.2.0** (0.2.2 cannot load InternLM2's tokenizer), accelerate, pandas, openai, timm, einops. The repo has no requirements file.
* **Stimuli**: the generated images are not released, so I regenerated them with the released `material/revised_dataset_generation.py`. A wrapper, `scripts/gen_stimuli.py`, seeds Python's RNG with 42 and also builds `pronoun_allsquare`. This produced 7 sets × 6144 images: vocabulary, pronoun, demonstrative, demonstrative_deprompt, demonstrative_allsquare, pronoun_allsquare and pronoun_nosquare. Trial counts and factor crossings match App. D.
* **Inference**: the released test scripts, unchanged apart from the fixes in §5, run from `codebase/test` with `PYTHONPATH=codebase`. For each model, `scripts/lane.sh MODEL` runs:
  * `vocabulary.py`, `pronoun.py` and `demonstrative.py` with `--exp exp_main --material exp_main` (Fig. 2, all 6144 trials, seed 42, greedy decoding, default dtype bf16);
  * for Gemma-3 only, `pronoun.py` and `demonstrative.py` with `--exp exp_instruction_learning --material exp_main` (Fig. 4A, 6144 trials);
  * `ablation.py` with `--exp exp_pronoun_nopers|exp_demonstrative_nopers` (Perspectival-Ling), `exp_pronoun_deprompt|exp_demonstrative_noproximity` (Spatial-Ling), `exp_pronoun_allsquare|exp_demonstrative_allsquare` (Spatial-Visual) and `exp_pronoun_nosquare|exp_demonstrative_deprompt` (Spatial-Vis+Ling). Each runs on the matching material with a `_q` suffix.
  * The five model lanes ran concurrently on one H200.
* **Scaled down**: the Fig. 3 ablations use a seeded random subset of **1536 of the 6144 trials** (`scripts/make_subsets.py`, seed 0). The same trial IDs are used in every condition of a task. Running all 8 ablations × 5 models at 6144 trials (≈1–2.6 s/trial under GPU sharing) would not have fit the 30-hour budget. To compare each ablation with an "original" on the same trials, I also report the full main-run accuracy restricted to those 1536 trials (`*_main_q`). It is within 1.5 points of the full-set number for every model.
* **Scoring**: the paper scores answers with GPT-4o as an LLM judge (`answer_eval_sentence`, "do these two strings mean the same thing"). No OpenAI key was available, so this was replaced by **normalised exact match** (lowercase, no punctuation) of the model output against the ground-truth sentence. `scripts/score.py` also computes a lenient score, which counts an answer as correct if it contains exactly one of the 4 options (word-boundary match) and that option is correct. **Strict and lenient accuracy are identical for every one of the 57 runs**: almost every answer was a verbatim option, at most with a trailing period. The rare non-option outputs (<2%, e.g. an object name) contain no option at all. The judge only decides equivalence of short option strings, so on these outputs it could only differ through its own errors. I therefore treat the numbers as comparable and still set the flag.
* Not run: GPT-4o and Llama-4-Scout (closed or API-only, per the user's note); in-context learning for Fig. 4 (the exemplar images and desc under `material/in_context_examples/` are not released); the human experiments; Appendix sanity checks, prompt variants and Table 9 baselines.

Small models change by at most 2.2 points, except Ovis2-8B demonstratives at +4.9 (paper +5.6). ✔ in direction; the small-model possessive levels are 10–18 points below the paper's.

  * Possessives fall to chance (≈24–25%) for every model, against 37–41% in the paper. This follows from the released prompt for this condition, which also drops who is gazing at what (discrepancy D1).
  * Demonstratives match the paper for all 5 models, to within 0.8 points.
* Demonstrative ablations reproduce numerically for most cells: 17 of 20 within tolerance.

## 4. Why not fully reproduced
* **Closed and API models not run**: GPT-4o and Llama-4-Scout. Their bars carry the paper's "best model vs human" numbers (85.7% possessives, 70.2% demonstratives).
* **Scaled-down ablations**: 1536 of 6144 trials (binomial SE ≈1.2 points), because of the time budget.
* **Unexplained small-model possessive gap of 8–12 points.**
  * Ruled out: same code, same prompts, temperature 0, scoring (strict = lenient), and option-position bias.
  * Possible causes I could not test: different upstream model revisions or remote code (Ovis2/InternVL repositories have been updated since), different transformers versions, or the GPT-4o judge accepting non-identical answers.
* **In-context learning** exemplars are not released.
