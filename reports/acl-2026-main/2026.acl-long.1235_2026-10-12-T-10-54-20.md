**In one line:** the uncompressed baseline reproduces Table 1 (11 of 13 tasks within tolerance), but the released DASH does not:

- **Accuracy.** On LongBench-E it scores **37.89 vs 46.76** in the paper (vanilla 53.07). Excluding the two summarization tasks, where the paper's baseline itself does not match, it is **39.89 vs 54.04** (vanilla 57.19 vs 56.52).
- **Versus random halting.** On our 8-task subset it does *worse* than random halting at the same budget.
- **LooGLE cloze.** DASH collapses from 60.0 to 35.5 exact match. The paper reports DASH ≥ vanilla.
- **Speed.** The measured prefill speedup is **1.14×**, not 1.74×. End-to-end it is 0.93×, i.e. slower.

The released DeltaPress uses a different halting signal than the paper describes. It also does not skip the FFN for halted tokens.

## What the paper claims (main results targeted)

The model is Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M. Token halting starts at layer l_s = ⌊0.4L⌋ = 11 with ρ = 0.667 (about 40% effective token reduction).

- **Table 1** (LongBench-E, 13 tasks): DASH avg 46.76 vs vanilla 48.87, the best of all compressors.
- **Table 3** (LooGLE): DASH "Avg" 19.94 vs vanilla 22.69, and the best cloze score (73.6 / 83.1).
- **Table 2 / Table 6 / Fig. 6** (efficiency): FlashAttention prefill speedup 1.74× on LongBench-E. Table 6 gives per-category prefill speedups of 1.72–1.89× and end-to-end 1.28–1.56×.
- **Not run:**
  - The VL results (Fig. 5, Table 7 on Qwen2-VL-7B). No VL evaluation pipeline is released; `presses/mllm.py` is only a press, and `EffiVLM-Bench-main.zip` is a third-party benchmark with no DASH integration or configs.
  - The other text backbones (Tables 17/18).
  - The baselines FastV, SnapKV(pr.), D3 and LLMLingua2, and SlimInfer/LazyLLM. None of their implementations or configs are in the release, so the "beats X" claims are NOT_COMPARABLE.

## What I ran

### Code
- Repo `https://github.com/verach3n/DASH` (commit 831cb45), cloned into `./codebase`. It contains no edits from me.
- NVIDIA kvpress was cloned into `./kvpress` and checked out at **v0.3.0**. The DASH `base_press.py` is a modification of exactly this version; v0.3.0 has the smallest diff to it.
- I followed the README integration steps:
  - copied `presses/delta_press.py` into kvpress;
  - registered `DeltaPress` in `kvpress/__init__.py` and in `evaluation/evaluate_registry.py` as `"delta_press": DeltaPress()`;
  - replaced `kvpress/presses/base_press.py` with the DASH version (README §3.3).

### Environment
- Conda env with Python 3.11, torch 2.7.1+cu126, transformers 4.56.2 (kvpress v0.3.0 requires ≥4.56), datasets 2.21, pandas 2.3.3, flash-attn 2.8.3.
- kvpress's `evaluate.py` automatically uses `flash_attention_2` when flash-attn is installed, matching the paper's FlashAttention setting.
- Hardware: 1×H200. The GPU was shared with another user's job for most of the day.

### Commands
These are the README's "Key configuration" (`dataset=longbench`, the 13 `*_e` dirs, `press=delta_press`, `compression_ratios=0.667`) and `no_press` for vanilla, looped by `scripts/run_longbench_e.sh` / `scripts/run_tasks.sh`.

```
cd kvpress/evaluation
python evaluate.py --dataset longbench --data_dir <task>_e --model Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M \
   --press_name {no_press|delta_press} --compression_ratio {0.0|0.667} --device cuda:0
python evaluate.py --dataset loogle --data_dir {shortdep_cloze,shortdep_qa,longdep_qa} ...   # same presses
python evaluate.py --dataset loogle_local --data_dir longdep_summarization_part{0..3} ...    # LooGLE summ. in 4 equal chunks
python scripts/time_prefill.py --per_task 8          # prefill latency, quiet GPU (results_timing/timing_quiet.*)
python scripts/time_prefill.py --per_task 3 --e2e    # + end-to-end latency (results_timing/timing_e2e.*)
python scripts/aggregate.py                          # -> results_summary.json
```

### Settings and how they relate to the paper
- **Same as the paper:** model; l_s = 11 (start_ratio 0.4); ρ = 0.667; keep_first_n = 64 and keep_last_n = 32 (the code defaults, also cited in Table 5); greedy decoding; kvpress default `max_new_tokens` per task.
- **Unknown:** the paper does not say which `max_new_tokens` / question handling it used.
- **LongBench-E data source.** kvpress's processed dataset `Xnhyacinth/LongBench` no longer exists on the HF Hub (it returns "Invalid username or password"). I rebuilt the `*_e` configs locally from the official `zai-org/LongBench` `data.zip`. I used the templates and processing of kvpress's own `evaluation/benchmarks/longbench/create_huggingface_dataset.py` (`scripts/build_longbench_e.py`); only the source (local jsonl) and the sink (local parquet) changed.
- **LooGLE data source.** `simonjegou/loogle` (the kvpress registry default). `longdep_summarization` was split into 4 equal row chunks of 129 so that out-of-memory failures on the shared GPU only lose one chunk. The summarization metrics are means over rows, so the mean of the 4 equal chunks equals the full-set value. All 4 chunks were completed for both configs.

### Reproduction-only additions (not part of the release)
- `kvpress/kvpress/presses/delta_variants.py` holds diagnostic variants that only swap the ranking signal at l_s:
  - `DeltaNormPress`: the paper's Eq. 2, Δ = ‖U^(l_s)‖₂, halting the lowest;
  - `DeltaNormHighPress`;
  - `DeltaRandomPress`.
- `scripts/` holds the runners, the dataset rebuild, the timing script and the aggregation script.

## Why it is not reproduced

The code runs and the vanilla pipeline reproduces the paper's baseline. The method's numbers do not reproduce. The causes found:

1. **Halting signal differs from the paper (code_vs_paper, major).**
   - Paper (Eq. 2, Alg. 1): Δ_t = ‖U_t^(l_s)‖₂, and tokens with low Δ are halted.
   - Released `DeltaPress`: `stability = 1 − cos(U^(l_s), U^(l_s−1))` per token, where U is the pre-residual attention output. Its `metric="cos"` default compares the attention outputs of layers 11 and 10. Tokens whose attention-output *direction* changed least are halted.
   - Running the paper's ‖U‖₂ rule ourselves (supplementary) also does not reach the paper's numbers. The mismatch therefore cannot be fully explained by this difference.
2. **FFN is not skipped (code_vs_paper, major for efficiency).**
   - The paper says halted tokens skip "both self-attention and FFN".
   - In the released code the hooks are on `layer.self_attn` only. The MLP runs on the full sequence in every layer. Halted tokens also receive `h + LN(h)` in place of an identity update (the post-hook fills skipped positions with the attention *input*). This does not affect outputs, because halted tokens never re-enter attention, but it removes the FFN FLOP savings.
   - This, together with per-layer `.item()` syncs and `print`s in the hooks (including at every decode step), explains the 1.14× prefill and 0.93× end-to-end speedups.
3. **Evaluation settings for summarization and LooGLE are not specified, and the paper's baselines don't match kvpress defaults.**
   - LongBench-E gov_report / multi_news: 6.97 / 6.57 in the paper vs 36.01 / 24.80 for us.
   - LooGLE: RL 0.04–0.23 in the paper vs 0.32–0.67 for us, and cloze 73.5 vs 60.0.
   - The paper presumably used a different generation length or scorer that the release does not document.

## Other notes

- **Why BASELINE_STRONGER_THAN_REPORTED is set.** Vanilla run through the released pipeline scores 36.01 / 24.80 on gov_report / multi_news (paper 6.97 / 6.57), and much higher LooGLE ROUGE-L. On the stronger baseline, DASH's quality loss is larger in absolute terms (gov_report −5.0, multi_news −2.0; the paper has ≈0).

- **Shared GPU.** The GPU was shared with another user's job for most of the run. Several of my runs hit CUDA OOM on 100k–287k-token LooGLE documents or during parallel runs. Every failed task or chunk was re-run to completion; no partial result is used.
- **Duplicate run.** A duplicate random-halting chain I started by mistake was killed (my own PIDs).
- **Wall-clock.** About 10.7 h (10:54 → 21:35 CDT). Most of it was LongBench-E summarization and LooGLE generation on the shared GPU.
