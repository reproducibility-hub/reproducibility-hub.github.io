Table-based QA (the MLLM extracts the table, then Qwen2.5-7B answers from the table alone) lifts misleading-chart accuracy by +17.2 / +20.7 / +20.7 pp (paper +19.6 / +15.4 / +20.3) for Qwen2VL-7B / Ovis1.6-9B / InternVL2.5-8B. All three gains are significant, and non-misleading accuracy drops, as in the paper. The Exp.1 vulnerability finding also reproduces: every model scores lower on misleading than on non-misleading charts. Exp.1 accuracies match the paper's CHARTOM-free values to within 0–3 questions.

Some secondary claims hold only partly:
- Redrawing helps significantly for Ovis1.6-9B (+16.1 vs +14.7 pp). For Qwen2VL-7B it gives only +4.6 pp, not significant (paper: +9.8 pp, significant).
- The table-based QA cost on non-misleading charts is in the same direction (−4.4 / −8.8 / −10.3 pp) but not significant on our 68 non-misleading instances.
- The misleader warning gives Ovis1.6-9B a significant +9.2 pp (paper: +4.9 pp, and no warning gain is significant).

All of this was measured on a subset about 60% the size of the paper's datasets (CHARTOM missing), so the significance tests have less power.

## What the paper claims / what was targeted

- Exp.1 (Fig.4, Table 3): MLLM QA accuracy on misleading charts (CALVI 45 + CHARTOM 56 + real-world 42 = 143) is much lower than on non-misleading charts (CALVI 15 + CHARTOM 56 + VLAT 53 = 124).
- Exp.3 (Fig.6, Table 7, the paper's main contribution): six inference-time correction methods for Qwen2VL-7B, Ovis1.6-9B and InternVL2.5-8B. The methods are misleader warning, +axes, +table, +table+axes, table-based QA (Qwen2.5-7B) and redrawing (Qwen2.5-7B → matplotlib). The paper's claims:
  - table-based QA improves misleading accuracy by 15.4–19.6 pp, with a significant cost on non-misleading charts for two models;
  - redrawing gives significant but smaller gains for two models;
  - the other methods are not significant.

## Data situation

- CALVI, real-world (images downloaded from the bad-vis-browser GitHub by `dataset_preparation.py`) and VLAT ship with the repo. All 42 real-world images downloaded.
- **CHARTOM** is a third-party dataset that must be requested from its authors (README: "Please contact the authors of CHARTOM to get access"). It could not be obtained, so every run uses CALVI + real-world (misleading, n=87) and CALVI + VLAT (non-misleading, n=68).
- For Exp.1 the paper's CHARTOM-free numbers can be recovered exactly. Correct counts are integers: `round(acc_T3·143) − round(acc_T5·56)` out of 87, and likewise out of 68 for the non-misleading split (Table 5 reports per-model CHARTOM accuracy). See `scripts/paper_derived.py` and `paper_derived_exp1.json`. For Exp.3 the paper reports only full-dataset numbers, so we compare deltas vs the default prompt and their significance.

## Why not fully reproduced

1. **Missing third-party data:** CHARTOM is available only on request, so all numbers are on ~61% of misleading and ~55% of non-misleading instances. Exp.3 absolute numbers cannot be compared, and the McNemar tests have less power.
2. **Closed-source MLLMs** (GPT4, GPT4o, Gemini-1.5-flash/pro, Claude-3.5-sonnet; 5 of the 19 in Exp.1) need API keys that are not configured here, so they were not run.
3. **TinyChart / ChartInstruction:** these need external code folders that the README tells you to copy. That code is incompatible with transformers ≥4.45, which Qwen2-VL requires (`forward() got an unexpected keyword argument 'cache_position'`), so neither was run.
4. **Not attempted:**
   - Exp.2 (Lauer Likert consistency): its Wilcoxon test runs across all 19 MLLMs and the paper calls it preliminary.
   - Appendix: CoT, DePlot/MatCha, ChartGemma/GPT5-mini correction methods.

Bugs and gaps that were worked around without editing the code:
